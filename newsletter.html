01

US Urges Fix for Widely Used Software After Critical Alerts

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg · October 22 · exposure estimate is Wiz's (security vendor)

The Office of the National Cyber Director is coordinating a response with the FBI, CISA and the NSA to two vulnerabilities in PHP, the open-source language behind millions of websites, which security firm Wiz estimates affect about 70% of the internet. PHP's maintainers have released patches and urged immediate adoption, and Cloudflare's CTO said the company has observed early attacks trying to exploit the flaws. Finland took its public-facing internet services offline as a precaution, and Dutch government sites and some German and Austrian university sites were also taken down.

02

PHP flaws put US cyber brokers on alert over shutdown coverage

Insurance Business America, quoting Cowbell and Coalition (cyber insurers) · October 22 · insurer commentary

Cowbell's chief commercial officer said brokers and clients should check now how their cyber policy treats a voluntary shutdown and what waiting period applies, since precautionary outages can cost revenue before any breach is confirmed. Coalition's head of emerging risks said its policy has no clause barring coverage if a patch is not applied. Coalition also warned that PHP supports database and backup administration tools, so exposed systems may go beyond public websites.

03

Chubb CEO Greenberg: Industry Has to 'Put Arms Around' AI Liability

Insurance Journal · October 22 · executive remarks on Chubb's Q3 earnings call

Chubb CEO Evan Greenberg called AI liability a growing and emerging exposure that is “not contemplated in casualty underwriting to date.” He said the industry has to “put our arms around this,” as it did with emerging liabilities over the last 50 years. He suggested responses could include coverage clarifications, exclusions, separate AI liability coverages and certification standards for AI and agentic best practices.

04

Seven in 10 boards now build cyber into enterprise risk, Willis D&O survey finds

Insurance Business America, citing Willis, a WTW business (broker survey) · October 21 · global survey; sample size not stated

Willis found 70% of organizations formally include cyber risk in their board-level enterprise risk framework, and 60% quantify potential financial losses from a cyber incident. 48% have cyber insurance and a further 19% plan to buy it within two years; of those insured, 71% buy standalone cover. Phishing and social engineering topped respondents' concerns at 48%, followed by ransomware at 38%.

05

Insurance M&A Down 27% in Q3 2026: OPTIS

Insurance Journal, citing OPTIS Partners (investment bank) · October 22 · US and Canadian agency deals

OPTIS Partners counted 144 US and Canadian insurance agency deals in the third quarter, down 27% from 197 a year earlier, and 439 year to date, down 19%. Private-equity-backed or hybrid buyers were involved in 76% of transactions, with BroadStreet Partners leading at 57 deals. OPTIS said Gallagher, Brown & Brown and Aon made fewer acquisitions but bought very large PE-backed firms, and it expects a more active fourth quarter.

Warehouse owner wins nearly $2M in bad-faith suit against insurer

Business Insurance · October 21 · jury verdict; any appeal not reported

A federal jury awarded $1.94 million plus $6,000 in damages to Amp Services LLC and its owner against Auto-Owners Insurance over a 2023 fire at a warehouse in Granite Falls, North Carolina. The jury found the owner did not burn the building and that Auto-Owners refused to pay without a reasonable investigation. The insurer was still requesting documents more than a year after the fire, while its expert testified the investigation was reasonable given a fire department arson finding.

Artemis · October 22 · deal being marketed, not yet closed; update to yesterday's $350m item

Hannover Re has raised the target for its Acorn Re 2026-1 parametric earthquake cat bond a second time, to $450 million from $350 million and an initial $200 million. 08

Mexico gets its first World Bank Cat DDO, to complement cat bonds and parametric insurance

Artemis, reporting the World Bank · October 22 · approved financing

The World Bank Group approved $500 million for Mexico's first Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option, a standby emergency credit line the government can draw once it declares an emergency. It runs three years with possible extension and covers natural disasters, climate impacts and public health emergencies. It sits alongside Mexico's $420 million and $175 million 2024 World Bank cat bonds and roughly $575 million of parametric insurance for 2026–2027.

09

Automation Is Changing Manufacturing Safety. Leaders Need to Change With It.

Risk & Insurance, written by Nationwide's manufacturing industry leader (insurer commentary) · October 20 (date from the article's own page)

The article says injury frequency across much of manufacturing keeps falling as automation takes over repetitive and hazardous tasks, but incidents in automated plants can be more severe, such as amputations and struck-by injuries. It says risk concentrates in non-routine work such as maintenance, startup, changeovers and jam clearing. It recommends reassessing hazards for that work, refreshing lockout/tagout procedures, training for decision-making and using frontline input as an early warning.

10

Injured Workers Are Waiting Longer for First Treatment, NCCI Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing NCCI (rating bureau) · October 21 (date from the article's own page) · accident years 2016–2023; Business Insurance also reports it

NCCI found time to first treatment for injured workers rose by 2 days nationally between accident years 2016 and 2023, with time to first physical therapy up 8 days and to first major surgery up 10 days. Overall treatment duration was essentially flat at 218.0 days versus 217.8, and claims treated on the day of injury fell to 43% from 48%. Changes varied by state, from a 4-day decrease in time to first treatment in Oregon to a 6-day increase in Nevada.

11

Amazon ordered to provide treatment, criticized over handling of injury claim

Business Insurance · October 21 · trial-level workers' comp ruling, Tennessee

The Tennessee Court of Workers' Compensation Claims ordered Amazon to arrange additional treatment for a worker who hurt their lower back lifting heavy items in April 2024 and to promptly schedule a return visit with the treating physician. The judge denied the worker's request to restart temporary disability benefits. The judge criticized Amazon for seeking opinions disputing the work link, framed as second opinions, and for including a questionable 2022 medical record to sway a physician.

12

The Cost of Losing Your Cool: Tallying the Losses of a Supermarket Cyberattack

Insurance Journal, written by lawyers at Zelle and C. Lewis France (legal commentary) · October 22 · no loss figures given; follows earlier coverage of the attacks

Attackers remotely took over refrigeration equipment running Danfoss software at about 350 French supermarkets, with some units reportedly reaching 50°C and some causing fires; no ransom was demanded and no group has claimed responsibility. The authors say the losses could fall under cyber, property, both, neither, or equipment breakdown endorsements that may carry their own cyber limitations. They flag open questions on direct versus contingent business interruption, cyber physical-damage exclusions and whether state attribution could bring war exclusions into play.
