WR Berkley Reports Q3 Net Income Decrease, Operating Income Up

Insurance Journal · · October 20 · company-reported quarterly results

W. R. Berkley reported third-quarter net income of about $446 million, down about 12.7% from a year earlier, while operating income rose to $456.5 million from $440.2 million. The consolidated combined ratio was 90.8 against 90.9, with accident-year catastrophe losses of about $85.9 million versus $78.5 million. The reinsurance and monoline excess segment's combined ratio worsened to 83.4 from 81.1.

02

W. R. Berkley cuts casualty reinsurance by a quarter as pricing fails to hold

Insurance Business America, citing Berkley's Q3 earnings call · · October 20 · company commentary

W. R. Berkley's casualty reinsurance net premiums written fell 25% in the third quarter to $144.2 million from $192.7 million, the third straight quarterly decline. CEO W. Robert Berkley Jr. said casualty reinsurance ceding commissions do not make economic sense for the company, and management expects the reinsurance segment to keep shrinking by roughly 10% to 20%. Insurance segment rate increases excluding workers' compensation averaged about 4%, down from 6% to 7% in prior quarters.

Industry Panel Highlights Challenges in Selling Cyber Insurance to SMEs

Insurance Journal, reporting a NetDiligence Cyber Risk Summit panel · · October 19 · panelists' views; no figures given

A panel at the Cyber Risk Summit in Philadelphia, held earlier in October, said cyber is still a very small share of property/casualty premiums and that many retail agents serving small businesses are not equipped to discuss it. Arch's Jamie Schibuck said recent growth has come largely through endorsements that are a watered-down product, and suggested parametric-style payouts for business interruption. Marsh Re's Damini Mago said reinsurance and ILS capital is available and the constraint is demand.

05

3M to Argue Australia's $1.4 Billion 'Forever Chemicals' Lawsuit Legally Flawed

Claims Journal, carrying Reuters · · October 20 · government allegations; 3M disputes; early case-management stage

Australia's government is suing 3M and its local unit for more than A$2 billion (about US$1.38 billion) in contamination costs at 28 military bases where 3M's firefighting foam was used, alleging public nuisance, failure to warn and misleading claims. 3M says the public nuisance claim is not available under Australian authority and the consumer protection claims may be time-barred. 3M's defense is due Dec. 11, 2026, and Australia says it has already spent A$1.3 billion on contamination impacts.

06

Insurers settle with fire foam maker over PFAS claims

Business Insurance · · October 19 · settlement; terms and amount not disclosed

Insurers led by Zurich American, with units of Axa XL and AIG also named, settled National Foam Inc.'s coverage suit over firefighting foam PFAS claims, with all claims dismissed and each side bearing its own costs. A 2025 federal ruling in San Francisco had required the insurers to defend National Foam against firefighter PFAS suits but not suits over contaminated water supplies, and a trial had been set for December 2027. National Foam faces thousands of foam lawsuits, most consolidated in a federal multidistrict litigation in South Carolina.

Insurer Rolls Out Policy to Protect Data Centers Against Growing Backlash

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg (Artemis also reports it) · · October 20 · new product; limits and capacity not disclosed

Arbol has launched cover for data center developers and financiers against government-imposed moratoriums that halt or suspend construction, paying a predetermined amount if a covered action stays in effect beyond an agreed waiting period. Premiums vary by geography, permit category and project exposure, and Arbol excludes jurisdictions that already have active moratoriums. The article cites Data Center Watch's count of at least 106 projects, about $198 billion of investment, blocked or delayed by local opposition in the first half of 2026.

08

Jamaica's parametric cat bond payout funded electricity restoration: Chief Fiscal Economist, Francis

Artemis, reporting a conference talk by a Jamaican finance ministry official · · October 20 · government official's account

The full $150 million from Jamaica's IBRD CAR 2024 parametric catastrophe bond was triggered by Hurricane Melissa in October 2025, and the government passed the proceeds to Jamaica Public Service Company to speed repairs to the damaged western network. Chief Fiscal Economist Terron Francis told the ILS Bermuda Convergence conference the payout arrived within 30 days and that about 98% of the western side had power again by February or March 2026. He said Jamaica avoided emergency borrowing, with debt-to-GDP rising only from about 62.4% to 65.5%.

Clear exit terms and liquid collateral key to lowering casualty sidecar tail risk charges: AM Best

Artemis, citing AM Best (rating agency) commentary · · October 20 · rating-agency view; no capital charge figures given

AM Best says clear exit terms, high-quality liquid collateral and transparent risk transfer help lower tail-risk capital charges for casualty sidecar sponsors, and that exit terms are converging on about seven years. It says exit mechanisms can provide liquidity or finality but do not remove reserve risk, and that it treats illiquid collateral conservatively. The article gives no numerical capital charge levels.

AI exclusions are spreading. Harbor just raised $10 million to insure the gap

Insurance Business America · · October 19 · startup funding announcement (Oct 13) and market feature

Harbor, a San Francisco startup that insures gaps left by generative AI exclusions, announced $10 million in seed funding on Oct. 13, led by Base10 Partners, Abstract and 8VC. As workers' comp rates soften, safety records carry more weight at renewal

Insurance Business America, citing Triple-I, Milliman and NCCI · · October 19 · industry forecasts

Workers' compensation is heading for a 12th consecutive year of underwriting profit, with a forecast 2026 net combined ratio of 92.7 and net written premium forecast to fall 0.7% in 2026 and stay roughly flat through 2028. 12

Power Sector's Breakneck Growth Is Outpacing Risk Management, FM Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing an FM (commercial property insurer) survey · · October 19 · date from the article's own page; insurer-published survey

FM surveyed 750 power generation risk decision-makers, plus 150 brokers and 150 financiers, and found 78% of power companies say site selection creates downstream exposure they did not anticipate and 59% name new technology adoption as their leading cost-of-risk driver. Some 61% say business interruption terms do not match real-world recovery timelines, and 65% of power companies and 75% of brokers say new and hybrid technologies are underinsured or inconsistently treated. Only about a third of power companies use engineering risk data to assess emerging exposures.

France riots highlight wider European risks

CIR Magazine, citing Verisk Maplecroft (risk analytics firm) · · October 20 · early reports; final costs depend on claims assessments

CIR reports France's latest unrest could cause larger losses than the 2023 riots, with more than 3,000 shops reportedly damaged or looted over one weekend; the 2023 riots damaged about 2,500 properties and generated roughly EUR 1 billion in insured losses. Interior minister Laurent Nuñez offered his resignation, and protests also took place in Italy, Belgium and Portugal that weekend.
