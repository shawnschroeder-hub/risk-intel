01

Verisk estimates Hurricane Isaias insured onshore property losses in $1bn to $2bn range

Artemis, citing Verisk (catastrophe modeler) · October 13 · modeled estimate; update to Tuesday's item

Catastrophe modeler Verisk estimates insured onshore property losses from Hurricane Isaias at $1 billion to $2 billion, covering wind and privately insured storm surge across residential, commercial and industrial property and autos, including contents and time-element coverage. It expects wind to account for the vast majority of losses and does not expect rain-driven flooding to be a significant factor. The figure is below Euler ILS Partners' $2 billion to $4 billion and Cotality's $2 billion to $3 billion estimates.

02

‘Unusual’ Isaias Brings Winds, Downed Trees But Fewer Claims Than Expected

Claims Journal (Insurance Journal group) · October 14 · early carrier claim counts

Florida carriers report modest early claim counts in the Panhandle, including about 3,000 claims on about 20,000 Universal P&C policies and about 300 on about 15,000 Security First policies. More than 670,000 customers lost power across Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Mississippi, and at least seven tornadoes struck central and southeast North Carolina. Agents warned of roofing companies and public adjusters going door to door promising full replacement costs.

03

E&S market slowing as some warn of ‘excess’ capital

Business Insurance, citing WSIA data and carrier and broker executives · October 13 · executive views

E&S premium volume rose 2.8% in the first half of 2026, down from 13.2% a year earlier, according to the Wholesale & Specialty Insurance Association's report on 15 stamping-office states. AmTrust's Erich Bublitz said property rates have fallen 30% to 40% in some cases, hitting large shared and layered accounts hardest, and said capital from private equity, newer reinsurers and fronting companies has pushed primary general liability rates below what fundamentals support. Risk Placement Services' Ania Caruso expects single-digit casualty increases for many insureds and double digits for accounts with poor loss histories, adverse venues or large auto fleets.

04

Generative AI Exclusions Spread Across Contractor Insurance Policies

Beinsure, citing a Trades Coverage review of state form filings · October 13 · filings, not confirmed on issued policies

Trades Coverage counted 4,078 state-level filings of ISO generative AI exclusion forms across 49 states and D.C. through July 31, of which 2,369 had taken effect and 3,955 involve lines such as general liability, umbrella and excess, package and businessowners policies. The broadest form, CG 40 47, removes coverage for bodily injury, property damage and advertising injury arising from generative AI. The forms are optional endorsements, the filings do not show how often they are attached to issued policies, and Trades Coverage found no admitted standalone product to buy the cover back.

05

Texas high court orders another look at refinery settlement dispute

Business Insurance · October 13 · remanded; no final ruling on who pays

Refinery operators Blanchard Refining and Marathon Petroleum paid $86 million of a $104 million settlement with workers hurt in a 2016 fire at the Galveston Bay Refinery, then sought reimbursement from contractor Industrial Specialists, whose own employees were barred by Texas workers' comp law from suing it. A trial court awarded the refiners $46.48 million plus interest and fees, but an appeals court reversed, finding the service contract not explicit enough to shift that share of the loss. The Texas Supreme Court vacated the appeals ruling and sent the case back for reconsideration under its later S&B Engineers v. Scallon Controls indemnity decision, without deciding whether Industrial must pay.

CVS Settles Justice Department Case Over Opioid Prescriptions for $315M

Insurance Journal, citing Reuters · October 14 · settlement; no admission of wrongdoing

CVS Health will pay $315 million to resolve Justice Department allegations that its pharmacies filled medically unnecessary opioid prescriptions and billed federal programs such as Medicare, in a case that began as a former employee's whistleblower complaint. CVS admits no wrongdoing and, under a separate DEA agreement, must set up processes to monitor potentially illegal dispensing. Walmart settled a similar case in August for $50 million.

Apple alleges OpenAI, its hardware chief and a former iPhone engineer sought confidential product design, manufacturing and supply-chain information from former Apple employees, including by asking job candidates to bring prototypes. OpenAI denies the allegations and says Apple has not identified specific stolen trade secrets.

Artemis · October 14 · investor view; remarks Oct. 13

Calidris Investment Partners co-CEO Philippe Trahan told the ILS Bermuda Convergence conference that casualty ILS has moved from mainly bilateral deals to a syndicated market. Swiss Re says increased reinsurance supply a positive, will align alt capital where it makes sense

Artemis · October 13 · reinsurer executive view

Swiss Re's Leopoldo Camara said more capital entering reinsurance is positive because growing risks need it, and that Swiss Re will align alternative capital where it makes sense through its Alternative Capital Partners unit. He said exposures are becoming more systemic, citing larger individual risks, geographic clustering and more integrated supply chains. His main example was data centers, where insured values of billions, sometimes tens of billions, sit in small locations.

Insurance Business America, citing FBI IC3, Chubb and Swiss Re · October 14

FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center data show U.S. internet crime losses of nearly $21 billion in 2025, up 26% from 2024, a total that includes consumer fraud and investment scams. California power shutoffs expose business interruption gap for brokers

Insurance Business America · October 14 · broker views

PG&E warned of planned power shutoffs that could affect about 70,000 customers in 29 California counties from Wednesday through Saturday to reduce wildfire risk. Brokers say standard business interruption cover may not pay for losses from a precautionary shutoff when there is no physical damage, and utility service interruption terms and limits vary. Some parametric alternatives carry minimum premiums starting at $25,000.

Mass. pot repeal vote puts work safety, medical-use protections in focus

Business Insurance · October 13 · ballot measure; vote Nov. 3

Massachusetts voters will decide on Nov. 3 whether to end recreational marijuana sales and home cultivation from Jan. 1, 2028, while medical marijuana and adult possession of up to one ounce would stay legal. Repeal supporters cite workplace injuries and impaired driving, while opponents say ending licensed sales would remove product testing and push buyers to illicit sellers.
