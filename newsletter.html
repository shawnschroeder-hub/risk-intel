01

Global Commercial Insurance Rates Drop 5% in Q3; Signs of Stabilization

Insurance Journal, reporting Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index (broker-published) · October 21 · third-quarter 2026 data

Commercial insurance rates fell globally for the ninth straight quarter, down 5% on average, with property down 10% and global casualty up 1%, the index's only increase, which the article ties to US rate increases linked to litigation and large settlements. Global cyber rates fell 4%, the 13th consecutive quarterly decline, while US cyber rates were flat, ending a streak of declines. The article says underwriting scrutiny was most seen with catastrophe exposure, US casualty, D&O and AI-linked cyber exposures.

02

Chubb Q3 Net Income Down Slightly on More Catastrophe Losses

Insurance Journal · October 21 · company-reported quarterly results

Chubb's third-quarter net income was about $2.84 billion, which the article's text says was up 1.5% from a year earlier even though its headline says down slightly. The property/casualty combined ratio was 84.1 against 81.8, and pretax net catastrophe losses were $705 million versus $285 million. North America commercial P/C net premiums written fell 2.7%, or rose 1.7% excluding large account and E&S property.

03

There's a lot of hungry and extremely naive capacity available: Chubb CEO Greenberg

Artemis, reporting Chubb CEO Evan Greenberg's remarks · October 21 · executive commentary

Greenberg said the commercial property and casualty market continues to grow more competitive globally, especially in the US and London, and described a lot of hungry and extremely naive capacity. He said competition is spreading from property into casualty, where pricing in many areas is not keeping pace with loss costs that remain steady but well above general inflation. He said Chubb's diversification and local presence leave it relatively well positioned.

04

Gallagher Re estimates global insured cat losses at $92bn for 9M'26, lowest since 2019

Artemis, citing Gallagher Re's Q3 Natural Catastrophe and Climate Report (reinsurance broker) · October 21 · broker estimate; Business Insurance and Claims Journal also report it

Global insured natural catastrophe losses for the first nine months of 2026 were $92 billion, the lowest for the period since 2019 and 29% below the 10-year average of $128 billion, with six straight quarters without a single event above $10 billion. Severe convective storms made up about 60% of the total, including at least $43 billion in the US, a fourth straight year above $40 billion, and at least $9 billion in Europe. Hurricane Isaias, which made landfall on Oct. 9, falls outside the nine-month period and is excluded.

05

U.S. cyber premiums rebound, policy numbers surge: NAIC

Business Insurance, citing NAIC (state insurance regulators) data · October 21 · calendar-year 2025 data

US cyber direct written premiums rose 3.5% in 2025 to about $9.46 billion, after a 7.11% fall in 2024. Policies in force rose 33% to 5.84 million, reported claims rose 20% to 56,323, and the industry-wide loss ratio was 53% versus 48.7%. Endorsement-based policies rose 53.8% in count and made up nearly 63% of policies in force but about 4% of premiums.

TRIA Reauthorization Would Push More Terrorism Risk Onto Insurers, A.M. Best Says

Risk & Insurance, citing A.M. Best (rating agency) · October 21 (date from the article's own page) · legislation pending, not enacted; follows earlier coverage of Senate action

A.M. Best says the House proposal, H.R. 7128, would raise the loss threshold that triggers the federal backstop and add a deadline for certifying an attack as terrorism, so insurers would absorb more losses before federal cost-sharing applies. It says the certification deadline gives insurers more certainty but could lead to premature or inaccurate determinations, especially for cyber, foreign-actor or coordinated attacks. The Senate passed its own clean seven-year extension, S. 4395, in late September, and the two chambers must reconcile the bills.

Hannover Re targets 75% upsized $350m of limit from new Acorn Re parametric cat bond

Artemis · October 21 · deal being marketed, not yet closed; follows the Oct. 12 report of the $200m target

Hannover Re upsized its seventh Acorn Re parametric earthquake cat bond by 75% to $350 million from a $200 million target. The cedent is Oak Tree Assurance, a Vermont workers' compensation captive of the Kaiser Permanente group, with a sliding-scale payout by earthquake magnitude and location and a 25% minimum payout. Ford Recalls 102,000 F-Series Trucks Over Faulty Speedometer

Claims Journal, carrying Reuters, citing NHTSA · October 20 · regulator-announced recall

Ford is recalling 102,159 US trucks because the analog tachometer and speedometer may stick or move erratically and give inaccurate readings. 09

The Challenges of Relying on Historical Data in Risk Management

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS) · October 20 · expert commentary

Experts quoted say historical data is a weak predictor in a fast-changing risk environment with new and interconnected threats. They say poor data quality, contingency plans built on old disasters and boards' short-term focus can lead companies to underestimate emerging risks. They recommend treating past data as a starting point, using real-time monitoring and early-warning signals, and building adaptable resilience-based plans.

French Riots Seen as Insurance Test Before New Regime Kicks In

Claims Journal, carrying Bloomberg, citing DBRS Morningstar (rating agency) · October 21 · analyst estimate; follows the Oct. 20 CIR item on the same unrest

DBRS Morningstar estimates insured losses from France's latest unrest at below EUR 1.2 billion, which it calls a meaningful earnings loss for the French property and casualty industry but unlikely to cause a capital event. Its analysts say the main concern is aggregation of thousands of correlated claims, and that a state-backed riot insurance framework due to take effect in a few months should reduce uncertainty over extreme riot losses. Axa and BPCE Assurances extended claim-reporting deadlines to 30 days from the usual 5.

PERILS lifts July European convective storms insured market loss estimate to €2.8bn

Artemis, citing PERILS (catastrophe loss data aggregator) · October 20 · second estimate; a third is scheduled

PERILS raised its insured market loss estimate for the July 13-19 severe convective storms across Germany, France, Switzerland and Italy to EUR 2.806 billion from EUR 2.186 billion in late August, a rise of roughly 28%. The estimate covers property and motor losses. A third update is scheduled for Jan. 19, 2027.
