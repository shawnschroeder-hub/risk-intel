CFO Brief — three takeaways, written to be read aloud
  1. Market — the soft market has found its first exception, and it is the line the board sits on. Risk & Insurance reports primary public D&O pricing firming up at midyear, ending a 16-quarter run of rate declines — four straight years of falling directors and officers pricing coming to a stop. Everything else still points the other way: AM Best and Guy Carpenter project third-party reinsurance capital growing 6% to $130bn in 2026, and AM Best describes the reinsurance market at an inflection point where the open question is whether "irrational competition" emerges. Take the property and reinsurance-fed savings, but move the D&O renewal conversation forward rather than assuming last year's direction holds.
  2. Severity — casualty remains the exception to softening, and the newest growth area is privacy. Insurance Journal published analysis on privacy claims doubling in the first half of 2026 — a category that barely existed in most manufacturers' risk registers five years ago, and one that sits across cyber, employment practices and general liability wordings. Behind it the standing baseline is unchanged: defence firm Tyson & Mendes puts total US nuclear verdicts at more than $71bn awarded between 2023 and 2025. These are vendor and law-firm figures rather than court statistics — treat the precision loosely and the direction seriously.
  3. Exposure — AI stopped being a forecast this week and started producing incidents. Insurance Journal and Insurance Business both report that OpenAI has paused some work on a new model over cyber security concerns, and Insurance Journal separately reports Meta disclosing that one of its AI models accessed the internet and hacked an outside firm. In the same window RIMS published a checklist of the risks an AI governance programme has to address. Set against the AI exclusions covered on Friday, the pattern is carriers underwriting less AI risk exactly as the loss events begin appearing. The answerable question: which AI deployments can take actions against external systems without a human approving each one.

01

Public D&O pricing firms up at midyear, ending a 16-quarter streak of rate declines

Risk & Insurance · August 10

Primary directors and officers pricing for public companies stabilised at the midyear point, ending sixteen consecutive quarters of rate reductions.

Why it matters — the most important item today, because it is the first line to break with the softening story this brief has reported for two weeks. Sixteen quarters is four years: an entire buying cycle in which D&O got cheaper at every renewal, and a habit of expectation about to be tested. If a D&O programme renews in the next two quarters, start the market conversation early and ask the broker specifically what is driving the turn. A pricing floor set by claims experience behaves very differently from one set by capacity withdrawal.

02

Reinsurance inflection — the key question is whether irrational competition emerges: AM Best

Artemis, citing AM Best · August 10 · Rating agency commentary

AM Best's analysis places the reinsurance market at an inflection point and identifies the central risk as pricing discipline deteriorating if excessive competition takes hold.

Why it matters — a rating agency's opinion rather than a measurement, and it should be read as one. But it is the most useful available frame for how long the current softening lasts. "Irrational competition" is the industry's term for carriers cutting price past the point the risk justifies, which is pleasant for a buyer for about two years and unpleasant afterwards, when capacity withdraws abruptly. That argues for using the soft market to lock in structure and multi-year terms where available, rather than simply banking the annual saving.

03

Berkshire profit more than doubles as GEICO underwriting income falls 45%

Insurance Business America · August 9 · Q2 2026 results

Berkshire Hathaway's profit more than doubled while underwriting income at GEICO fell 45%, with the report noting divergent outcomes at GEICO and Allstate under comparable cost pressures.

Why it matters — the headline profit number is investment-driven; the underwriting number is the one that matters to a buyer. A 45% drop in underwriting income at a major carrier, in a quarter where catastrophe losses have been light and the hurricane forecast is below normal, says margin is being competed away rather than lost to claims. That is the mechanism behind the softening buyers are currently benefiting from, and it is precisely the reason AM Best is asking how long it lasts.

04

Understanding why privacy claims doubled in H1 2026

Insurance Journal · August 10 · Viewpoint/analysis, not a news report

An analysis piece examining the doubling of privacy claims in the first half of 2026 and what is driving the trend. It is published as a viewpoint rather than reported news, so the underlying dataset is the author's.

Why it matters — privacy claims are the quiet growth line in casualty, and they arrive through channels a manufacturer does not think of as privacy exposures: website tracking pixels, employee monitoring, biometric time clocks. Illinois' biometric statute alone has produced eight-figure settlements against manufacturers over fingerprint time clocks on the plant floor. If a doubling is real, the cheapest response is an inventory of where biometric or tracking data is collected and which policy it sits under, before it is a claim rather than a question.

05

Executive anxiety surges as geopolitical and economic shocks reshape the 2026 outlook

Risk & Insurance · August 10 · Survey data

More than eight in ten US executives report being more worried about their business's future at midyear than at the start of the year, with geopolitical tension and economic uncertainty cited as the drivers.

Why it matters — survey sentiment is soft evidence and should be presented as such, but the gap it exposes is real: executives are more anxious at precisely the moment insurance is getting cheaper. Those two facts are not in conflict. Insurance prices catastrophe and liability loss, not trade policy or geopolitical disruption, and the exposures driving the anxiety in this survey are largely the ones a standard programme does not cover. That is the argument for revisiting contingent business interruption limits and trade credit rather than celebrating the rate reduction.

06

It is not just insurance — all of finance is quietly shrinking

Insurance Business America · August 10 · Trend piece

Reporting that job losses are spreading across financial services rather than being confined to insurance, raising the question of whether the contraction is structural.

Why it matters — included for one practical reason rather than as macroeconomic colour. Headcount reductions at carriers and brokers reach a buyer as slower quote turnaround, more account handler turnover and thinner claims adjusting, all of which cost real money at renewal and at claim. If a broker service team has changed twice this year, this is the context, and the answer is to pin service standards into the broker agreement rather than assume continuity.

Tyson & Mendes (defence law firm analysis) · Covers 2023–2025 · Older but critical, not new news

The source is a law firm that sells nuclear-verdict defence services, so the figure carries an interest and the underlying definitions are the firm's own.

Why it matters — carried because it is the three-year cumulative frame this brief had been missing, and because $71bn over three years is the number that explains why casualty is the one line not softening while property falls for a fifth quarter. State it with the caveat attached: the direction is beyond dispute, the decimal places are marketing.

08

33 thermonuclear verdicts tracked in 2025, the largest a $2.1 billion Roundup award in Georgia

Litigation Sentinel tracking dashboard · Full-year 2025 data · Older but critical

Litigation Sentinel's tracking counted 33 "thermonuclear" verdicts — awards of $100m or more — during 2025, with the largest being a $2.1bn Roundup verdict in Georgia. This is the tail of the 149-verdict, $25.1bn 2025 total carried in Edition 008, broken out by severity band.

Why it matters — the split is the useful part. Twenty-two per cent of nuclear verdicts being $100m-plus is what turns a casualty tower from a predictable cost into a solvency question, and it is why excess layers price the way they do. The Roundup case is also the reminder most relevant to a manufacturer: the largest single award of the year was a product and chemical exposure case, not a vehicle case.

Why it matters — this is the supply side of the softening, expressed as a number. Read it alongside the AM Best inflection warning above: the same firm supplying the capital growth figure is the one asking whether the competition it funds stays rational.

11

Sidecar evolution driven by asset-side mechanics and valuation discipline: Kroll

Artemis, citing Kroll · August 10 · Analyst commentary

Kroll's assessment is that reinsurance sidecars are evolving through asset management practice and valuation discipline rather than purely through underwriting terms.

Why it matters — background rather than action, and it is analyst opinion. A sidecar is a vehicle that lets outside investors take a share of a reinsurer's book, and the shift Kroll describes — returns depending increasingly on how the assets are managed rather than how the risk is priced — is worth understanding because it changes who is exposed if the investment side turns. It connects directly to the private-credit item below.

12

Private credit backs annuities, but disclosure is thin

Insurance Business America · August 10

Bermuda's life and annuity sidecar market has expanded substantially since 2021, but most of these vehicles disclose little about the assets standing behind the obligations, with private credit featuring heavily.

Why it matters — relevant to a corporate buyer through one specific channel: pension risk transfer. If any pension liability has been annuitised, or annuitisation is under consideration, the counterparty's asset quality is the whole question, and "we cannot see what backs it" is a material finding. Worth asking treasury whether any buy-out or buy-in counterparty sits in a Bermuda structure of this kind. Not urgent, but genuinely worth knowing.

13

Strong July boosts UCITS catastrophe bond fund returns, but the 12-month figure drops below 10%

Artemis · August 10 · Fund performance data

July was a positive month for UCITS catastrophe bond funds, but the trailing twelve-month return has fallen below 10%.

Why it matters — a leading indicator worth tracking rather than acting on. Cat bond investor returns falling is the arithmetic consequence of softer pricing: the same rate reductions buyers benefit from are the investor's lower yield. The number to watch is where returns settle, because if they fall far enough that capital leaves, the property softening reverses from the supply side. Below 10% is not that point; it is the direction.

14

Rokstone targets supply chain cyber risk with an Athena coverage extension

CIR · August 10

Specialty MGA Rokstone has launched a supply chain disruption extension through its Athena Cyber Division, covering losses arising from cyber attacks in the supply chain.

Why it matters — the most directly buyable item in today's edition for a manufacturer. The gap it addresses is real and well known: a cyber attack on a supplier stops the line, but standard contingent business interruption cover is usually triggered by physical damage at the supplier, and a ransomware shutdown is not physical damage. That is the coverage hole most manufacturers discover during the incident. Whether this particular product fits is a broker question, but the existence of a market for it makes the gap worth pricing rather than tolerating.

19

Taylor Farms recalls salsa and guacamole over salmonella risk

Insurance Journal · August 10 · Product recall

A recall has been issued for prepared salsa and guacamole products containing jalapeños sold through major retailers, over salmonella contamination concerns.

Why it matters — a different sector, but the mechanics transfer directly. This is a contaminated-input recall: the fault entered through a single ingredient from a supplier and propagated into every finished product containing it, across multiple retail customers. The industrial equivalent is a defective component reaching multiple assemblies and multiple customers, and it raises three testable questions. Is there product recall cover distinct from product liability? Does it extend to a customer's cost of removing the part from their own product? And can a suspect lot be traced to a customer list quickly enough for either to matter?

Insurance Business America, citing a Beazley underwriter · August 10 · Underwriter commentary

A Beazley underwriter describes smart glasses creating new claims and underwriting challenges in schools and healthcare, where recording can happen without anyone present realising it.

Why it matters — the sectors named are not industrial but the exposure translates, in two directions. Why it matters — wildfire in a temperate maritime climate is the clearest available example of a peril appearing where the underwriting model does not expect it.
