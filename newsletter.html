Marsh targets more cost cuts; reports revenue increase

Business Insurance, citing Marsh McLennan's third-quarter results (broker-published rate data) · October 15

Marsh McLennan reported third-quarter revenue of $6.86 billion, up 8%, and said global primary commercial insurance rates fell 5% after a 6% decline in the second quarter. 02

KCC Says Isaias Insured Losses to Be $3 Billion; AM Best Calls Storm an Earnings Event

Insurance Journal, citing Karen Clark & Co. (catastrophe modeler) and AM Best · October 15 · modeled estimate; update to Wednesday's Verisk item

Karen Clark & Co. estimates privately insured wind and storm surge losses from Hurricane Isaias at about $3 billion, covering residential, commercial and industrial property, vehicles and business interruption but excluding National Flood Insurance Program losses. AM Best called the storm an earnings event for insurers, well below the level of a capital or solvency event, and said it should not trigger a pricing surge in Florida. The figure is above Verisk's $1 billion to $2 billion estimate carried Wednesday.

03

French Supermarkets’ Cyber Insurance Unlikely to Cover Food Losses, Insurers Say

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 15 · insurer and broker views; update to Tuesday and Wednesday items

Insurers told Reuters that cyber policies typically exclude physical losses such as spoiled food, after hackers took control of cooling units at about 350 French supermarkets and raised fridge and freezer temperatures to 50°C (122°F). Intermarché told AFP its insurance did not cover this type of incident, and one underwriter noted claims could be complicated if a state actor is responsible because cyber policies typically exclude acts of war. Aon's David Molony recommended specific cover for perishable goods, and Les Mousquetaires' president estimated total costs could reach tens of millions of euros.

04

States sue OpenAI, claiming ChatGPT harms young users

Business Insurance, carrying Reuters · October 15 · allegations in newly filed suits

Eight states (Iowa, Kansas, Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia) filed separate state-court suits alleging ChatGPT is designed to be agreeable and engaging in ways that coach users, including teenagers, toward self-harm or harm to others. The suits claim violations of state consumer protection laws and public nuisance, and seek civil penalties, damages and court-ordered changes to how ChatGPT works. OpenAI had not responded to a request for comment, and Florida's attorney general filed the first such state suit in June.

05

Senator must pay coal mining company debt, judges rule

Business Insurance · October 14 · Sixth Circuit ruling

The Sixth Circuit ruled against Sen. Jim Justice in Lexon Insurance Co. v. Justice, upholding the premium portion of a $25 million district-court judgment ($14 million in unpaid collateral and $11 million in unpaid premiums) owed to Sompo International's surety unit on bonds guaranteeing mine land restoration. Justice had personally guaranteed his coal companies' 2019 agreement requiring $20 million in collateral and $200,000 in monthly premiums, but they paid only $5.75 million in collateral and stopped paying premiums in 2021. A unanimous panel rejected his arguments that his liability was capped at $3.5 million and that the agreement expired in 2021.

World Bank confirms signing of Nepal quake catastrophe bond mandate

Artemis · October 15 · mandate signed; no bond issued yet

The World Bank and Nepal's government signed a mandate agreement to explore a parametric catastrophe bond that would pay out after a major earthquake, giving Nepal fast access to funding. The proposed project envisages a three-year bond, and Artemis' July report put the target at $80 million to $190 million of fully collateralized financing. The agreement is described as the start of a collaboration to assess feasibility, and no arrangers or launch timeline have been named.

Workers comp medical, indemnity severity each up 4%: NCCI

Business Insurance, citing NCCI's State of the Line report (rating-bureau data) · October 14 · preliminary estimates

NCCI's updated State of the Line report estimates indemnity and medical lost-time claim severity each rose 4% in accident year 2025, while lost-time claim frequency fell an estimated 2%. Business Email Compromise Claims Jump 30% as A.I. Reshapes Cyber Risk

Risk & Insurance, citing Coalition (cyber insurer) claims data · October 15 · article date read from the article page

Coalition says business email compromise claim frequency rose roughly 30% in 2025 versus the second half of 2024, which it attributes largely to AI-assisted phishing kits that bypass multi-factor authentication. Average BEC losses remain under $30,000, while funds transfer fraud frequency fell 18% in 2025 and ransomware frequency was unchanged. Coalition's data covers more than 110,000 policyholders, and it expects funds transfer fraud to rise 20% to 50% over the next year.

Allianz enhances cover for manufacturing businesses

CIR Magazine, reporting an Allianz UK product announcement · October 14 · date inferred from the article's URL code

Allianz UK has expanded its Commercial Select cover for manufacturers with annual turnover of £10 million to £1.5 billion, adding higher limits and broader protection against production disruption, supply chain problems and workplace injuries. New features include psychological treatment for employees after a workplace injury and engineering inspection and compliance services for lifting equipment, power presses and pressure systems. Allianz said the changes follow an update to its wholesale and retail proposition and are part of a wider review of mid-market sector cover.

11

Hyundai Recalling 94,600 US Vehicles Over Fire Risk From Overheating

Claims Journal (Insurance Journal group) · October 15 · NHTSA recall; remedy not yet available

Hyundai Motor America is recalling 94,622 vehicles, certain 2015-2016 Genesis and 2017-2020 Genesis G80 models, because a stuck power seat control switch can cause the front power seat motors to overheat, creating a fire risk while parked or driven. NHTSA said the remedy is still being developed, and Hyundai advises owners to park outdoors away from other vehicles and structures until it is repaired. The article reports no injuries or crashes.

12

Worker prevails in scaffold-law ruling over ladder fall

Business Insurance · October 14 · state appeals court ruling

New York's Appellate Division, First Department, ruled for a worker on scaffold-law liability in De Souza v. 4720 Third Ave LLC, after plywood sheets he was removing from a ceiling struck his unsecured eight-foot A-frame ladder and he fell. The court rejected the defendants' argument that he was solely responsible, noting he followed his employer's instructions. It also said the defendants remained obligated to secure the plywood against falling, even though removing it was the purpose of the work.

Container Ship Sinks in Strait of Hormuz as Attacks Ramp Up

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg · October 15

The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride was hit by unknown projectiles while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Oct. 13, caught fire and sank, with all 21 crew evacuated uninjured. A second Global Feeder Shipping vessel, GFS Giselle, was struck Oct. 14 and reached a safe anchorage with hull damage, and the bulk carrier Cape Dao sank after a Sept. 23 attack that killed one crew member. Allianz Commercial counts 33 container ship total losses from 2016 to 2025, which the article describes as relatively rare.
