01

US Warns of ‘Ongoing Cyber Threat’ to Industrial Refrigeration Systems

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · November 6 · joint federal advisory

The FBI, CISA and the departments of health, transportation and agriculture jointly warned that unidentified actors are targeting internet-accessible temperature and commercial refrigeration controllers at grocery and cold-storage sites. The agencies said exploitation could change temperatures and cause fire hazards, inventory loss and shorter equipment life, and urged operators to take controllers off the public internet, isolate them from corporate networks and patch promptly. Reuters reports at least three waves of attacks on commercial fridges and freezers in recent months, including a hack of French freezers last month that caused tens of millions of euros in damage.

02

Liberty Mutual Q3 profit falls 7% as catastrophe losses rise

Business Insurance · November 5 · company results

Liberty Mutual’s third-quarter net income fell 7% to $2.07 billion as catastrophe losses rose to $585 million from $114 million a year earlier. The consolidated combined ratio worsened 4.8 points to 89.5%, and net written premium fell 2.8% to $10.74 billion. Global Risk Solutions wrote $4 billion, up 0.2%, while US Retail Markets fell 5.6%.

03

Medicaid coverage losses drive up comp medical costs: WCRI

Business Insurance, citing the Workers Compensation Research Institute (research body) · November 4 · still relevant

A WCRI study of 31 states found that Medicaid disenrollment in 2023–2024 raised average 12-month medical payments on lost-time claims by 3.1%, including 6.1% for manufacturing workers and 10.1% for construction workers. Hospital payments rose 6.7%, with no measurable change in claim frequency, indemnity benefits or attorney involvement. Researchers suggest workers who lose Medicaid lean more on comp medical benefits, and note the claims had only 12 months of maturity.

04

High court blocks insurer’s recovery of fatality settlement

Business Insurance · November 3 · Virginia Supreme Court, certified question · still relevant

The Virginia Supreme Court, answering a question from the 4th Circuit, held that contractors working on a communications tower were exempt from the state’s Overhead High Voltage Line Safety Act after a worker died when a steel cable he was handling contacted a power line. That meant the electric cooperative’s insurer could not recover the $800,000 settlement it paid from the subcontractor and general contractor. The court did not decide whether a settlement without a liability finding could otherwise qualify for reimbursement.

05

Insurers urged to adapt reinsurance structures as secondary peril losses climb: Marsh Re

Artemis, citing a Marsh Re report (reinsurance broker) · November 5

A Marsh Re report says reinsurance programmes, models and pricing remain focused on rare large catastrophes even as secondary perils such as severe convective storm, wildfire and flood drive losses. US insured severe convective storm losses have exceeded $30 billion every year since 2020, and higher retentions since 2023 have shifted more of those frequent losses onto primary insurers. It recommends blending vendor models with insurers’ own claims experience and reviewing aggregate and second-event covers.

06

Aon and Blackstone funds launch Cortina, a new Lloyd’s syndicate offering efficient reinsurance

Artemis · November 6 · subject to final Lloyd’s approval; size per unnamed sources

Aon and Blackstone funds have launched Cortina, a new Lloyd’s syndicate managed by QBE that is expected to start underwriting on January 1, 2027, subject to final Lloyd’s approval. Sources told Artemis the arrangement covers around $1.6 billion of premiums, taking 3% following lines on property catastrophe treaties and 5% on other treaty lines for Aon clients. Aon’s clients will also receive a client dividend.

07

High-cost pain drug drives California comp drug spending

Business Insurance, citing the California Workers’ Compensation Institute (research body) · November 5

The California Workers’ Compensation Institute says meloxicam rose from 1.7% of anti-inflammatory drug payments in 2020 to 30.8% in 2025, as its average payment per prescription climbed from $9 to $92. Four generic 5- and 10-mg capsule products made up 12.6% of 2025 prescriptions but 75.7% of payments. Conventional 7.5- and 15-mg tablets accounted for 87.4% of prescriptions at an average payment of $23.

08

Geopolitics tops life sciences’ economic risk list, Willis says

Insurance Business America, citing Willis (WTW) and Oxford Analytica (broker report) · November 6 · respondent count not stated

A Willis and Oxford Analytica report ranks geopolitical lock-out, US price and payment reforms, transatlantic divergence, budget competition and tariff volatility as the top economic risks for life sciences in 2026–2027. US pharmaceutical tariffs began July 31 for 17 large manufacturers and widened on Sept. 29 to mid-sized and smaller drugmakers, at rates that can reach 100%. Duties are capped at 15% for goods from the EU, Japan, South Korea and Switzerland under existing trade deals.

09

The Hidden Risk Management Value of Insurance Site Inspections

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS) · November 6 · consultant commentary, not news

A risk consultant argues that carrier site inspections assess how predictably a business manages loss, and that a clean inspection report is not a clean risk profile because carriers look only at insured risks. Property, workers’ comp and general liability inspections each focus on different exposures. Advisory recommendations, not just mandatory ones, can affect renewal terms, pricing and capacity, the author writes.

11

Cyber incidents could cost UK financial firms £1bn

CIR Magazine, citing ESET’s 2026 UK Financial Services Security Report (vendor survey of 500 firms) · November 6

Security vendor ESET’s survey of 500 UK financial services firms found 97% had at least one cyber incident in the past year, at an average cost of £726,000. Although 99% held cyber insurance, firms said on average 44% of incident costs were not recovered through their policies. ESET puts potential sector losses at more than £1 billion.
