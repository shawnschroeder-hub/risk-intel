01

Cyber insurance at a crossroads: AI reshapes risk as market growth stays steady

Risk & Insurance, citing Swiss Re (reinsurer research) · September 2

Swiss Re projects global cyber premium of $16.4bn in 2026 on a 5% compound annual growth rate since 2022, with rate reductions easing from 13% in 2025 to 5% in 2026. North America accounts for $10.7bn of global premium, Europe $3.42bn and Asia-Pacific $1.7bn, with average large-corporate limits of $120m in the US against $90m in Europe. Swiss Re's finding is that AI is amplifying cyber risks insurers already understand rather than creating new categories of loss, and it identifies penetration of only 5–20% among smaller firms.

02

Zelenskyy puts insurers on notice as Russian airspace becomes a war-risk flashpoint

Insurance Business America · September 2 · Warning and market expectation, not a coverage change

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the "safe days in Russia's skies are over," addressing airlines and their insurers directly, while roughly 400 Western-leased aircraft worth over $10bn remain stranded in Russia since 2022. The European Union Aviation Safety Agency maintains a conflict-zone bulletin flagging Russian airspace as high risk, and aviation risk firm Osprey Flight Solutions describes Ukrainian strikes deep inside Russia as near-certain through the rest of 2026. The reporting anticipates closer scrutiny of policy wording at renewal — tighter exclusions or higher premiums — rather than immediate withdrawal of cover.

AIG executive chair and former CEO Zaffino set to exit for Palantir

Insurance Journal · September 2

Peter Zaffino leaves AIG's board on 15 September 2026 to become global head of financial services at Palantir Technologies from 15 January 2027, remaining with AIG as a senior adviser. He had been chief executive from March 2021 and moved to executive chair on 1 June 2026; lead independent director John Rice takes the chair. The move follows AIG's artificial intelligence partnership with Palantir, which Zaffino championed for underwriting.

04

US urges hands-off approach to AI regulation at G20 tech meeting

Insurance Journal · September 2 · Advocacy, not adopted policy

At a G20 technology meeting in North Carolina, US technology adviser Michael Kratsios argued against building new AI regulatory frameworks, saying policymakers should write rules only for genuinely novel situations. Meta's Mark Zuckerberg opposed restrictions on open-weight models at the same meeting and Elon Musk criticised EU rules. This is Kratsios's stated position rather than an agreed G20 outcome, and it sits against the EU AI Act's phased implementation.

05

Understanding and mitigating digital accessibility liability risk

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS) · September 2

Federal ADA Title III filings reached 8,667 in 2025, roughly triple the 2013 volume, with 3,117 of them website accessibility claims — a 27% year-on-year rise. The European Accessibility Act became applicable in 2025, covering specified digital products and services through member-state law, while Section 508 governs federal-agency technology and reaches suppliers through covered procurements. The article reports that mid-market companies and regional brands, not only large enterprises, are increasingly targeted.

06

Judge says Smucker's lawsuit against Trader Joe's over frozen PB&J can proceed

Insurance Journal · September 2 · Allowed to proceed, not decided

US District Judge John Adams ruled on Friday that J.M. Smucker's trademark suit against Trader Joe's may proceed in federal court in Ohio, and rejected a request to move the case to California. Smucker alleges Trader Joe's frozen peanut butter and jelly sandwiches copy the round shape, crimped edges and packaging of its Uncrustables brand. Smucker seeks restitution and delivery of the products and packaging for destruction; the ruling lets the claim advance and decides nothing on the merits.

07

India's NCDEX expands parametric weather derivative offering to Chennai rainfall

Artemis · September 2

The National Commodity and Derivatives Exchange has launched a Chennai rainfall futures contract, the second Indian city covered after Mumbai in May 2026. The contract settles financially on measured precipitation at a specified India Meteorological Department reference station over a defined observation window, with no physical delivery. Chennai receives about 1,400mm of rain a year, roughly 70% of it between September and December, and the exchange names construction, utilities, transport and logistics among potential hedgers.

Manufacturing & Workplace9–11
09

UK datacentre growth raises critical supply chain concerns

CIR Magazine, citing the UK Critical Minerals Intelligence Centre (British Geological Survey) · September 2

More than 90 new datacentres are planned in the UK by 2030, and the centre warns that this expansion alongside quantum computing will strain global supply of copper, aluminium, rare earth elements, lithium and cobalt. Many of these materials come from a small number of exporting countries and are often produced only as by-products of other mining, which the report identifies as the source of geopolitical vulnerability. Lead author Dr Holly Elliott said the UK cannot become self-reliant and that access to global supply chains and strategic partnerships remain vital.

10

Insurance industry employee confidence tanks on AI concerns

Insurance Journal, citing Glassdoor's Employee Confidence Index and Indeed Hiring Lab (vendor research) · September 2

Insurance employee confidence fell seven percentage points between July 2025 and July 2026 on Glassdoor's Employee Confidence Index, a vendor measure rather than an official statistic. Entry-level adjuster postings are reported down 50% year on year in the accompanying Indeed Hiring Lab data. Claims adjusters registered the most negative sentiment toward AI of any role covered, citing both job displacement and concern that AI degrades work quality.

11

Chinese-heritage insurer targets Asian exporters in US debut

Insurance Business America · September 2 · Utah only · No AM Best rating disclosed

Usurance Insurance Company, a Los Angeles-based carrier backed by Pasaca Capital and led by chief executive Charles Huang, received a Utah certificate of authority effective 30 July 2026 for property, liability, vehicle liability and marine and transportation lines. It targets Asian exporters and businesses with cross-border operations, a segment the reporting says has struggled to obtain admitted cover in the US. The reporting discloses no AM Best financial strength rating, premium volume, capital or reinsurance detail, and admitted expansion would require state-by-state authority.

12

AI speeds up underwriting, but the decision stays human: Hiscox VP

Insurance Business America · September 2 · Insurer's own account

Mike Maletsky, vice president of underwriting transformation at Hiscox, says the insurer uses AI to augment rather than replace underwriters, who retain the final decision. He argues specialty business resists full automation because no two accounts are identical, and that AI mainly widens the information available and removes administrative work, shortening the time to quote. He expects future underwriters to be judged on critical thinking and on the ability to challenge AI-generated conclusions.

13

Northern Re grows in-force premium since launch to more than $1 billion

Artemis · September 2

Northern Re, a Cayman-based collateralised reinsurer with New York operations, has passed $1bn of in-force premium since launching in early 2023, on a capital base of $325m after a $150m raise this year. Northern Re said it has written more than 100 bespoke contracts at an average line of about $20m, deploying over $100m on individual opportunities on occasion. It expects further growth in structured solutions, retrocession and casualty transactions.
