Lead Stories1–4
01

A.I. data centers, energy projects to drive non-life premium growth as pricing softens

Risk & Insurance, citing the Swiss Re Institute (reinsurer research; projections) · November 23

The Swiss Re Institute projects global real non-life premium growth bottoming at 0.5% in 2026 before recovering to 1.0% in 2027 and 2.0% in 2028. It projects underwriting margins falling from 4.4% in 2026 to 1.7% in 2027 and about zero in 2028, with commercial property and personal motor slowing most sharply. The institute counted 190 corporate jury verdicts above $10 million in 2025, totalling $25.6 billion, up 41% from 2024.

02

Nippon Dynawave facing $10.1M in penalties, costs over deadly Washington tank failure

Claims Journal, reporting Washington L&I and Ecology · November 24 · citations issued; company may appeal

Washington’s Department of Labor & Industries issued $9.1 million in penalties for 75 safety violations, the largest in its history, and the Department of Ecology added $961,000 in penalties and cost recovery. The May 26 rupture of a chemical tank at the company’s Longview paper mill released about 900,000 gallons of caustic white liquor; the article reports 11 workers killed and 12 injured. L&I issued 43 egregious willful violations, one for each week the tank stayed in use after the initial report, said it will refer the case for potential criminal prosecution, and gave the company 15 days to appeal.

03

Workplace injuries, illnesses fall 3% in 2025

Insurance Journal, reporting Bureau of Labor Statistics data · November 24 · fatality data still to come

Private employers reported 2.4 million nonfatal workplace injuries and illnesses in 2025, down 3% and the lowest since BLS began this series in 2003. Manufacturing cases fell 8.5% to 304,200, and the overall recordable case rate was 2.3 per 100 full-time workers. BLS will release 2025 fatality data separately.

04

Iowa blast reveals pollution legal liability gaps in standard cover

Insurance Business America, citing HUB International, Aon and WTW (broker research) · November 24

The US Army plans to detonate about 9,500 lb of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant after the 8th Circuit declined to block it. HUB International is cited as saying an absolute pollution exclusion appears in roughly 98% of general liability and property policies, and Aon’s forecast as finding fewer than one in five commercial buyers purchase specialist environmental cover. WTW’s 2026 outlook puts site pollution liability pricing at flat to +10%.

05

Allied World secures upsized $375m 2001 CAT Re 2026-1 cat bond at reduced pricing

Artemis, citing market sources · November 24 · pricing per sources, not an official release

Allied World, part of Fairfax Financial, secured $375 million of annual aggregate retrocession in its third and largest catastrophe bond. Artemis says all three tranches priced at the bottom of reduced guidance, at spreads of 7.75%, 12.75% and 17.75%. Cover runs from January 1, 2027 for US named storm, US and Canada earthquake and European windstorm.

06

SafePoint hones in on $250m target for Nature Coast Re 2026-4 catastrophe bond

Artemis · November 23 · final pricing not yet confirmed

SafePoint Insurance is targeting $250 million of four-year, per-occurrence named-storm reinsurance covering nine Gulf and Southeast states from December 1. The single tranche carries a 0.46% expected loss with price guidance at a 3.25% spread. Artemis says SafePoint has about $1.25 billion of cat bond protection this year.

07

Atrium and CRC launch Fractal Re II third-party capital backed reinsurance portfolio at Lloyd’s

Artemis · November 23 · structure not yet disclosed

Lloyd’s underwriter Atrium will receive third-party capital-backed reinsurance for program business written through Syndicate 2026, arranged with its parent CRC Group’s risk solutions unit. It follows Fractal Re I, a $270 million casualty sidecar in 2024. Artemis says the underlying risk-transfer structure is not yet clear.

08

Aon launches $300 million data center surety program

Business Insurance, citing Aon (broker) · November 23

Aon launched a surety program with $300 million of initial capacity that lets US data center developers post bonds instead of cash collateral or letters of credit for utility and grid-operator security requirements. Aon says those requirements have ranged from $5 million to $300 million, many between $20 million and $100 million. Each arrangement is subject to the panel insurers’ underwriting approval.

09

Viewpoint: Captives weren’t a hard market moment; they’re a permanent shift

Insurance Journal — opinion by a Sedgwick captive-services executive · November 24 · opinion

The author argues captive formation is continuing to grow rather than reversing as the commercial market softens, with smaller and midsize companies now gaining access. The piece advises starting from actual losses, exposures and peer benchmarks, and names an unrealistic business plan as the main failure point.

10

Factories halt production amid surging power costs

Business Insurance, summarising a Traders Union report citing Bloomberg · November 23

European wholesale electricity prices are about double last winter’s levels, and some factories are suspending production despite having orders. A German plant run by Italy’s Feralpi Group halted operations repeatedly this autumn. Buyers on short-term power markets are more exposed than those on long-term contracts.

11

Retail ranks among least-insured sectors as ransomware attacks peak

Insurance Business America, citing CFC (insurer) and Sophos (vendor) surveys · November 24

CFC claims data for 2021–2025 show ransomware attacks on retail spiking 50% in November versus other months. CFC’s survey finds 43% of retail businesses carry cyber insurance, against 81% for financial services and manufacturing. Sophos puts average retail ransomware recovery cost at $1.76 million, excluding any ransom.

12

Waymo recalls self-driving cars over closed freeway lanes, manual traffic control

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · November 24 · recalls announced Nov 20

Waymo filed two recalls with NHTSA, one over vehicles entering closed freeway lanes and one over responses to manual traffic control. Waymo reported no collisions or injuries from the freeway events, and the California DMV barred its vehicles from freeways after robotaxis entered construction zones.

13

Florida workers’ compensation insurance rates to fall 7.4% in 2027

Beinsure, citing NCCI and WCRI · November 23 · approved by the state regulator

Florida’s Insurance Commissioner approved a 7.4% average cut in voluntary-market workers’ comp rates effective January 1, 2027, the 11th consecutive annual decrease. WCRI data cited in the piece show medical payments per claim up 4% in 2025 for cases with more than seven days of lost work. Kentucky approved a 0.0% loss-cost change for 2027.

14

Dispensing channels drive largest share of workers’ comp prescription cost variation

Risk & Insurance, citing WCRI (research institute) · November 23

WCRI found claims with any mail-order dispensing had $1,350 more in drug payments than retail-only claims, and physician-plus-retail claims $794 more. The study covered 2.2 million claims across 31 states with injuries from January 2018 to March 2024. Dispensing channel explained 37% of the variation, the largest single factor.

15

Iran probed over cyberattacks on 50 ships, raising coverage gap questions

Insurance Business America, citing The Wall Street Journal · November 24 · investigation; no attribution established

US authorities are investigating whether Iran is behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 commercial vessels, with Russia also being examined and no responsibility yet established. The Coast Guard deployed cyber protection teams to at least four vessels approaching US ports. The article notes Lloyd’s has required standalone cyber policies to carry state-backed cyber attack exclusions since March 2023.

16

Gulf airports face soaring war premiums, shrinking cover

Business Insurance, citing Arabian Gulf Business Insight and a Marsh Middle East executive (broker) · November 23

War and political violence premiums for Gulf international airports have risen up to 100-fold since late February, according to Marsh Middle East’s head of war and terrorism. Insurers have raised deductibles, tightened limits and added exclusions as airports become targets in the Iran conflict.

17

PRA’s Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test highlights the resilience of the Lloyd’s market

Lloyd’s (press release; Lloyd’s own assessment) · November 24

The Prudential Regulation Authority’s 2026 stress test was a live three-week exercise in May involving 25 insurers representing more than 80% of the UK general insurance market. Lloyd’s says its market stayed financially resilient and able to pay valid claims. It plans to engage materially affected firms in Q1 2027 and publish a fuller lessons assessment by the end of H1 2027.
