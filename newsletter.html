01

Commercial property premium decreases of 6.2% drive overall soft Q3: CIAB

Insurance Journal, citing the Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers (broker trade body; member survey) · November 18

The CIAB’s Q3 2026 survey found average commercial premiums down 1.3% across all account sizes, a slight moderation from the 2% decrease in Q2. Commercial property fell 6.2%, the largest decrease of any line, while D&O fell 4.1% and workers compensation 3.6%. Commercial auto rose 6.7%, up from 4.5% in Q2 and its 61st consecutive quarterly increase.

02

Strike risk grows at busiest US ports as contract talks stall

Claims Journal, via Bloomberg · November 18 · strike threatened, not started; employer group’s characterization

The contract covering about 250 ILWU Local 26 security workers at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports runs to 12:01 a.m. Saturday, Nov. 21, and the Pacific Maritime Association says the union will not extend it, creating “the real potential for a strike.” The PMA expects dockworkers and marine clerks, who are not party to the dispute, to join any strike. The two ports handle about a third of US seaborne containerized goods.

03

AI losses are hiding inside existing policies, Allianz Commercial warns

Insurance Business America, citing an Allianz Commercial report (insurer) · November 19 · insurer’s assessment

Allianz Commercial says confirmed AI-related losses significantly understate the true exposure because most AI claims are filed under cyber, D&O, product liability or crime. It says individual cases it has observed could each produce industry losses of about $100 million. Its report sorts AI losses into four types, including “AI-dependency” losses, where an ordinary outage is amplified because a business cannot run without its AI processes.

04

Senators warn White House that AI-enabled cyber threats outpace defenses

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · November 18 · letter from two senators, not legislation

Senators Maria Cantwell and Mike Rounds wrote to the chair of the White House Super Intelligence Task Force that defenses are “not keeping pace” with AI’s ability to find and exploit software weaknesses. They urged immediate action to protect critical infrastructure, financial institutions and widely used software, and called for security standards for autonomous AI agents. The top US homeland security official said on Tuesday that the recent Microsoft, air-traffic and JPMorgan outages were tied to aging systems, not cyberattacks.

05

Trump administration unveils project to combat cybercrime

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · November 19

The administration has formed a Threat Disruption Center to support the Homeland Security Task Force, involving more than 30 federal agencies and more than 440 state and local partners. The government is paying TRM Labs about $95 million for technical support, with an AI platform as the center’s main investigative tool. Victims told the FBI they lost almost $21 billion to cybercrime last year, roughly a 25% increase.

Allied World raises target to $375m for 2001 CAT Re 2026-1 catastrophe bond

Artemis · November 19 · deal still in marketing, not yet priced

Allied World has raised its target for annual aggregate retrocessional protection to $375 million from $325 million, covering US named storm, US and Canada earthquake and European windstorm on an industry-loss trigger. 07

Lancashire enters cat bond market seeking $175m S6 Re aggregate peak peril retro

Artemis · November 18 · deal in marketing, not yet priced

Lancashire Holdings is sponsoring its first catastrophe bond, seeking $175 million of five-year aggregate retrocession through S6 Re Ltd. covering North America storm and earthquake, Japan earthquake and typhoon, and European windstorm. The notes use an annual aggregate, weighted industry-loss trigger with a $10 billion franchise deductible. Price guidance is 4.75% to 5.5%, against an initial expected loss of 2.41%.

08

Supermarket fridge hacks expose a blind spot in cyber insurance portfolios

Insurance Business America, citing the Paris prosecutor’s office and Coalition (cyber insurer; exposed-host counts) · November 18 · Oct 11 attack, new insurance analysis

Attackers took remote control of refrigeration controllers at about 700 French retail sites on Oct. 11, causing food spoilage and roughly 20 fires, according to the Paris prosecutor’s office. Coalition counted about 1,600 internet-exposed hosts for each of the two controller brands targeted, across 87 countries, including 278 in the US. An MS Amlin underwriter said food manufacturers and pharmaceutical cold chains could face similar exposures, and underwriters are asking whether control systems are segmented from corporate networks.

09

Chemical worker’s lymphoma benefits upheld over benzene exposure

Business Insurance · November 18 · intermediate appellate ruling

The Intermediate Court of Appeals of West Virginia upheld workers compensation benefits for a worker who spent about 40 years at a Bayer chemical plant and was diagnosed with follicular lymphoma in 2021. The court rejected Bayer’s arguments that the science did not show a clear link between benzene exposure and the disease, and that the review board improperly shifted the burden of proof to the employer.

10

Mail-order drugs drive higher comp prescription costs: WCRI

Business Insurance, citing the Workers Compensation Research Institute (research body) · November 17

A WCRI study of 2.2 million workers compensation claims in 31 states found that claims with any mail-order dispensing had first-year prescription payments $1,350 higher than retail-only claims. Dispensing channel explained 37% of the variation in prescription costs, more than state drug policies at 23%. Dermatological medications made up $462 of the mail-order difference.

Report: Up to 82% of data center disaster losses uninsured

Business Insurance, citing Jupiter Intelligence (climate-risk analytics firm), via Reinsurance News · November 19 · vendor estimate; new data since the Nov 18 data center item

Jupiter Intelligence estimates that up to 82% of annual flood, wind and wildfire losses at global data centers are uninsured, or $3.1 billion to $3.6 billion. The article attributes the gap partly to major operators choosing to self-insure and to scarce cover in high-risk regions. China accounts for half of global expected losses but only 17% of asset value.

12

Drone attack sinks LPG tanker in Black Sea

Business Insurance, citing Reuters · November 19 · no attribution reported

A Liberian-flagged liquefied petroleum gas tanker sank off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast on Tuesday after projectiles struck it, a day after a wheat-laden cargo ship sank in the same area. The report describes it as the latest in a series of drone attacks amid the widening Russia-Ukraine conflict in the Black Sea, and does not name who was responsible.
