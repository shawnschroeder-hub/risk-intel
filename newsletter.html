01

Verisk: Cargo Theft Losses Rise Despite Drop in Reported Incidents

Claims Journal, citing Verisk CargoNet (data vendor) · November 5

Verisk CargoNet counted 2,072 US cargo thefts in the first nine months of 2026, down 25%, but estimated losses rose 39% to $718.9 million as the average theft value jumped 72% to $448,456. Fictitious pickups rose to roughly 25% of incidents from 18%, and thefts of $1 million or more reached 19 in the third quarter, up from nine. Food and beverage led the quarter with 94 incidents, followed by metals with 63, of which 34 involved copper.

02

AIG Steps Back from NPW Growth Target on Pressure in Some Lines

Insurance Journal · November 5 · follows yesterday’s Q3 results item

AIG has withdrawn its February guidance for low- to mid-teens growth in net premiums written, after growing them 11% in the first nine months of 2026. CEO Eric Andersen said AIG “will not chase growth for growth’s sake” and that Lexington is shrinking its US property book as new entrants and excess capacity intensify competition. He cited rising losses in reps and warranties and energy, while data centre cover is one of Global Specialty’s fastest-growing lines.

03

Lockheed to Pay $30M to Settle Trump Administration Claims Over DEI Practices

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · November 5 · settlement, no liability admitted

Lockheed Martin will pay nearly $30 million to settle federal claims that it falsely certified anti-discrimination compliance to win government contracts while favouring non-white workers in hiring, promotion and pay. Of that, $22 million resolves the false-certification claims and $7.5 million goes to more than 1,000 employees, according to the EEOC, while the former vice president whose whistleblower suit started the case receives $4.4 million. Lockheed denied the allegations and said it did not admit liability.

04

El Nino Poses ‘Meaningful Risk’ of Higher Inflation in 2027, SF Fed Paper Says

Insurance Journal (Reuters), citing a San Francisco Fed Economic Letter · November 4 · paper dated Oct 2

A San Francisco Fed paper estimates El Niño may have raised US headline prices by about 0.3 percentage point between April and July 2026, an effect expected to fade by year-end. It says El Niño could lift prices by as much as 1 percentage point in 2027. Reuters notes inflation has been above the Fed’s 2% target for more than five and a half years.

05

Sawmill loses bid to block injured worker’s negligence suit

Business Insurance · November 4 · North Carolina Court of Appeals; suit proceeds, no liability finding

The North Carolina Court of Appeals ruled that Edwards Wood Products cannot use workers’ compensation exclusivity to block a negligence suit by a staffing-agency mechanic paralysed in 2023 when a crane cable snapped at its Laurinburg sawmill. The court found no express or implied employment contract that would make the sawmill his special employer. It also noted the sawmill had earlier argued before the state Industrial Commission that no employment relationship existed, and held it could not now take the opposite position.

06

R&W claims frequency, severity increase: Liberty Mutual

Business Insurance, citing Liberty Global Transaction Solutions (insurer claims study) · November 4 · 2025 data

Liberty Global Transaction Solutions says representations and warranties claim notifications rose 26% to 176 in 2025, the most across its six reports since 2019, and now reach nearly one in four policies. Accounting and financial issues drove 52% of amounts paid and reserved. Payments of $10 million or more made up about 75% of claims paid, up from about 68% at the end of 2024.

Cat bond risk spreads fall to lowest level since 2017, as tightening continued in October: Plenum

Artemis, citing Plenum Investments (ILS fund manager) · November 5 · data as of Oct 30

Plenum Investments says the cat bond market’s insurance risk spread fell to 4.33% at Oct 30, its lowest since August 2017 and about 13% below a year earlier, after narrowing about 5% during October. Total coupon yield fell to 8.41% from 8.74% on Sept 25, while expected loss rose to 2.55% from 2.25% a year earlier.

09

Pa. lawmaker plans bill to double minimum comp benefits

Business Insurance · November 4 · planned bill, not yet introduced

Pennsylvania Rep. Tim Brennan has circulated a co-sponsorship memo for a bill to double the minimum weekly workers’ compensation benefit for certain permanently disabled workers, from $100 to $200. It would apply to workers injured before Jan 1, 2010, and follows a unanimous recommendation from the state’s Workers’ Compensation Advisory Council. The $100 minimum has not risen since 2007.

Half of organisations lack formal crisis management strategy

CIR Magazine, citing Everbridge’s 2027 Global Risk & Resilience Outlook (vendor survey) · November 5 · sample size not stated

Everbridge finds half of organisations have limited or no formal strategy for managing critical events. It says 77% of leadership teams were caught off guard by a geopolitical development at least once in the past year. A quarter of organisations (24%) never test their business continuity plans.

Swiss Re’s P&C reinsurance result delivers, despite competitive market weighing

Artemis · November 5 · company results, first nine months

Swiss Re reported nine-month group net income of $4.2 billion, including $1.3 billion in the third quarter. P&C Re net income fell 6% to $2.2 billion on a 77.3% combined ratio, while insurance revenue fell to $12.8 billion from $14 billion and new-business margin dropped to $1.8 billion from $2.5 billion. Swiss Re linked the decline to a competitive market, with pricing pressure greatest in property catastrophe lines that have had fewer losses.

Lloyd’s of London Forms New Oversight Arm in Efficiency Push

Insurance Journal (Bloomberg); also Lloyd’s press release · November 4

Lloyd’s is merging governance, performance, reserving and syndicate capital-setting into one performance and oversight function led by Rachel Turk, with a “low-teen” number of roles potentially cut. Capital modelling and aggregation teams will report to CFO Jim Bichard, and Jacob Palmer joins as chief strategy officer. No cost-savings figure was given.

Stray Drones Spawn a Growing Area of the Insurance Market

Insurance Journal (Bloomberg) · November 5 · Baltics and Poland

Insurers in the Baltics and Poland are selling war-spillover cover as stray drones become a business risk, and solar operator Sunly has insured parks valued at €600 million ($670 million). Lithuanian insurer Lietuvos Draudimas has signed more than 100 business contracts since May, and Telia has insured Lithuanian infrastructure, including data centres, for up to €50 million. The EBRD has halved the market’s former standard that insured assets sit at least 100 kilometres from the front line.
