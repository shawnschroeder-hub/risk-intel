01

Maersk hit by cyberattack, ransom demand

Business Insurance, citing BleepingComputer · November 12 · data-theft details are the attackers’ claims, not verified

A cyberattack hit Danish shipping company A.P. Moller–Maersk over the weekend. A ransomware group is threatening to release about 100 GB of data it says it took from Maersk’s logistics and supply-chain platform unless it is paid $5 million. The group claims the data includes driver records, administrative accounts, passwords and mobile-device logs.

02

Nvidia’s Huang ousted by board in move that roils AI economy

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · November 12 · development of a Nov 11 item; investigation under way, no findings

Nvidia’s board removed CEO Jensen Huang, named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO and hired a Wachtell, Lipton partner to conduct an independent investigation. The move follows reports of Huang’s relationship with a former executive of Megaspeed, a customer under US investigation over potential chip smuggling; Megaspeed and Nvidia deny wrongdoing. Shares fell as much as 14% and were down 7.5% by mid-morning, erasing more than $300 billion in market value.

03

Commercial rates fell 5% in Q3. Broker growth matched it. Not evenly

Insurance Business America, citing Marsh (broker-published index), TD Cowen and OPTIS Partners · November 12

Global commercial insurance rates fell 5% in the third quarter, according to the Marsh Global Insurance Market Index, while TD Cowen puts median organic growth for listed P&C brokers at 5%. Aon’s commercial risk solutions grew 7% organically, while Brown & Brown grew 1.2% excluding contingents as soft property rates weighed. OPTIS Partners counts broker deal volume down 19% year on year through Q3.

04

US commercial insurance renewal rate increases slow across major lines in Q3 2026

Beinsure, citing the Ivans Index · November 11 · US index; a different dataset from Marsh’s global figures

The Ivans Index shows US commercial renewal rate increases slowing across major lines in the third quarter. Average renewal increases were 6.88% for umbrella, 5.65% for commercial property, 5.61% for business owner’s policies, 4.12% for general liability and 3.63% for commercial auto, while workers’ compensation fell 1.17%.

05

Property losses cluster in a small fraction of sites, FM finds

Insurance Business America, citing FM (insurer) and Marsh · November 11 · insurer’s own analysis

FM says 30% to 40% of its gross loss dollars come from about 1,000 of its roughly 60,000 insured sites, a pattern it says repeats every year and can be identified in advance. By FM’s own analysis, locations in its weakest risk quartile are seven times more likely to suffer a loss than those in its strongest, and that loss is 30 times more severe. The article also cites Marsh data showing global commercial property rates down 10% in Q3, the ninth straight quarterly decline.

06

Marsh says Fed should ease up on banks that insure AI loans

Insurance Journal · November 12 · broker advocacy; no regulatory decision yet

Marsh is urging US regulators to cut the capital banks must hold against loans covered by credit risk insurance, as lenders look to offload data-center exposure. Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said on November 10 that the Fed will finalize its latest capital rules in the coming weeks. Barclays is seeking credit cover on more than $10 billion of data-center debt, and HSBC has explored a similar deal.

07

Tort costs nearly doubled in a decade. Casualty pricing shows it

Insurance Business America, citing Triple-I and the US Chamber Institute for Legal Reform (both tort-reform advocates) · November 12

A Triple-I analysis of NAIC statutory data puts US tort costs at $579.7 billion in 2023, up from $299.7 billion nine years earlier. Other liability ($249.3 billion) and personal auto liability ($188.1 billion) together make up nearly three-quarters of the total. The article cites WTW data showing umbrella and excess liability as the fastest-rising commercial lines through the first half of 2026.

08

Staffing firm’s insurer needn’t defend worker wage class action: judge

Business Insurance · November 11 · federal district court ruling (N.D. Ill.)

A federal judge in Chicago ruled that Philadelphia Indemnity, a Tokio Marine unit, need not defend staffing firm Remedial Environmental Manpower against a wage-and-hour class action by workers it placed at a poultry processor. Because the staffing professional liability policy counted Remedial’s employees as insureds, the judge held that its insured-versus-insured exclusion applied. The duty-to-defend claim was dismissed with prejudice, and the duty-to-indemnify claim was dismissed as premature.

11

Data center power demand is outpacing the grid tenfold, and new risks follow

Insurance Business America, citing Howden (broker report) · November 12 · broker estimates

Howden’s report “Insuring the data center supercycle” says US data center power demand has grown about 19% a year since 2000, against about 2% for the grid, taking data centers to 5% of US grid capacity in 2025. Howden estimates power constraints add 24 to 72 months to the most constrained builds, which it puts at $800 million to $2.4 billion of lost revenue for the largest facilities. It also says half of facilities reported an impactful incident in the past three years, at an estimated $500,000 to $1 million each.

12

Lloyd’s calls for clearer war risk insurance coverage and pricing

Beinsure · November 12 · conference remarks, not a market rule

Lloyd’s chief of performance and strategy Rachel Turk called for clearer war risk exclusions, standalone cyber war policies that define insured state-backed events, and sublimits to keep systemic war exposures measurable. She said wordings should be updated for hybrid warfare and state-backed cyberattacks, because traditional war definitions were built around conventional conflict. She spoke at AM Best’s Insurance Market Briefing Europe in London.

13

European insurers tighten riot insurance coverage as civil unrest risks rise

Beinsure, citing Morningstar DBRS, Howden and Verisk Maplecroft · November 12

European insurers are raising deductibles, lowering limits and, in some high-risk locations, withdrawing strikes, riots and civil commotion cover after more than 500,000 people joined protests across France in mid-October. Morningstar DBRS expects insured losses from the French protests to stay below €1.2 billion, against about €730 million from the June 2023 riots. Allianz says it has not imposed broad restrictions on the cover.

14

Nine in ten UK businesses hit by disruption – Chubb

CIR, citing Chubb (insurer survey) · November 12

Chubb’s 2026 Middle Market Risk Radar found more than nine in ten mid-market businesses in the UK and Ireland had at least one significant disruptive incident in the past three years. Cyber security was the top concern at 70%, and 37% reported disruption from an incident at a supplier or technology provider. Only 36% had a cyber policy that was regularly reviewed and tailored to changing risks.

15

Munich Re’s P&C reinsurance sees decline in Q3, while net result rises to €5.7bn for 9M’26

Artemis · November 12 · company results

Munich Re’s third-quarter P&C reinsurance net result fell to €938 million from €1.187 billion as major losses rose to €646 million from €118 million, pushing the combined ratio to 80% from 62.7%. The Colombian El Choco earthquake was the costliest event at €300 million. The group raised full-year net result guidance by €300 million to €6.6 billion.
