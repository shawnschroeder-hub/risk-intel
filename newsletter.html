07

Florida appeals court upholds $103 million verdict against Ford, affirming the product-defect presumption

Insurance Journal · August 4 · Original jury verdict 2024

Florida's Fifth District Court of Appeal affirmed a $103m award over a 2016 Mustang, supercharged by Roush Performance, that exploded in 2017 with the owner inside; the exact cause was never determined. The court upheld the Cassisi doctrine — that a product malfunctioning under normal use may be presumed defective at the time of sale — and rejected Ford's argument that the doctrine had been applied too broadly.

Why it matters — this is the manufacturer's story of the day. A presumption of defect arising from malfunction alone, with no proven cause, is precisely the mechanism that turns a contested product claim into a nine-figure one. It is worth understanding which of our jurisdictions apply a comparable presumption.

08

NHTSA determines certain older Ford vehicles pose "unreasonable safety risks"

The federal safety regulator determined that certain older Ford models present unreasonable safety risks.

Why it matters — read alongside the verdict above, it shows the two tracks running at once: a regulatory finding and a civil judgment against the same manufacturer. A regulator's determination is the kind of document that later appears in a plaintiff's exhibit list, which is why recall and defect files belong in the risk conversation and not only the engineering one.
