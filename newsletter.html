01

Massive data center risks require underwriting rethink

Business Insurance · November 2 · APCIA annual meeting panel (broker and reinsurer views)

Reinsurance and broker executives at the APCIA annual meeting in Boston said the largest data center projects, often costing tens of billions of dollars, concentrate cyber, property, AI liability and business interruption exposures in one place. Gallagher Re North America’s CEO called it an interconnected exposure that is complex to analyse, and Marsh Re’s Will Garland said more risk is being concentrated in one place. Munich Re’s Kerri Hamm argued that project sponsors such as large internet and cloud providers should share in risk transfer.

02

Warning over AI threat to critical infrastructure

CIR Magazine, citing the UK National Cyber Security Centre’s State of the Cyber Nation report (government agency) · November 3

The NCSC says the UK is highly likely to face an AI-powered ransomware attack on its critical national infrastructure within two years. It says hostile actors are increasingly likely to use AI to scale ransomware, social engineering and the exploitation of software vulnerabilities, and that years of underinvestment could leave organisations exposed. The government announced £60m for the first phase of a national Cyber Shield and £70m for a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre.

03

APCIA Says Claims Data Standards in Works to Better Combat Criticism

Insurance Journal · November 3 · trade-association statements

APCIA board chair David Bell, president and CEO of ALPS Corp., told the association’s annual meeting in Boston that the industry is “caught in the crosshairs” of criticism. He cited Wall Street Journal reports that a large share of auto and homeowners claims go unpaid, after which two senators asked some of the largest insurers for claims data. APCIA is developing a standardized claims-closed data-call template, and Bell said claims can close without payment for reasons other than a coverage denial.

04

Data Centers, AI Become Local Political Issues Without Federal Regs

Insurance Journal · November 3 · speaker opinions at the APCIA annual meeting

Political strategists David Axelrod and Karl Rove told the APCIA annual meeting that data centers have become a campaign issue at every level of government. Rove said that without federal oversight states will govern data centers individually, and he predicted no federal action on AI or data centers for the next two years. He cited Texas Gov. Greg Abbott’s moratorium on new data centers, which stays in place until developers present plans covering water, power, capacity sharing, local buy-in and property taxes, without tax incentives.

05

Roundup Settlement Expected Before Christmas, Bayer CEO Says

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · November 3 · settlement not yet approved · new development on the Oct 30 Roundup trial item

Bayer CEO Bill Anderson said on Nov 3 that the $7.25 billion Roundup settlement is expected before Christmas, adding that it could be any week now. The proposed settlement is under review by a Missouri state court judge in St. Louis, and the article reports no final approval. Bayer faces about 65,000 claims in US courts, and Anderson said far fewer individual cases would remain after a settlement.

06

Berkshire-Owned Utility’s Wildfire Lawsuits Head to Oregon Supreme Court

Claims Journal (Reuters) · November 3 · court review pending

The Oregon Supreme Court is due to take up claims against Berkshire Hathaway’s PacifiCorp over the 2020 Labor Day windstorm fires, with potential exposure described as tens of billions of dollars. A 2023 jury found PacifiCorp at fault and mini-trials awarded 201 plaintiffs $1.25 billion, but the Oregon Court of Appeals set aside the liability verdict and those judgments on April 8, 2026. PacifiCorp has settled with other claimants for about $2.3 billion through June 30, and more than 1,000 plaintiffs still await trial.

Artemis · November 3 · product launch

08

Hamilton secures upsized $300m Easton Re 2027-1 cat bond at reduced pricing

Artemis · November 3

Hamilton’s Easton Re 2027-1 cat bond was upsized to $300 million from a $250 million target, renewing a $200 million 2024-1 deal that matures early next year. Price guidance was cut from 4.75%-5.25% to 4%-4.75%, and the notes priced at a 4.25% spread with a 2.66% initial expected loss. The bond gives three-year retrocessional cover for US named storms and US and Canada earthquakes.

Huge data centers pose challenges for contractors, owners

Business Insurance, citing Industrial Info Resources · November 3 · IRMI Construction Risk Conference panel

Industrial Info Resources counts more than 2,900 planned data centers valued at about $2.4 trillion through 2030, though some may not be built. The article also cites a Texas Panhandle worksite housing at least 5,000 workers and high-voltage transformer lead times of about 30 months. Iron Mountain’s Dana Mango said data center owners can lose $50,000 to $100,000 per minute from power and temperature deviations.

10

UK Vehicle Production Rises Sharply in September Against Cyber-Hit Comparison

Claims Journal (Reuters), citing SMMT (trade body) data · November 3

UK vehicle output rose 36.3% to 74,031 units in September, the strongest growth since August 2022, according to the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders. The gain mainly reflects a weak comparison, as a cyber incident at Britain’s largest automotive employer disrupted production a year earlier. Output for the first nine months of the year fell 3% to 564,537 units.

11

Argenta forecasts 18%+ returns at Lloyd’s in 2026, lower profits for 2027 amid softening

Artemis, citing an Argenta Private Capital report (Lloyd’s capital provider view) · November 3

Argenta estimates a return on capital of more than 18% for its clients investing in Lloyd’s for 2026, helped by low catastrophe losses, but expects lower profits in 2027 as rates soften. It says planned 2027 combined ratios are the weakest since 2019, which it called not a cause for alarm, and that property re/insurance saw the steepest pricing decline in 2026. Lloyd’s capacity auctions began Oct 21, and the first auction saw the largest volume traded in over twenty years at lower average prices than last year.

12

PERILS finalises industry loss estimate for Australia east coast SCS at AU $1.62bn

Artemis, citing PERILS (industry loss data provider) · November 2 · final estimate for an event of Oct 26 – Nov 1, 2025

PERILS finalised its industry loss estimate for the Australian east coast severe convective storms at AU$1.62 billion, about 8% above its third estimate. Property made up 87% of the loss and motor 13%, with Queensland accounting for 94%. PERILS’ first estimate, in December 2025, was AU$1.108 billion.

13

Ebola outbreak spreads across central and east Africa

CIR Magazine, citing ECDC and WHO · November 1 · case figures as of Oct 29

A Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak centred in the Democratic Republic of the Congo had 10,274 confirmed cases and 4,992 deaths as of Oct 29, with transmission in 67 of the country’s 178 health zones. Kenya has reported nine cases, including two deaths, and Nigeria confirmed one imported case on Oct 20. The ECDC rates the likelihood of infection for people travelling to affected areas from the EU/EEA as low and the risk to the wider EU/EEA population as very low.
