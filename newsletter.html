01

A one-in-200-year US disaster could create a $700 billion protection gap, Moody's finds

Insurance Business America, reporting Moody's · September 25

Moody's found that 57.8% of global catastrophe losses since 2015 have gone uninsured. It estimates a one-in-200-year US catastrophe could cause $1.1 trillion in total losses with a $700 billion protection gap, nearly the size of the $785 billion global reinsurance capital base. Moody's also noted global P&C insurance capital of about $1.2 trillion is a small fraction of $319 trillion in global capital markets.

02

As cat bond loss multiples tighten, AM Best urges investors towards greater selectivity

Artemis, reporting AM Best · September 25

AM Best said the weighted average cat bond loss multiple fell to 2.4x year to date in 2026, from 3.0x in 2025 and 3.71x in 2024, as spreads fell while expected losses rose. It urged investors toward greater selectivity given abundant capital and record issuance. AM Best said January 2027 renewal pricing will depend significantly on the rest of the Atlantic hurricane season staying calm.

03

Interconnected systemic risks highlight need to expand ILS capacity: Swiss Re & LSE

Artemis, reporting Swiss Re Institute and LSE · September 25

Joint Swiss Re and LSE research reviewing filings from 91 Fortune 100 companies found a 24% increase in risk interconnections between 2019 and 2026, with AI and supply chains as key connection points. The report notes more than 40% of US data centers sit in tornado zones and 88% of Taiwanese semiconductor plants are in extreme seismic zones. It argues for expanding ILS capacity through public-private partnerships and layered reinsurance structures.

Insurance regulators defend state-led model in reply to Warren

Insurance Journal · September 25 · regulator letter, no legislative action

The NAIC replied to Sen. Elizabeth Warren's concerns about insurers' growing exposure to private credit, which followed revelations that insurers controlled by Guggenheim CEO Mark Walter misclassified more than $20 billion in loans as non-affiliated. The regulators said recent changes do not reflect "an insurance regulatory vacuum requiring enhanced federal guardrails." Apollo CEO Marc Rowan separately criticized the fragmented state system as open to regulatory arbitrage.

05

First US trial against TikTok to test claims platform fueled teen mental health crisis

Insurance Journal · September 25 · trial scheduled, allegations not findings

An Alabama state court jury in Montgomery is set to hear the state's claims that TikTok and ByteDance designed an addictive platform and misled consumers, with trial expected to last two to three weeks. It is the first case in the multistate litigation to reach trial; at least 27 other states and Washington, D.C. have filed similar suits. The state seeks penalties and other relief, and TikTok previously settled other cases selected for trial.

06

ILS capital providers well positioned to complement re/insurers' data centre needs: Stahel, LGT

Artemis, quoting LGT ILS Partners · September 25

LGT ILS Partners' Michael Stahel called data centres one of the largest new sources of insured property value in the US, with single campuses worth tens of billions exposed to hail, convective storms, tornadoes, earthquakes and flooding. He said accumulation risk, where one weather event hits multiple facilities, is the defining underwriting challenge. LGT ILS has formed a task force to evaluate the business and said capacity must be paired with strict accumulation management.

08

Comp claims study finds analytics gains, gaps in measuring results

Business Insurance, reporting Rising Medical Solutions · September 24

Rising Medical Solutions' survey of 338 workers' comp claims leaders found 41% use predictive modeling or AI-driven analytics, and half of those users reported better claim closure outcomes. Adoption was 57% among higher-performing organizations versus 34% among lower performers, though 35% of users had not measured the effect on closures. Eighty-five percent said administrative and compliance workloads get in the way of injured-worker communication.

09

Offshore energy's soft market has a hard pocket — and it's about to get bigger

Insurance Business America, citing IUMI, Willis and Gallagher Specialty · September 25

Global offshore energy premium grew just 0.1% in 2025 to $4.8 billion after six to seven years of softening, with upstream rates down about 11% and further double-digit cuts expected in 2026. Subsea construction stands out as a capacity-constrained "micro-hard market." London holds roughly 60% of global offshore energy premium, and renewables now make up 30% of London's book.

10

IUA calls for further Consumer Duty changes

CIR Magazine, reporting the IUA · September 25 · FCA consultation, not final rules

The International Underwriting Association welcomed the FCA's Consultation Paper 26/23 proposal to limit the international scope of the Consumer Duty rules. It said the proposed proportionality changes lack matching updates to insurance product governance rules. The IUA wants those rules rationalised so insurers can rely on brokers and MGAs for fair value assessments.
