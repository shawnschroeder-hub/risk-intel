01

Reinsurance rate declines may slow by 2028, soft market floor may not be found till 2030: Jefferies

Artemis, reporting Jefferies equity research (analyst opinion) · September 28

Jefferies analysts expect reinsurance price declines to accelerate at the January 2027 renewals and only start to slow in 2028, with a soft-market floor possibly not reached until 2030. They said the pace of softening has been closer to investor fears than to reinsurer management's "softening, but not soft" message. From 2028 they expect softening to shift from price to looser terms and conditions, as happened late in the 2010s soft market.

02

D&O's mixed signals — why the calm at the surface is not the whole story

Insurance Business America, quoting Willis and Aon · September 25

Securities class action filings fell 11% in 2025 to 207 cases, while the median settlement rose 21% to $17 million, a 10-year high. SEC enforcement actions fell 30% in fiscal 2025, but AI-related securities filings reached 22 by Aug. 31, 2026, versus 16 in all of 2025, and four tariff-related suits were filed in 2025. Aon's Jacqueline Waters pointed to legal defense expenses as a structural cost pressure.

Blackstone-backed insurance underwriter The Fidelis Partnership files for IPO

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · September 28 · filing, not yet priced

Bermuda-based specialty underwriter The Fidelis Partnership filed to list on the NYSE under the ticker "TFP." 04

Aon launches $2.5bn lifecycle program for gas power projects

Insurance Business America, reporting an Aon launch · September 28

Aon's Power Lifecycle Program offers up to $2.5 billion of erection all risks and delay in start-up cover during construction of gas power plants, moving to up to $2.5 billion of property damage and business interruption once operating. It combines what are normally separate placements into one structure, led by a panel of London carriers. Aon tied demand to data center growth and rising gas-fired generation.

05

Jury says Apple owes record $5.7B in haptic technology patent case

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · September 28 · jury verdict, appeal planned

A jury found Apple's Taptic Engine infringed two patents held by San Diego-based Taction and awarded $5.7 billion. Taction sued in 2021; a federal judge ruled against it in 2023 before the Federal Circuit revived the case. Apple says it does not use Taction's technology and will appeal.

06

Apple's record $5.7 billion patent loss puts litigation funding back in the spotlight

Insurance Business America · September 28 · federal bill introduced, not out of committee

Court disclosures show the Taction case was financed by Gronostaj Investments and Kenosha Investments, which separate litigation has identified as an indirect subsidiary of Burford Capital. The federal Litigation Funding Transparency Act, introduced in February, has not moved out of committee and would apply only to class actions, MDLs and coordinated proceedings of 100 or more cases, not a single-plaintiff suit like this. North Carolina banned third-party litigation funding outright in June 2026, and several other states have passed disclosure or registration rules.

07

TikTok reaches first state settlement over teen safety claims, agrees to user limits

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · September 28 · update to Friday's trial item — settled

TikTok settled Alabama's case, the first in the multistate litigation to reach trial, agreeing to a two-hour daily limit for teens, pauses after 15 minutes of use and stricter age verification. It will pay Alabama at least $100 million, rising to $300 million if 40 more attorneys general adopt similar agreements within a set period. At least 27 other states and Washington, D.C. have filed similar suits, and TikTok faces thousands of individual lawsuits.

A Santa Fe, New Mexico jury found Meta deceived state residents about how personal information would be shared with third parties and about its handling of hate speech and misinformation. The judge will set the penalties. The case was brought by New Mexico's attorney general in 2021.

11

California comp benefits outpace combined total of three large states

Business Insurance, reporting a California Workers' Compensation Institute analysis · September 25 · data is for 2023

California paid $13.88 billion in workers' comp benefits in 2023, up 7.1% versus 3.8% nationally, and more than New York, Florida and Washington combined. The state had 11.9% of the nation's covered jobs but 21.7% of benefit payments. Payments split roughly evenly between medical and indemnity, at about $6.95 billion each.

12

Nearly 100% of US now covered by NOAA's flood mapping tool

Insurance Journal · September 28

NOAA's Flood Inundation Mapping tool, which estimates land area covered by water from forecast river flows and current conditions, now covers nearly all US residents, up from 10% in 2023 and 60% in 2025. It maps flooding potential at neighborhood scale across 3.3 million river miles. Newly added areas include the Great Lakes region, New England, Florida and the Southeast coast.

Cyber dependencies weaken resilience, warns report

CIR Magazine, reporting ENISA's 2026 Threat Landscape · September 28 · covers 2025 incidents
