01

Bayer shares slip despite Roundup settlement approval

Business Insurance, via Reuters · November 23 · settlement approved Nov 20; analyst views labelled

A Missouri judge on Friday approved Bayer’s $7.25 billion settlement of Roundup weedkiller claims; Bayer had faced approximately 65,000 claims. Bayer says about 200 federal cases are still being litigated. Jefferies analysts said the approval materially reduced the size and uncertainty of Bayer’s glyphosate liabilities.

02

Honda, Toyota recall 1.6 million vehicles over Takata airbag rupture risk

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · November 23 · recall announced Nov 20; companies’ and NHTSA statements

Honda is recalling 810,592 older US cars and SUVs, and Toyota 790,000 vehicles worldwide including 314,000 in the US, because Takata airbag inflators could rupture. NHTSA said the automakers saw early evidence of degradation risk. Toyota expects replacement inflators to be ready by early 2027.

03

Companies’ El Niño anxiety is surging as weather perils loom

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · November 23 · company and consultant statements

Executives mentioned El Niño more than 600 times on third-quarter earnings calls in November, according to Bloomberg data. US seed producer Vylor said drought and El Niño cut $60 million from earnings, and lender Credicorp said it is slowing small-business lending. An Allianz Trade report cited in the article puts output lost to record European summer heat waves at $128 billion.

04

PacifiCorp hails expanded review of wildfire case

Business Insurance, via Reuters · November 23 · appeal pending; no ruling on the merits

The Oregon Supreme Court will review all potential errors in the 2020 wildfire class action against PacifiCorp, after an appeals court wiped out $1.25 billion of awards to 201 plaintiffs over a jury-instruction error. More than 2,400 plaintiffs say the utility’s equipment caused fires that damaged more than 2,000 properties, and Reuters reports potential damages could reach tens of billions of dollars. Briefing deadlines run into early 2027.

05

Construction-defect lawsuit clock starts at occupancy, NC appeals court says

Insurance Journal · November 23 · state appellate ruling

The North Carolina Court of Appeals held in Holton v. Valuebuild Homes that the state’s six-year statute of repose for construction-defect claims starts at occupancy. The certificate of occupancy was issued May 17, 2016, and the homeowners sued in January 2025, more than eight years later.

06

144A catastrophe bond issuance set to break annual issuance record in 2026

Artemis (own market tracking; projection) · November 23

Artemis data show almost $20.66 billion of Rule 144A catastrophe bonds settled so far in 2026, with a further $4.44 billion in the pipeline. That puts full-year 144A issuance on course for almost $25.1 billion, above the 2025 record of almost $24.98 billion. Artemis projects the outstanding market at just over $69.62 billion by year-end.

07

Beazley returns with $200m target for fifth PoleStar Re cyber catastrophe bond

Artemis · November 23 · deal in marketing, not yet priced

Beazley is targeting $200 million or more of cyber catastrophe reinsurance from PoleStar Re Series 5, with cover running through end-2029. Three tranches attach between $600 million and $1.1 billion, with price guidance from 6%–7% to 9.5%–10.5%, according to Artemis’s sources. It replaces a $160 million PoleStar Re bond maturing at year-end.

08

TD Insurance plans biggest cat bond yet

Business Insurance, citing Artemis · November 23 · deal in marketing

TD Insurance is returning to the catastrophe bond market for a third time, seeking at least C$200 million ($139 million) of per-occurrence reinsurance through three-year notes. The cover spans named storms, earthquakes, severe convective storms, winter storms and wildfires in Canada.

09

Cybersecurity experts patch commercial refrigeration flaw after European hacks

Business Insurance, via Reuters · November 23 · security firms’ findings

UK firm Omega Solutions identified a vulnerability in the operating systems of commercial fridges and freezers used by supermarkets, warehouses and restaurants, and Pen Test Partners found further weaknesses. The flaw has now been patched following hacks in Europe.

10

Retailers confront widening violence risks as insurance coverage comes under scrutiny

Business Insurance, citing the National Retail Federation (trade body) and Gallagher Bassett (claims administrator) · November 23

A July National Retail Federation report found 42% of retailers saw more guest-related violence. Violence-related claims are about 1% of workers’ comp claims for Gallagher Bassett’s retail clients but cost 40% more on average. From Jan. 1, 2027, New York retailers with 500 or more employees must provide silent emergency buttons.

How integration, workforce pressures, and A.I. are reshaping workers’ comp

Risk & Insurance, citing a Healthesystems survey (vendor) · November 23 · industry survey

The 2026–27 survey of 451 respondents, run by Healthesystems with Risk & Insurance, found workplace safety was the top concern at 55.3%. Integration ranked first among medical management priorities at 46.3%, and AI/machine learning was the top technology capability at 50.2%.

Russian cargo ship attacked by drones in Gulf of Finland

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · November 21, posted Nov 23 · officials’ statements

Two drones attacked the Russian cargo ship Sparta II about three nautical miles from Russia’s maritime border, according to the Leningrad Region governor, and the vessel drifted into Finnish waters. Finland’s border guard said the crew was evacuated to a nearby cargo vessel. Baltic ports handled 65.3% of Russia’s seaborne grain exports in October, per the Russian Grain Union.
