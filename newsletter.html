01

The 30-day renewal is dead — why brokers are starting earlier than ever

Insurance Business America · September 9 · The reporting does not name the source of the premium data

Insurance Business America reports US commercial insurance pricing fell 2% in the second quarter of 2026, the first quarterly decline since 2017, with commercial property down 6.3% while umbrella rose 5.3% and commercial auto 4.5%. Umbrella has now recorded 35 consecutive quarters of increases. Brokers quoted in the piece say they are opening renewal discussions 120 to 150 days before expiry, and that some established property accounts have seen premium reductions as large as 30%.

02

US P&C insurers post $31.7 billion underwriting gain in first half of 2026

Risk & Insurance, citing Verisk and the American Property Casualty Insurance Association (trade body) · September 8

Verisk and the American Property Casualty Insurance Association put industry net underwriting gain at $31.7bn for the first half of 2026 against $11.6bn a year earlier, with the combined ratio improving to 92.7 from 96.5 and policyholders' surplus reaching $1.3tn. Net written premium growth slowed to 2.1% from 5.2% in the first half of 2025, and net income after taxes rose 53% to $77.8bn. This is the Verisk/APCIA compilation, which sits alongside the AM Best figures carried in yesterday's edition and uses a different underwriting measure.

03

Trump administration stalled a survey of PFAS in wastewater, former EPA officials say

Insurance Journal · September 9 · Account of former officials, not an agency statement

Former EPA officials say a survey of PFAS discharges into wastewater treatment plants was submitted to the White House Office of Management and Budget in October 2024 and has sat unapproved for nearly two years. Stuart Shapiro, a former OMB staffer now a dean at Rutgers, said such reviews typically take around 60 days. Michigan, which collected its own PFAS data and required industrial dischargers to cut releases into treatment plants, recorded substantial reductions.

04

Amazon Boeing 767-300 cargo plane crash at Miami airport kills at least five

Beinsure · September 7 · Still relevant · Cause undetermined

An Amazon Prime Air Boeing 767-300 arriving from San Juan overran the runway at Miami International Airport on Sunday afternoon, killing at least five people and injuring five more. The aircraft was travelling at roughly 129 mph when it left the usable runway, and thunderstorms and gusting winds were reported at the time. The National Transportation Safety Board has deployed a go-team and the cause has not been determined.

05

Retrocession set for comeback as conditions evolve; collateralized tail protection stable: S&P

Artemis, citing S&P Global Ratings (rating agency) · September 9 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous

S&P Global Ratings says retrocession is set for a comeback as pricing and capacity improve, with the uptick concentrated in traditional uncollateralised cover while collateralised tail protection has stayed close to prior-year levels. S&P models 19 of the 20 reinsurers in its benchmark group retaining capital adequacy through a 1-in-250-year catastrophe loss, including an industry-wide loss above $300bn. The benchmark group had ceded about 50% of its modelled 1-in-250 exposure on average as at 1 January 2026.

06

Catastrophe bond market coupon falls 4.5% in August, now 13% lower than a year ago: Plenum

Artemis, citing Plenum Investments (ILS fund manager) · September 9

Plenum Investments reports the catastrophe bond market yield, or coupon available to investors, fell about 4.5% during August to just under 8.9%, against 10.22% in August 2025. The risk spread narrowed to 5.05% from 6.07% a year earlier, while average expected loss across the market rose to 2.50% from 2.24%. Plenum says the market yield has now returned to 2020 levels.

07

Data centre opportunity for ILS capital must be effective in terms of the economics: Swiss Re

Artemis, citing Swiss Re Institute (reinsurer research) · September 9 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous · Projections

Swiss Re Institute projects a $200bn premium opportunity by 2030 from data centres and renewable energy infrastructure, with AI data centre capital expenditure estimated at $1.6tn a year by 2031 and $7.6tn cumulatively between 2026 and 2031. Gianfranco Lot, chief underwriting officer for property and casualty, said the insurance industry is not able to cope with the demand for insurance, and that alternative capital needs to be effective in economic terms. Around 80 to 85% of large peak risks on data centre projects are currently reinsured.

08

Holiday cargo theft keeps climbing as criminals refine their timing and tactics

Risk & Insurance, citing Verisk CargoNet (vendor data) · September 8

Verisk CargoNet recorded 56 cargo theft incidents over the 2025 Labor Day period against 33 in 2021, a 70% rise, and across five holiday periods 194 of 273 incidents fell on the Thursday or Friday before or the Tuesday or Wednesday after the holiday, with California, Texas and Illinois accounting for 130. Food and beverage led targeted commodities in that holiday dataset at 49 incidents, followed by household goods, electronics, vehicles and accessories, and metals. Separately, cargo theft losses exceeded $359m in the first half of 2026, with the average stolen shipment valued at roughly $341,518.

09

Amazon accused in lawsuit of systematic discrimination against pregnant employees

Insurance Journal · September 9 · Allegations in a filed complaint, not findings

Workers' rights group A Better Balance filed a proposed nationwide class action against Amazon in Brooklyn federal court on 8 September, alleging the company denied pregnant warehouse workers accommodations such as seating, bathroom and water breaks and time off for prenatal appointments. The complaint, brought on behalf of four former warehouse employees, alleges violations of the federal Pregnant Workers Fairness Act and New York labour law, and seeks lost pay, punitive damages and an injunction. Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel said the company approved more than 99.9% of such requests in the last year.

10

NCSC warns of growing cyber risks from 'shadow AI'

CIR Magazine, citing the UK National Cyber Security Centre · September 9 · Guidance, not a requirement

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre has warned that employees adopting unapproved AI tools — "shadow AI" — can expose organisations to data loss, compliance breaches and increased cyber risk. The NCSC says staff typically turn to unsanctioned tools because approved options do not meet their needs or because policies are unclear. Its guidance asks organisations to set realistic AI policies, provide secure approved alternatives and improve visibility of AI use, noting that organisations cannot manage risks they are unaware of.

11

California workers' comp medical costs rose 5% in 2025, with medical-legal and equipment services leading the increase

Risk & Insurance, citing the Workers' Compensation Insurance Rating Bureau of California · September 4 · Still relevant

The Workers' Compensation Insurance Rating Bureau of California reports medical service costs per claim rose 5% in 2025 after a 10% increase in 2024, with medical costs making up 52% of total loss payments. The rise came from a 4% increase in average price per transaction and a 1% increase in utilisation, and was concentrated in Southern California at 8% while Northern California was stable. Medical-legal services accounted for 17% of medical payments and medical equipment and other services 13%, rising 47% since 2021 and 55% since 2020 respectively.

Older construction workers show mixed chronic disease picture, but chronic pain and anxiety persist

Risk & Insurance, citing CPWR — The Center for Construction Research and Training · September 9 · Survey years vary by measure

CPWR reports that 28.8% of construction workers aged 51 and over rated their hearing as excellent to very good in 2022, against 46.5% across all industries. Around 32.7% reported anxiety at least monthly in 2024, and 20.0% reported chronic pain daily or on most days in 2023. Recorded musculoskeletal disorder cases fell 29.0% between 2011–12 and 2023–24.

'Flight to quality' creates opening for HDI Global's US push: CEO

Insurance Business America, quoting HDI Global (insurer) · September 9 · Executive's own view

HDI Global chief executive Shadi Albert, who took the role on 13 July, said a flight to quality among buyers is creating an opening for the insurer's US expansion, with cyber and casualty the priority lines. 15

SEC seeks court order for ISS client data sought in ESG probe

Insurance Journal · September 9 · Investigation, not a finding

The Securities and Exchange Commission has asked a federal court to compel proxy adviser Institutional Shareholder Services to hand over four years of records including client identities and voting data, as part of an investigation opened in March into whether ISS is meeting its fiduciary obligations to clients. The SEC is examining whether the firm's proxy voting guidance is driven by a political or policy aim to the detriment of clients' interests, following a December executive order calling on the SEC chairman to restrict proxy advisers' guidance on diversity, equity, inclusion and ESG matters. ISS says it is committed to cooperating but argues that complying would expose it and its clients to retaliation for protected speech.
