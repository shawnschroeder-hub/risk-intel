01

Large commercial accounts see first price drop since 2017, WTW finds

Insurance Business America, citing WTW's Commercial Lines Insurance Pricing Survey (broker research) · September 10

WTW's Commercial Lines Insurance Pricing Survey recorded an aggregate commercial price increase of 0.5% in the second quarter of 2026, down from 2.5% in the first quarter and 3.8% a year earlier, with large accounts registering their first price decline since 2017. The survey measures renewal price change on comparable coverage, drawing on 43 participating companies representing about 20% of the US commercial market. A separate survey by CIAB, covering the first quarter, put commercial property down 5.5%, with 72% of its respondents reporting increased underwriting capacity.

02

Stellantis to recall over 200,000 Jeep vehicles over tire-pressure monitor

Insurance Journal, reporting NHTSA · September 10

Stellantis is recalling 201,976 Jeep vehicles in the US over a software error that may cause the tire pressure monitoring system to fail to detect low pressure or illuminate the warning light. Affected models include certain Wagoneer, Grand Wagoneer, Grand Cherokee, Grand Cherokee L, Grand Cherokee 4XE and Cherokee vehicles. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration says dealers will update the radio frequency hub software free of charge.

03

Risks becoming more global and volatile, value of reinsurance never more evident: Munich Re

Artemis, citing Munich Re (reinsurer) · September 10 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous

Munich Re said insured losses from non-peak perils exceeded $100bn in 2025 for the first time, and that global insured losses passed $100bn for the sixth consecutive year. It put the January 2025 California wildfires at $54bn in economic losses, which it described as the highest ever recorded for that peril. Board members Thomas Blunck and Stefan Golling said the value of reinsurance has never been more evident and that volatility is not a temporary phenomenon.

04

Builders' risk ends where? Brokers face data center handoff puzzle

Insurance Business America, quoting Liberty Mutual (insurer) and citing JLL (property adviser) · September 10

Amy Gross, Liberty Mutual's North America construction team lead, said brokers face uncertainty over where builders' risk cover ends and operational property cover begins on data centre projects, and argued for hybrid policies so coverage is unambiguous at handover. JLL expects global data centre capacity to reach roughly 200 gigawatts by 2030, nearly double its 2025 level, and projects average construction costs rising 6% in 2026 to $11.3m per megawatt excluding technology fit-outs. Gross said insurers are increasingly requiring upfront risk engineering rather than waiving it conditionally.

Catastrophe bond market shifts further towards indemnity triggers and per-occurrence coverage

Artemis, from its own deal directory · September 10

Artemis data shows indemnity triggers accounted for 78% of catastrophe bond limit issued so far in 2026, up from 75.6% in 2025 and 67.5% in 2022. 08

Recall confidence masks traceability weaknesses

CIR Magazine, citing Balloon One (supply chain technology vendor) · September 10 · UK food sector survey

Balloon One reports that 79% of UK chilled, convenience and ready-to-eat food businesses are seeing rising recalls, and 93% believe they could manage a recall quickly and accurately. Against that, 24% would need five hours or more to locate affected stock, 25% still track batch and expiry data on spreadsheets, and 77% must consult two or more separate systems during a recall. It puts the average estimated financial impact of a serious recall at £545,249.

Most firms report climate losses but admit data deficit

CIR Magazine, citing Sweep and Capgemini (vendor research) · September 10 · Loss bands reported in sterling across a five-region survey

Research by Sweep and Capgemini, surveying 1,000 senior business leaders across the US, UK, France, the DACH region and the Nordics, found 94% reported climate-related financial losses over the past two years. Around 30% put those losses above £738,000, while 20% reported minimal impact below £74,000. Some 79% acknowledged insufficient integration of sustainability data across their organisation.

10

Cyber attack concerns rise further

CIR Magazine, citing Proofpoint's 2026 Voice of the CISO study (vendor research) · September 10

Proofpoint's 2026 Voice of the CISO study, covering 1,600 chief information security officers across 16 countries, found 74% of UK CISOs believe their organisation faces material cyber attack risk in the next 12 months, up from 63% in 2025. Some 69% named human risk as their biggest vulnerability, up from 60%, and 71% viewed generative AI security as a risk. Proofpoint's global resident CISO Patrick Joyce said AI is fundamentally changing the CISO mandate, with security leaders asked to guard against the technology and enable its safe adoption at the same time.

"There's a log jam": why global brokerage M&A's biggest buyers could be pulling back

Insurance Business America, citing OPTIS Partners and quoting Aquiline (private equity) · September 10

OPTIS Partners recorded 695 North American agent and broker transactions in 2025, down 12% on 2024 and 24% below the five-year average, with publicly traded brokers' activity down 27% and private equity-backed brokers completing 10% fewer deals.
