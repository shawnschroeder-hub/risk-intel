01

Cranbury fraud leaves $8.7m hole that a $5m policy may not fill

Insurance Business America, citing township statements and The Wall Street Journal · November 11 · no charges filed; coverage not yet confirmed

Cranbury Township, New Jersey, sent about $26 million on September 28 after receiving fraudulent wiring instructions; police say about $17.3 million has been frozen and the remaining $8.7 million has moved through multiple accounts. The Wall Street Journal reported the township’s policy limit at $5 million, and the township says it is still working with its carriers on the terms and amount of any coverage. The township has not said how the false instructions arrived, which the article says will affect whether cyber or crime coverage responds, noting that social engineering sublimits are often far below policy limits.

02

Can workers’ comp rates keep falling if claims frequency levels off?

Insurance Business America, citing The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers and NCCI · November 11 · NCCI figures preliminary

Workers’ compensation premiums fell an average of 3.2% in the second quarter of 2026, the 18th straight quarterly decrease, according to The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers. NCCI’s preliminary figures show lost-time claim frequency fell 2% in accident year 2025, against a long-term average decline of 3.8%, while average medical and indemnity costs per lost-time claim each rose 4%. Private carriers posted a 2025 calendar-year combined ratio of 90.6%, their 12th consecutive underwriting gain, and an AmTrust executive said current pricing is sustainable.

03

Excess and surplus casualty tightens as James River shrinks premium

Insurance Business America, citing James River’s Q3 results and Risk Placement Services (wholesale broker) · November 11 · company results

James River Group’s gross written premium fell 22% in the third quarter, with excess and surplus premium down 19% to $170.4 million even as E&S submissions rose 8%. The company booked $19.2 million of net adverse reserve development on its E&S book, concentrated in a product liability portfolio now in runoff. Risk Placement Services puts E&S casualty loss-cost trends at 12% to 15% and describes the market as fragmented rather than uniformly hard or soft.

04

2026 US Cyber Insurance Rates Stabilize as Reinsurance Risks Persist

Beinsure, citing Howden Re (reinsurance broker) and NAIC data · November 9 · still relevant

US cyber insurance rates have fallen 9.7% since the start of 2024, compared with 24.5% outside the US, including a 5.4% decline in 2026 against Howden Re’s forecast of 4.9%, according to Howden Re. US cyber direct written premiums rose 3.5% to about $9.46 billion, per NAIC data. Howden Re also reports that vulnerabilities added to the MITRE database rose 105% year over year from January to September 2026.

Business Insurance · November 10 · 9th Circuit; allegations denied by SpaceX

The 9th Circuit upheld a ruling that eight former SpaceX engineers’ claims are exempt from the arbitration agreements they signed, rejecting SpaceX’s argument that the federal law letting sexual harassment disputes proceed in court does not cover them. 07

Pa. court limits comp offsets for Social Security increases

Business Insurance · November 10 · en banc ruling; three judges dissented

Pennsylvania’s Commonwealth Court, sitting en banc in McCartney v. Workers’ Compensation Appeal Board, held that employers cannot increase workers’ compensation offsets to capture later Social Security cost-of-living adjustments. The majority found the statute ambiguous and said larger offsets would undermine its purpose of protecting retirees against inflation. Three judges dissented, reading the statute to allow offsets against all Social Security retirement benefits.

Cyber Insurance Demand Rises as Awareness Gaps Persist, Munich Re Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing Munich Re (reinsurer survey) · November 9 · still relevant

Munich Re’s Global Cyber Risk and Insurance Survey 2026, covering more than 9,500 respondents in 20 countries, found 43% of C-level executives say their company is very likely to buy cyber insurance, up from 35% in 2021. Among non-buyers, price was cited as a barrier by 34%, up from 27%, and insufficient scope of services by 26%, up from 11%. Business interruption and liability losses were each cited by 48% as reasons to buy.

10

Warning over security gaps among AI suppliers

CIR, citing Black Kite (cyber risk vendor) · November 11 · vendor research

Black Kite’s 2026 AI Supply Chain Report assessed the external security of 18,160 AI companies and found 34.2% had at least one critical unpatched vulnerability. It found 9.8% had a vulnerability on CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list and 61.7% had weaknesses in DMARC email authentication. Black Kite says its network-activity and botnet findings are externally observed indicators, not confirmed compromises.

New tool targets hidden supply chain risks

CIR · November 10 · vendor product launch

Quantexa and PwC launched a Supply Chain Integrity & Fraud solution that combines a company’s procurement and financial data with external intelligence to map suppliers several tiers removed from its direct operations. The launch cites a PwC survey in which 91% of supply-chain leaders said trade policy and geopolitical risks were forcing significant strategy changes. Quantexa says it has been deployed with retailers, beverage distributors and logistics companies in North America and Europe.
