02

FM Offers $855 Million Resilience Credit for Second Straight Year

Risk & Insurance, citing FM (commercial property mutual) · October 27 · date from the article’s own page; insurer estimate

FM estimates it will return about $855 million to eligible policyholders through a 10% premium offset on policies renewing or reaching their anniversary during 2027. It is the second straight year of the credit, which FM launched in 2022 for climate risk and in 2025 expanded to operational risk and doubled from 5% to 10%. FM cited historically low losses among its policyholders, alongside a separate $1.5 billion membership credit announced in April.

03

Cyber Attacks and Data Loss Rank Among Top Board-Level Risks, WTW Survey Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing WTW’s D&O Liability Survey 2026 (broker survey) · October 28 · date from the article’s own page

Directors and officers surveyed by WTW rank cyber attacks and data loss among their three biggest concerns, behind only health and safety. Only 65% say their fellow directors have the skills to oversee cyber risk, and 55% feel well informed about their organisation’s cyber exposures. 78% report having an incident response plan, and 60% quantify potential cyber losses at least annually.

04

PwC Confirms Cybersecurity Incident Involving Employee Learning Records

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 28 · third-party vendor incident; extortion group’s claims unverified

PwC said on Oct. 23 it had learned of an incident at a third-party vendor hosting employee learning records, a day after the Everest extortion group claimed to have stolen 590 gigabytes of its data. PwC said the affected site holds no client data, sensitive personal data or financial information, and its own network was not affected. It declined to comment on how much data was taken or whether the group’s claims are accurate.

05

Edison Must Face Jury Over Equipment’s Role in Massive California Fire

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg · October 28 · pre-trial ruling, not a verdict

A Los Angeles Superior Court judge refused Southern California Edison’s request to rule that Eaton Fire property owners have no recourse under inverse condemnation, which can make utilities liable without negligence; the issue goes to an initial trial in late January. The judge also rejected insurers’ bid for a ruling without trial letting them recoup claim payouts from Edison. The January 2025 fire killed 19 people and damaged or destroyed more than 9,000 homes and businesses, and Edison says it disagrees with the ruling.

06

Meta and Snap Face Major Legal Test of Youth Social Media Addiction Claims

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 28 · trial opening; jury selection begins Oct 28

Two teenage plaintiffs go to trial against Meta and Snap in Los Angeles, only the second individual trial alleging platforms were deliberately designed to be addictive; the companies lost the first in March and paid $6 million in damages. The cases are bellwethers for more than 3,000 consolidated suits in Los Angeles Superior Court, with over 3,800 related suits pending in federal court in California. Meta is appealing the March verdict on the argument that Section 230 should bar such claims.

10

Safety gaps leave new employees vulnerable beyond orientation

Business Insurance, citing WCRI and Travelers data · October 28

A Workers Compensation Research Institute report found first-year employees accounted for 39% of injuries in 2022–2024, and those with under two years’ tenure for 54%. Travelers’ analysis of more than 1.2 million workers’ comp claims from 2021 to 2025 put first-year employees at about 37% of injuries and 34% of claim costs. Safety specialists from the National Safety Council, Travelers and ICW Group urge scheduled checkpoints, supervisor observation and clear hazard-reporting channels after orientation.

11

California proposes penalties, tighter rules for medical provider networks

Business Insurance · October 26 · proposed rule; public hearing in December

California’s Division of Workers’ Compensation proposed a $1,000 penalty each time a judge or the appeals board finds a medical provider network error unreasonably delayed treatment authorisation. It would add a $500 penalty per provider not given required discount-arrangement disclosures within 30 days, and limit provider directories to participating providers. The division is taking written comments ahead of a December hearing.

12

Court rejects Delta workers comp benefit reduction

Business Insurance · October 27 · Court of Appeals of Georgia; remanded to the comp board

Delta cut a baggage handler’s total disability payments from $725 to $438 a week in July 2024, citing a 2023 physician report. The Court of Appeals of Georgia reversed the cut, finding the report did not specifically release him to return to work or show he could work with restrictions. The case returns to the state workers’ compensation board, including on attorney fees.

Weak dollar flatters marine premium growth as attritional claims build, IUMI finds

Insurance Business America, citing IUMI’s 2026 Stats Report (trade body data) · October 28 · calendar-year 2025 data

Global marine premiums rose 5.5% to $42.6 billion in 2025, which the International Union of Marine Insurance attributes mostly to a weaker US dollar rather than a firmer market. IUMI says cargo loss ratios improved but attritional claims continued to build, pressuring profitability.

Willis urges mining to strengthen resilience as rates fall

CIR Magazine, citing Willis’ Mining Risk Review 2026 (broker report) · October 28

Willis reports rate reductions across major mining insurance lines, with property placements often oversubscribed and global liability capacity above $1.2 billion for an eighth straight year of growth.
