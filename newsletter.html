01

Broad AI Exclusions in Insurance Policies Could Create Uncertainty, Marsh CEO Says

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 16 · broker executive view

Marsh CEO John Doyle said on a post-earnings call that a broad-based AI exclusion in insurance policies could create "real confusion and uncertainty," and he called for a more nuanced way to assess AI risks. Marsh Risk CEO Nick Studer said AI that extends an existing peril cannot be unbundled from the causal chain leading to a loss. Doyle said claim activity involving AI has been very limited so far.

02

Travelers Insurance Reports Solid Underwriting, Record Investment Income for Q3

Insurance Journal, reporting Travelers' third-quarter results (company-reported) · October 16

Travelers reported third-quarter net income of $1.92 billion, essentially flat from $1.89 billion a year earlier, with pre-tax catastrophe losses of $538 million versus $402 million. Business Insurance net written premiums were $5.87 billion, up 4% adjusted for the Canadian divestiture, on record new business of $699 million and 86% retention, with growth in every line except property. Business Insurance posted an 88.9% combined ratio.

03

Specialty insurers weigh AI liability risks, resist exclusions

Business Insurance · October 15 · insurer executive views

Interviews at the Wholesale & Specialty Insurance Association's annual marketplace found specialty insurers more likely to assess AI risks case by case than to exclude them outright. Executives said they do not plan to adopt the optional generative AI exclusions that ISO filed for general liability last year. AmTrust's Erich Bublitz noted that most policies are silent on AI, so insurers may not know their AI exposure.

Moody's estimates insured losses from hurricane Isaias in $2.5bn to $4.5bn range

Artemis, citing Moody's RMS Event Response (catastrophe modeler) · October 16 · modeled estimate; update to earlier Isaias items

Moody's RMS puts insured Isaias losses at $2.5 billion to $4.5 billion with a best estimate of $3.5 billion, covering wind, storm surge and flooding across residential, commercial, industrial, auto and watercraft lines. Artemis lists other published estimates from Verisk's $1 billion to $2 billion for onshore property to Karen Clark & Co.'s $3 billion. Artemis says that at these levels Isaias is not expected to be a significant reinsurance event, aside from a few very low layers.

CCRIF to pay US $17.4m to Panama after M7.7 quake triggers parametric coverage

Artemis, citing CCRIF SPC (parametric risk pool) · October 16

Connecticut expands workers comp benefits for assault victims

Business Insurance · October 15 · enacted; workers' compensation provisions effective Oct 1

Governor Ned Lamont signed H.B. 5003 on May 11, 2026 as Public Act 26-12, and its workers' compensation provisions took effect October 1. Covered employees injured in on-the-job assaults receive 100% of average weekly earnings without the usual weekly benefit cap. Coverage applies to teachers and other employees of specified public education boards and to health care providers at qualifying facilities.

09

Appeals court revives wage claims over unpaid wait at grocery chain

Business Insurance · October 15 · 2nd Circuit ruling at the pleading stage

In Santos v. Lidl, the 2nd US Circuit Court of Appeals revived federal and New York wage claims by a store clerk and stocker that a lower court had dismissed as a non-compensable preliminary activity. The majority found she plausibly alleged compensable waiting time because she had to report ready to work but could not enter the store or reach the time clocks, about once a week. One judge dissented, and the ruling allows the claims to proceed rather than deciding them.

Businesses struggle to quantify nature-related risks

CIR Magazine, citing a Biodiversify and Little Blue Research survey (advocacy research) · October 16

In a survey of 229 sustainability professionals, 72% said their organisations have started integrating nature into business processes, up from 64% in 2024, and 16% report full integration. Some 49% cited difficulty quantifying nature-related risks and opportunities, and 42% cited challenges assessing supply chain impacts. The survey was published ahead of COP17, which begins on October 19.

Lloyd's issues guidance and expectations for broker-originated reinsurance structures

Artemis, reporting a Lloyd's market bulletin · October 16 · framework takes effect January 1, 2027

Lloyd's set out a framework for structures in which a broker brings capital-market or asset-manager backing to a portfolio placed at Lloyd's, following Aon's reported partnership with Blackstone. Proposals must align with Lloyd's cross-cycle 95% net combined operating ratio target, and such structures may initially hold up to 10% of an individual broker's treaty portfolio. Brokers cannot own or capitalise the structures, and the member aligned to the managing agent must provide at least 19.99% of capital.
