Hurricane Isaias strengthens, ~Cat 2 landfall at single-digit billion insured loss potential forecast

Artemis, citing the National Hurricane Center and Gallagher Re (reinsurance broker) · October 8 · forecast, subject to change

Isaias became a Category 1 hurricane early on Oct. 8, with sustained winds reaching 80 mph, and the NHC described a Category 2 landfall between Mississippi and the Florida Panhandle as possible, late Friday or early Saturday. 02

US Oil Producers Ramp Down Gulf Output 25% on Storm Isaias

Insurance Journal, citing Bloomberg · October 8

By Wednesday afternoon about 25% of Gulf crude output and 16% of natural gas production had been shut in, with workers evacuated from eight platforms. Grid operator MISO declared conservative operations in its Gulf Coast region through Oct. 10, and Mexico's largest fuel-import port, Tuxpan, has been closed since late Monday. Enki Research estimated damages could exceed $4.5 billion if the storm's effects reach Pensacola.

03

Business interruption claims soar: Allianz report

Business Insurance, citing Allianz Commercial claims analysis (insurer data) · October 8

Allianz Commercial says average business interruption claim values have risen more than 30% a year over the past two years, and the average BI claim now exceeds €850,000 ($951,575), about 70% more than the average property damage claim. Fire and explosion is the costliest BI cause, at more than 40% of claim value, and caused nine of the 10 costliest man-made events. The analysis covers 7,888 claims worth about €6.74 billion from 2021 to 2025.

04

Insurers must defend Walmart in opioid lawsuits: appeals court

Business Insurance · October 7 · appellate ruling; defense costs only

The Arkansas Court of Appeals unanimously upheld an order requiring Walmart's primary insurers (two AIG units) and dozens of excess insurers to pay for its defense in more than 2,400 opioid lawsuits. The court rejected the argument that the suits allege intentional conduct rather than an "accident," reasoning that the core claim is a failure to put proper controls in place. The ruling does not decide settlement coverage, and Delaware courts have ruled the other way for Albertsons, Rite Aid and CVS.

05

Texas suits add to countertop silicosis liability crisis

Business Insurance · October 7 · pending litigation

Liberty Mutual units sued artificial stone makers Wilsonart and Architectural Surfaces Group in Texas federal court, citing a combined 230 cases brought by fabricators who contracted silicosis. Since May, juries have awarded silicosis victims $47 million, $17.45 million and $7.1 million against stone makers and distributors. Travelers and an Argo Group unit are separately contesting coverage for hundreds more silica suits.

06

Private insurers double commercial flood market share: GAO

Business Insurance, citing a GAO report · October 7 · report released Oct. 1

The GAO found private insurers' share of commercial flood policies rose from 23% in 2018 to 48% in 2025, and their residential share from 6% to 11%. NFIP policies fell from about 5.1 million to 4.7 million over the period, and the program's commercial building limit has stayed at $500,000 since 1994.

07

Southern Ontario severe storm outbreak industry loss initially estimated C$563m by CatIQ

Artemis, citing CatIQ (loss-index provider) · October 8 · initial estimate

CatIQ's initial estimate puts insured losses from the Sept. 2–3 severe storm outbreak in Southern Ontario, including Toronto, at C$563 million, a figure that may rise as claims are processed. The estimate covers commercial and residential property, auto claims and loss adjustment expenses. The outbreak brought large hail, damaging winds, flash flooding and multiple confirmed tornadoes from Windsor to Ottawa.

AI exclusions struggle to gain traction

Business Insurance, quoting EPIC, Willis, Zurich North America and Coalition executives · October 7 · conference views

Executives at the Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers' Insurance Leadership Forum said AI exclusions are not gaining wide acceptance, with the market preferring to handle AI exposure through underwriting. ISO filed optional generative-AI exclusions for general liability last year, but Zurich North America's CEO said Zurich does not use blanket AI exclusions. Coalition's chief revenue officer said some brokers told it they would move business if insurers imposed them.

09

Data center boom brings premium growth, aggregation risks

Business Insurance, quoting Aon, Willis, Liberty Mutual and EPIC executives · October 7 · broker and carrier views

Aon's global head of property said data centers could generate more than $100 billion in new premiums over the next five to 10 years, and that Aon could likely place limits above $10 billion on a project now. Insurers' main struggle is tracking accumulation, since phased campuses leave builders risk and operational property exposure at the same site. EPIC's chief client officer said property and business interruption capacity is plentiful but professional liability is much more limited.

10

Cargo Vans, Work Pickups Struggled in IIHS Crash Avoidance Tests

Claims Journal, reporting IIHS ratings · October 8

In new IIHS front crash prevention ratings, cargo vans and heavy-duty pickups scored worse than passenger vehicles, with many marginal and poor ratings. The Chevrolet Express 2500 and GMC Savana 2500, which lack automatic emergency braking, rated poor in both vehicle and pedestrian tests, while the Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 2500 rated good for vehicle-to-vehicle prevention. IIHS says crashes involving heavy- or medium-duty vehicles or light vans killed 6,215 people in 2024.
