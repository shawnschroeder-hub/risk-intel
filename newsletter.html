US Supreme Court weighs bid by oil companies to avoid climate lawsuit

Business Insurance · October 5 · oral argument scheduled today; update to Sept 30 item

The Supreme Court is scheduled to hear arguments Monday on ExxonMobil and Suncor Energy's bid to dismiss a suit by Boulder, Colorado officials seeking damages for climate change harms under state law. The Trump administration backs the companies' position that federal authority over air pollution preempts such claims, and nearly 60 similar suits by state and local governments are pending. Justice Samuel Alito has recused himself, and a decision is expected by June 30, 2027.

02

Sedgwick: Property claims face delays, are growing more complex

Insurance Journal, citing Sedgwick's Loss Adjusting Insights report (claims administrator) · October 5

Sedgwick's annual Loss Adjusting Insights report says property claims are getting slower and more complex, citing lead times for specialized equipment that have more than doubled over five years and a construction industry needing 349,000 more workers this year. It expects a quarter of claim adjusters to retire by the end of 2027. Verisk data cited in the report show catastrophe claims made up 43% of U.S. assignments in Q2 2026, up from 34% five years earlier.

03

Energy casualty underwriters want brokers to look harder at the contracts

Insurance Business America, citing Markel (insurer view) · October 5

Markel's U.S. energy product line leader says contracts between equipment manufacturers and operations and maintenance providers are "probably an underestimated exposure," particularly in battery storage. He says contractual ambiguity drives up legal costs when disputes arise, and urges brokers to confirm insureds have qualified legal contract review and that contractors carry adequate limits. Developers planned 86 GW of new U.S. utility-scale capacity in 2026, including 24 GW of battery storage.

04

Insurers must cover Silicon Valley Bank's loss in loan fraud

Business Insurance · October 2 · federal district court ruling

A federal judge in the Eastern District of North Carolina ruled that Chubb's Federal Insurance and W.R. Berkley's Berkley Regional must cover Silicon Valley Bank's loss under fidelity bonds after a fund manager used forged partnership and subscription agreements to obtain a $150 million credit line. The judge rejected the insurers' argument that emailed PDFs could not be "original" documents, finding electronic documents can serve as originals for loan transactions. The bank funded about $95 million and lost more than $70 million; each bond carries a $15 million limit.

08

Life sciences risk is shifting and coverage may not follow

Insurance Business America, citing a Zurich Insurance Group report (insurer) · October 5

A Zurich report, based on a survey of 62 specialists and interviews with 34 experts, says life sciences risks are evolving faster than insurance coverage. It points to the EU's revised Product Liability Directive, which from December 2026 treats software and AI systems as products, and to liability for failed AI systems spreading across device makers, software developers, cloud providers and clinicians. It also flags long-tail exposure from outcome-based contracts and cyber risk from single-source suppliers.

09

Worker's Virginia filings block Tennessee comp benefits

Business Insurance · October 2 · Tennessee comp court ruling

A Tennessee Court of Workers' Compensation Claims judge ruled that a Virginia resident employed by Tennessee-based Pollard Management cannot pursue Tennessee benefits for a back injury after actively pursuing Virginia claims for the same injury. The worker filed two Virginia claims in April 2025 and confirmed that choice to the adjuster before hiring a Tennessee attorney. The judge found he was unlikely to establish Tennessee jurisdiction at a full hearing.

10

Louisiana court upholds comp forfeiture over undisclosed injuries

Business Insurance · October 2 · state appellate ruling

Louisiana's Second Circuit Court of Appeal affirmed the forfeiture of workers' comp benefits for a Willis-Knighton Health System employee who answered "no" on 2022 and 2024 medical questionnaires about prior workplace accidents and recommended surgery. Records showed a prior neck injury at another employer with cervical fusion recommended, plus separate knee and foot injuries from a workplace fall. The court found the false answers concerned the same condition at issue and hindered the employer's ability to seek second injury fund reimbursement.

Hackers breached propulsion system of US-bound oil tanker

Insurance Journal (Bloomberg) · October 5 · update to Sept 21 item; investigation ongoing

FBI and Coast Guard investigators found that hackers gained temporary access to the digital propulsion system of the VL Prosperity, a very large crude carrier approaching Texas this summer that agents boarded in late August after it lost communications. The cause and responsible party remain under investigation, and the FBI says there are no reports of operational disruption, physical danger to crews or environmental impact. By September, U.S. agencies were tracking cyber threats against nearly 20 vessels worldwide.
