01

Isaias: 47,000 Claims and $582M in Losses in Florida So Far, OIR Reports

Insurance Journal, citing carrier reports to the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation · October 19 · early claims data; update to last week's Isaias estimates

Carriers have reported about 47,000 Hurricane Isaias claims and an estimated $582 million in insured losses in Florida, about a week after the Oct. 9 Panhandle landfall. Santa Rosa County accounts for 22,894 claims and Escambia for nearly 13,700, and about 12% of claims had closed by Oct. 15, nearly all without payment. For comparison, Hurricane Sally in 2020 produced 72,000 claims and $577 million in losses within six weeks.

02

How One Hacker Used AI Agents, a Few Thousand Dollars to Break Into Websites

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 19 · researcher findings; victims' statements as reported

Reuters reports that a Chinese-speaking hacker used AI agents to break into websites of more than 100 companies, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, in a campaign that began Sept. 25 and, per a security researcher, targeted e-commerce sites in the U.S., Canada, Australia and Japan. A successful intrusion typically took about 23 hours, and the campaign used roughly $5,000 in AI tokens, or about $50 per successful hack. Deere reported no indication sensitive information was accessed, SharkNinja said a standalone product-support site was hit and contained, and Cummins is investigating a breach of a third-party vendor's website.

03

Late August Euro SCS and hail event "Raphaela" estimated near EUR 3.3bn industry loss: PERILS

Artemis, citing PERILS AG (industry loss-data agency) · October 19 · initial estimate; follows the early-September Gallagher Re estimate

PERILS AG initially estimates insured industry losses from the Aug. 26–28 severe convective storm and hail outbreak at EUR 3,296 million across property and motor lines in France, Germany, Switzerland and Italy. PERILS says Switzerland saw one of the largest catastrophe insured loss events on record for its industry, and that this is the second European convective storm event above EUR 2 billion in 2026. Hail measured 4–8 cm in all four countries, with stones up to 13 cm in Italy, and no storm-related fatalities were reported.

06

Litigation costs keep outpacing commercial auto rate increases

Insurance Business America, citing an AM Best special report · October 19 · rating-agency analysis

AM Best says first-year commercial auto liability loss payments grew 6.4% a year from 2014 to 2024, more than double the 2.8% average economic inflation rate, despite 14 consecutive years of rate increases. The line posted a $4.9 billion underwriting loss in 2024, and AM Best estimates it is under-reserved by $4 billion to $5 billion. AM Best attributes the gap to rising jury awards, more attorney involvement and third-party litigation funding, and calls the pressures structural rather than cyclical.

Appeals Court Blocks Class Actions Over Tesla's Self-Driving Claims

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · October 19 · 9th Circuit ruling dated Oct 16; procedural, not on the merits

A three-judge panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals threw out class certification orders covering California Tesla buyers who allege false advertising of the company's full self-driving capability, finding too many individualized questions about what buyers saw before purchase. The ruling does not address the merits; the lawsuit remains pending, but the buyers cannot currently pursue their claims together as a class. Tesla denies the claims, and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce filed a brief supporting Tesla.

Uber Wins Bid to Dismiss £250 Million Black Cab Driver Suit

Claims Journal, carrying Bloomberg · October 19 · London ruling; claimants may appeal

A judge dismissed a £250 million (about $333 million) claim by more than 13,000 London black cab drivers alleging Uber improperly obtained a London operating licence, holding the claims were time-barred because the relevant public material could have been discovered by February 2018. The claim was first notified to Uber in 2019, and after a litigation funder withdrew, no further letter was sent for about four and a half years. The firm that ran the claim said it was disappointed and is considering its options.

Fire Remains Costliest Cause of Business Interruption Globally as Claim Severity Surges

Risk & Insurance, citing an Allianz Commercial claims analysis · October 10 · still relevant; insurer-published data

Allianz Commercial's analysis of 7,888 business interruption claims worth about EUR 6.74 billion from 2021 to 2025 found fire and explosion the costliest cause, at over 40% of claim value, with natural catastrophes second at 34%. The average BI claim exceeded EUR 850,000, about 70% above the average property damage claim, and average claim values rose more than 30% a year over the past two years. The two costliest non-natural-catastrophe events were both fires at semiconductor factories.

When someone else's outage becomes your claim

Insurance Business America, citing Allianz Commercial · October 19 · insurer-published data; feature

Allianz Commercial found contingent business interruption made up about 15% of the value of cyber claims above EUR 1 million in the first half of 2025, up from 6% in 2024. Its global head of cyber claims says faulty software updates, configuration mistakes and infrastructure failures now drive a meaningful share of large cyber BI claims. Cyber BI waiting periods are typically eight to 12 hours, and cover usually runs 90 to 180 days versus 12 to 24 months for property.

Allianz must face cooler coverage suit over finger injuries

Business Insurance · October 15 · federal district court ruling on the duty to defend

A federal court in the Northern District of Illinois ruled that an Allianz unit must defend Igloo Products, a unit of Dometic Group, in some of six underlying suits over about 1 million recalled 90-quart coolers whose handle allegedly pinched users' fingers. The judge found four of the six suits plausibly alleged bodily injury from an "occurrence," held that the duty to defend turns on the facts alleged rather than the legal theories, and found two suits outside coverage. Allianz had issued Dometic four commercial general liability policies covering 2021 to 2025.

Cal/OSHA proposes $212,500 in fines over fatal tunnel collapse

Business Insurance · October 16 · proposed penalties; one contractor has appealed

Cal/OSHA proposed $212,500 in fines against two contractors over an April 14 collapse inside the Canyon Tunnel project near Knights Ferry, California, that killed a geologist and seriously injured another worker. Drill Tech Drilling & Shoring faces $95,000, including a "willful-serious" tunnel support violation, and design firm Provost & Pritchard Engineering Group faces $117,500; investigators found both let work continue without removing or adequately supporting identified dangerous ground. Provost & Pritchard has appealed, and a separate Cal/OSHA investigation is ongoing.

Alabama high court upholds airline's liability for comp retaliation

Business Insurance · October 16 · state supreme court ruling, over four dissents

The Alabama Supreme Court upheld a finding that Envoy Air fired an employee in retaliation for her workers' compensation claim after a 2018 injury at Mobile Regional Airport, and a Mobile County jury's $675,000 award ($250,000 for mental anguish and $425,000 in punitive damages). The court directed the trial court to explain in writing why it refused to reduce the damages but did not decide whether the awards were excessive. Four justices dissented, arguing the firing rested on a separate reason, a suspected falsified doctor's note.

Lloyd's issues guidance and expectations for broker-originated reinsurance structures

Artemis, reporting a Lloyd's market bulletin · October 16 · framework effective Jan 1, 2027

Lloyd's issued a market bulletin setting a framework for broker-originated reinsurance structures that takes effect Jan. 1, 2027, anchored to its cross-cycle 95% net combined operating ratio target. Under it, brokers cannot own or capitalise the structures, the member aligned to the managing agent must provide at least 19.99% of capital, and lead reinsurers must provide at least 25% of capacity. Initially the structures can hold up to 10% of an individual broker's treaty portfolio.
