US tort costs hit $580bn as casualty market feels the strain

Insurance Business America, citing US Chamber Institute for Legal Reform and Triple-I (advocacy/trade research) · Nov 2 · report published June 2026
US tort costs reached $579.7 billion in 2023, up from $299.7 billion in 2014, according to a US Chamber Institute for Legal Reform study produced with Triple-I; the 2023 figures are projections. Costs grew 10.38% a year over the period, against 5.17% for nominal GDP and 2.74% for inflation. Commercial lines accounted for $377.7 billion, of which other liability was $249.3 billion.

Property softening levels off as casualty diverges: Baldwin

Insurance Business America, citing The Baldwin Group Market Pulse (broker data) · Nov 1
The Baldwin Group’s Q3 Market Pulse puts US commercial property rates down 8.0%, the sixth straight quarterly decline and nearly matching Q2’s 8.1% fall. General liability rose 3.5%, commercial auto 2.9% and umbrella 5.6%, while workers’ compensation was down 0.1% and cyber up 2.0%, its highest reading since early 2023. Baldwin says its index shows the direction of rate change rather than exact rates, because it also reflects exposure, limit and deductible changes.

Gallagher sees buyers increasing limits as property rates fall

Business Insurance · Oct 29 · broker earnings commentary
Gallagher’s CEO said clients are “using the savings to buy back coverage” as property pricing eases. Gallagher reported global property renewal premiums down 6% in Q3, against a 10% fall in Q2, with casualty up 4%, professional lines up 2% and workers’ compensation up 1%. Third-quarter revenue rose 18.2% to $3.93 billion, with 5% organic growth.

CNA’s profit halves on long-term care reserves, cat losses

Business Insurance · Nov 2
CNA’s Q3 net income fell about 51% to $196 million, driven by a $137 million after-tax long-term care reserve charge and higher catastrophe losses. The property/casualty combined ratio rose to 97% from 92.8%, including 2.8 points of catastrophe losses and 2.4 points of prior-year reserve development. Commercial lines ran at 99.7%, up from 92.7%, while net written premium rose 3%.

Some trends signal potential hope for US commercial auto: AM Best

Insurance Journal, citing AM Best (rating agency) · Oct 30
AM Best reports US commercial auto posted a $2.2 billion underwriting loss in 2025, a 55% improvement on 2024, with adverse reserve development at a 10-year low. The line has lost money on underwriting every year since 2015, and liability coverage, 77% of net premiums written, lost more than $4.6 billion in 2025. Physical damage produced a $2.4 billion gain, which AM Best links to stronger fleet monitoring and maintenance.

Impersonation fraud surge exposes gaps in standard cyber cover

Insurance Business America, citing Allstate Identity Protection and the FTC · Nov 2
Many crime and cyber policies carry a “voluntary parting” exclusion that can bar cover when an insured authorises a transfer, even under deception. Allstate Identity Protection data show impersonation scams made up nearly 60% of its cases from January to September 2026. The FTC reports Americans lost $3.5 billion to imposter scams in 2025, nearly three times the 2020 total.

Stonemaker named in verdicts totaling $100M over deadly disease

Business Insurance · Oct 30 · jury verdicts
California juries have returned silicosis verdicts of $67.7 million and $33 million involving engineered-stone maker Caesarstone, with fabrication shops also found partly liable. In the larger case the jury found the product design defective and awarded $60 million in non-economic damages; in the $33 million case it rejected Caesarstone’s misuse defence. California’s worker safety agency drafted a rule in September to ban silica-based artificial stone.

US court reinstates ‘Sunday Ticket’ class action verdict against NFL

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Nov 2 · appeal ruling; damages to be retried
The 9th Circuit reinstated a Los Angeles jury’s roughly $4.7 billion class-action verdict against the NFL over Sunday Ticket pricing, which the trial judge had thrown out in 2024. The appeals court held the judge wrongly rejected the plaintiffs’ expert witnesses but ordered a new trial on damages. The NFL said it is weighing further court review.

Bayer wins Roundup weedkiller cancer trial while awaiting settlement ruling

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Oct 30 · settlement not yet approved
A St. Louis jury ruled unanimously for Bayer’s Monsanto on Oct 29 in the first Roundup trial since the US Supreme Court’s June 2026 ruling that federal law requires herbicide labels to match the EPA-approved label. Bayer’s proposed $7.25 billion settlement of tens of thousands of claims still awaits approval by a Missouri state judge. Bayer faces about 65,000 Roundup claims in US courts.

GM to pay $220M to settle privacy claims over sale of driver data to insurers

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Oct 30 · announced settlement
General Motors will pay $220 million to settle claims by 35 states and the District of Columbia that it sold Smart Driver behaviour and location data to brokers, who sold it to insurers that used it to set and sometimes raise rates. GM must limit the driving data it collects and obtain express consent before collecting or sharing it. The reporting does not say whether a court has approved the settlement.

Cat bond risk spreads may decrease 10% in 2027, but market healthy and growing: Plenum

Artemis, citing Plenum Investments (fund manager view) · Oct 30
Plenum Investments says catastrophe bond risk spreads have tightened about 15% on last year and may fall about another 10% in 2027, depending on loss activity. 3

AI’s building boom exposes a cargo insurance blind spot

Insurance Business America, quoting Marsh (broker) · Nov 2
Marsh’s US and Canada cargo practice leader says the AI infrastructure build-out is widening the gap between the insured value of critical equipment such as turbines, cooling systems and chips and the losses if it is delayed or damaged in transit. Under many sale terms the manufacturer insures equipment only to destination, which may not cover the buyer’s delay penalties or liquidated damages. Marsh reports more uptake of delay-in-start-up cover for project cargo, particularly for data centres.

Four in five manufacturers face monthly downtime – Aviva

CIR Magazine, citing Aviva (insurer survey) · Oct 30
An Aviva survey of 500 senior decision-makers at small and mid-sized UK manufacturers found 84% suffer unplanned downtime at least monthly and 18% weekly. Supply-chain disruption was the most cited cause (22%), followed by cyber incidents (17%), with equipment failure and staff shortages at 14% each. 79% are worried about the cost of replacing machinery, plant or equipment after a breakdown, fire or other major event.

Washington paper mill faces $77,800 in workplace safety fines

Business Insurance · Oct 30 · citations
Washington’s Department of Labor & Industries has cited Nippon Dynawave’s Longview paper mill for $77,800 in fines. The violations include failing to protect employees from extremely hot materials and engulfment hazards, and leaving the paper machine pulp system out of a required hazardous-materials piping safety program.

Supermarket refrigerators hit by cyber attack

CIR Magazine · Nov 1 · no attribution established
Attackers altered settings on remotely controlled refrigeration and freezer systems at supermarkets in Ireland, Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden and Estonia, including stores of Musgrave, Ahold Delhaize, Carrefour Belgium, Coop and ICA. Retailers pulled chilled and frozen food, and at one Swedish store a freezer was set to 50°C. The incidents follow a larger October attack in France; no link has been established and no group has claimed responsibility.

UK businesses underprepared for converging disruption risks

CIR Magazine, citing Aon (broker research) · Nov 2
Aon’s UK business disruption report found 30.8% of organisations suffered significant financial loss from supply-chain disruption, 27.3% from business interruption and 9.3% from cyber incidents. Only 21.6% to 24.6% have formal risk management plans for these three risks, and 12.4% to 16% have quantified their potential losses.
