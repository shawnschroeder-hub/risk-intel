Property rates set to continue falling, liability pressures ease: Willis

Business Insurance, citing Willis (broker outlook) · Oct 1 · broker forecast
Willis projects property rates falling 5% to 15% for single-insurer programs and 15% to 25% or more for shared and layered programs, with its own second-quarter book averaging a 14.5% decrease. General liability is forecast to rise 2% to 10% or more, while large-account workers' comp ranges from a 3% decrease to a 2% increase. Cyber is expected between down 5% and up 5%, with D&O relatively flat.

Zurich Insurance completes acquisition of Beazley

Insurance Journal · Oct 1 · deal closed
Zurich Insurance Group completed its £8.1 billion ($10.7 billion) acquisition of Lloyd's insurer Beazley, paying shareholders 1,310 pence per share. Beazley now operates as a Zurich subsidiary with Adrian Cox continuing as CEO, and its six Lloyd's syndicates join Zurich's specialty business. The combined specialty operation is expected to write about $15 billion of gross premium a year.

Bayer faces trial test of remaining Roundup claims after Supreme Court win

Insurance Journal · Oct 1 · trial under way; no verdict
A trial that began Sept. 29 tests whether design-defect claims against Bayer's Monsanto unit can proceed after the Supreme Court's June 2026 ruling in the company's favor, a theory the court did not directly address. Three plaintiffs outside Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion class settlement allege Roundup was defectively designed and caused their non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Bayer faces about 65,000 remaining claims, and the EPA's glyphosate re-evaluation is expected to finish in late 2026.

Lloyd's underwriters lose bid to revive D&O exclusion fight with former Lordstown Motors

Insurance Business America · Oct 1 · ruling Sept 22; NY appellate court
New York's Appellate Division, First Department denied Lloyd's underwriters' request to reargue a June ruling requiring them to advance defense costs despite a retroactive date exclusion. The primary D&O policy carried a $5 million limit, with at least $45 million of excess coverage above it, for securities suits and regulatory probes over alleged misstated vehicle orders. The court held insurers must advance defense costs where not every cause of action depends on pre-policy conduct.

Workers in first two years on job account for 54% of comp injuries: WCRI

Business Insurance, citing the Workers Compensation Research Institute · Sep 30
WCRI's analysis of 8 million non-COVID claims found workers with under two years on the job had 54% of workers' comp injuries in 2022–2024, though they were about a third of workers. Nearly 40% of injuries happened in a worker's first year and 16% in the first three months. Construction and mining had just 7% of injuries but the highest cost per claim, nearly $40,000 for claims with more than seven days of lost time.

New York approves 22% workers' compensation cost reduction

Insurance Journal · Jul 17 · older but relevant — takes effect today, Oct 1
New York's Department of Financial Services approved a 21.9% workers' comp loss cost reduction that takes effect Oct. 1, 2026. The state estimates savings of more than $1 billion for businesses, about $1,779 per employer. It credits lower lost-time claim frequency over the past three years and workplace safety efforts.

Newsom signs cold-storage legislation

Business Insurance · Sep 29 · signed into law; effective Jan. 1, 2027
California's AB 817 requires cold-storage facilities of at least 20,000 square feet to set up a contingency fund of up to $20 million for emergency community relief, through a dedicated account, surety bond or insurance policy. SB 716 raises penalties to $50,000 per violation for nonresidential structures when violations lead to a gubernatorial emergency. Both take effect Jan. 1, 2027, first in the Boyle Heights area, expanding statewide from July 1, 2028.

Generali's aggregate reinsurance in focus as European cats and weather bite in 2026

Artemis, citing Jefferies (investment bank analysts) · Oct 1 · analyst view
European catastrophe and weather losses in 2026, including Windstorm Kristin at a €1.727 billion industry loss, July hailstorms and wildfires, have pushed Generali toward its aggregate reinsurance trigger. Insurance Journal · Oct 1 · preliminary regulator study
The European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority released a preliminary study finding extreme heat poses material risks to insurance lines covering medical costs and income protection. EIOPA Chair Petra Hielkema said climate change also has quieter, less visible consequences through heat waves' effects on human health.
