P&C industry's best half in years hides a casualty problem

Insurance Business America, citing AM Best (rating agency) · Sep 8
AM Best reports US P&C net underwriting income of $31.2bn in the first half of 2026 against $10.9bn a year earlier, with the combined ratio improving four points to 92.5 and catastrophe losses falling to 6.2 points from 10.8. Net written premiums grew 3% and net income rose 55% to $77.8bn. Against that, commercial auto carried $2bn of reserve deficiencies in 2025 and has now seen 59 consecutive quarters of rate increases, with bodily injury and commercial liability losses continuing to worsen.

MV Dali pushes P&I market into uncharted overspill territoryReserved, not settled

Insurance Business America, citing a Gallagher Specialty midyear review (broker research) · Sep 8
The MV Dali's March 2024 collision with Baltimore's Francis Scott Key Bridge is now reserved at over $2.8bn, a figure that has exhausted the International Group's pooled reinsurance limit and entered the collective overspill layer, which had never previously been called upon. A reinsurer initially declined to pay $180m and member clubs temporarily covered the shortfall before the reinsurer agreed to honour the claim. Programme limits were raised to $3.35bn at the February renewal, and the twelve clubs held $6.8bn of combined free reserves, with no member assessments triggered.

Cyber premiums are falling as AI attacks accelerate – Moody's

Insurance Business America, citing Moody's (rating agency) · Sep 8
Moody's reports average cyber premiums fell roughly 11% in 2025, with global cyber premium at $15.3bn in 2024 and projected to exceed $30bn by 2030, still under 1% of global property and casualty premium. Ransomware drives about 60% of large cyber claims by value, and 29% of executives now name cyber as their top business risk. Moody's warns that property, casualty and business interruption policies not written affirmatively for cyber can carry unintended silent cyber exposure, with outcomes turning on physical-loss requirements, exclusions and causation wording.

The hidden coverage gaps behind America's terrorism backstop

Insurance Business America, quoting the Insurance Information Institute (trade body) · Sep 8
Mark Friedlander of the Insurance Information Institute says businesses can still be exposed under the federal terrorism programme, because terrorism cover is offered rather than automatic and underlying policies commonly exclude nuclear, biological, chemical and radiological events. A cyber-related loss engages the programme only where the cover sits in an eligible line and the event meets the certification requirements. Friedlander says uncertainty around reauthorisation could prompt insurers to pull back terrorism coverage, raise prices or add restrictive exclusions.

No return to indiscriminate soft-market behaviour in reinsurance: Storr, AntaresUnderwriter's own view

Artemis · Sep 8 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous
Mat Storr, head of reinsurance at Antares Global, said he expects an orderly renewal rather than a dramatic shift in pricing, despite abundant capital in the market. He argued reinsurers remain focused on portfolio quality, transparent data and risk-adjusted returns rather than deploying capital for growth. He suggested buyers with well-performing portfolios may continue to see the better outcomes.

Viewpoint: reinsurance market to experience further softening, M&A on ample capacity

Insurance Journal — signed viewpoint by Fitch Ratings (rating agency) · Sep 8
Brian Schneider, senior director at Fitch Ratings, writes that record capacity from both traditional reinsurers and alternative capital continues to outpace reinsurance demand, with the 2026 renewals favouring buyers and property rates down substantially. He expects return on average equity to stay attractive in the low teens as reinsurers remain selective, and further M&A on ample capacity. Total alternative capital outstanding reached a record of more than $144bn at 30 June 2026, with catastrophe bonds at $63bn against $59bn a year earlier.

Cat bonds and casualty sidecars buoyant, but investors still don't like surprises: Josefs, S&P

Artemis, citing S&P Global Ratings (rating agency) · Sep 7 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous
S&P Global Ratings credit analyst Maren Josefs said cat bonds and casualty sidecars have been the two main stories in the ILS market this year. Average modelled expected loss in cat bonds has risen from 1.86% in 2023 to 2.85%. She cautioned that casualty sidecars, which typically run five to seven years and up to ten against one to three for property, have not yet reached maturity without losses, and that "investors don't like surprises."

Shipping's two-tier system is now a structural risk, nations warn

Insurance Business America, citing the Consultative Shipping Group (government grouping) · Sep 8
The Consultative Shipping Group, 18 maritime nations representing more than a fifth of global trade by tonnage, warned that an unregulated shadow fleet operating outside standard insurance, safety and transparency frameworks has created a two-tier shipping system. The group said that when shipping supply chains fragment, the global economy fragments with it, citing the Strait of Hormuz and the surge in tanker war-risk premiums after the February 2026 conflict. It pointed to the Caroline Bezengi, a shadow-fleet vessel carrying roughly 800,000 barrels of Russian crude when struck by a limpet mine with no traditional P&I cover in place.

Warning over inland waterway supply chain risks

CIR Magazine, citing TT Club (transport insurer) · Sep 8
TT Club says recurring drought and low-water restrictions on inland waterways should be treated as a business continuity risk rather than an occasional environmental problem, citing vessel loading constraints, navigation bottlenecks, groundings, higher freight costs and knock-on disruption to ports and warehousing. Risk assessment manager Neil Dalus said drought can affect vessel capacity, route availability, port operations and the wider transport network. TT Club advises operators to map inland waterway dependencies, build river-level and drought forecasts into planning, and arrange alternative road, rail and storage options before restrictions bite.

Your client's AI platform could be helping cybercriminals

Insurance Business America, quoting Travelers (insurer) · Sep 8
Lauren Winchester, head of cyber risk services at Travelers, says ransomware actors increasingly abuse legitimate tools already present in a compromised environment, noting that once inside "they sometimes live off the land." The FBI logged more than 22,000 AI-related complaints in 2025 with about $893m in reported losses; separately it recorded over $3bn in business email compromise losses and more than 3,600 ransomware complaints. Winchester advises brokers to raise AI governance and deployment controls with clients.

Barrel of oil now carries up to $8 in war risk insurance, expert saysStill relevant

Insurance Business America, quoting Lloyd's List · Sep 4 · market estimates
David Osler, law and insurance editor at Lloyd's List, estimates that war-risk insurance now accounts for roughly $7 to $8 of the cost of a barrel of crude. Marine insurers have paid about $2bn in claims across more than 70 cases since the Iran conflict began, which he places as the second-largest marine payout in over a decade behind the 2024 Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse. War-risk premiums have risen from a fraction of a percentage point to as much as 10% of a vessel's value in high-risk zones.

9/11 transformed BI disputes – what brokers should review 25 years laterStill relevant

Insurance Business America, quoting Reed Smith (law firm) · Sep 4
Reed Smith insurance recovery partners Richard Lewis and Courtney Horrigan note that business interruption accounted for roughly one third of the $32.5bn of insured losses from the 9/11 attacks in 2001 dollars, and put BI litigation at more than 1,600 non-COVID cases afterwards against about 400 before. They say contingent business interruption and leader-property extensions were frequently dropped or heavily sublimited after 9/11, and that civil authority wording can leave gaps around government closures. They advise checking whether coverage is tied to a specific location and preserving pre-loss financial records before a claim arises.

FBI investigating possible exposure of millions of American IDsInvestigation only

Insurance Journal · Sep 4 · still relevant
The FBI confirmed it is looking into a possible breach at an identity verification company that may have exposed driver's licence scans belonging to millions of Americans. A criminal service called Nexus advertised access to more than 160 million North American driver's licences on a Russian-language forum, plus data from over 10 million other documents, and disappeared shortly after the investigation was confirmed. The source of the data has not been established, and journalist Brian Krebs verified document authenticity with nine individuals whose licences were offered for sale.

London market sees treaty insurance premiums fall – IUA

CIR Magazine, citing the International Underwriting Association (trade body) · Sep 7
The International Underwriting Association reports London company market treaty reinsurance premiums fell 6% to £11.270bn in 2025 from £11.985bn, with treaty business making up 26% of London company market premiums. Property treaties accounted for £3.083bn, motor £2.393bn, marine £1.555bn and liability £1.365bn. The IUA describes a shift to more competitive pricing, with growth deliberately selective across classes and regions.

Atlantic hurricane season remains most tranquil since 1941Season to date

Insurance Journal · Sep 4 · still relevant
The Atlantic has produced five short-lived tropical storms and no hurricanes so far this season, with accumulated cyclone energy at 4.4 — the lowest for this point in a season since 1941. Forecasters put the chance of well-below-average activity over the following two weeks at 97%, and the strongest winds recorded were 50 mph. Colorado State University's Phil Klotzbach attributes the calm to dry air, stable patterns and record wind shear amplified by El Niño.
