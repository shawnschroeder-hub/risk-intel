3M Commits $7.35 Billion to Settle PFAS-Related Claims

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Nov 4 · court approval not reported
3M will pay $7.35 billion over nine years to resolve almost all of the 115,000 to 120,000 personal-injury claims centred on a federal court in South Carolina, which blame PFAS chemicals for cancers, thyroid disease and ulcerative colitis. Only $50 million is due in 2026 and nothing in 2027, with about 20% of payments falling in the final two years. 3M still faces water-contamination claims that Bloomberg Intelligence estimates could cost up to $15 billion.

AIG’s General Insurance Q3 Underwriting Income Down 29%

Insurance Journal · Nov 4 · company results
AIG’s General Insurance underwriting income fell to $564 million in the third quarter, with catastrophe losses of $283 million, up from $100 million, driven by North American severe convective storms. The combined ratio rose to 91.3 from 86.8, while net premiums written grew 7% to $6.7 billion. North America commercial underwriting income fell 57% to $167 million, and CEO Eric Anderson cited “a more challenging market environment.”

Alternative capital driving greater options for insurance buyers: Aon

Artemis, citing Aon’s Q3 2026 Global Insurance Market Insights (broker research) · Nov 4
Aon says abundant capacity and strong competition favour buyers across most major lines, with well-performing property risks in the US, UK, Latin America and the Pacific seeing double-digit price reductions. Loss-impacted, catastrophe-exposed and higher-hazard risks still face closer scrutiny and smaller relief, and buyers are turning more to captives, structured solutions and parametric products. Aon advises using better exposure data to test limits and retentions and reinvesting savings in resilience.

Binance’s $6.3bn hack began with a fake job offer, and it had no cyber cover

Insurance Business America, citing Binance’s incident report and Bloomberg Law · Nov 3 · no-cover report is from an unnamed source
Attackers spent months social-engineering Binance staff, including a fake recruiter’s coding test, before draining an estimated $6.3 billion from hot wallets on Oct 29; the FBI attributes the attack to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, which North Korea denies. Bloomberg Law, citing a person familiar with the matter, reports Binance had no cyber insurance to claim on. The article notes that Lloyd’s has required state-backed attack exclusions in standalone cyber policies since March 2023, though it is unclear whether this theft meets that threshold.

Aon expands follow-form facility to 35% for 2027

Business Insurance · Nov 4 · broker facility
Aon’s Client Treaty, a QBE-led facility backed by Lloyd’s syndicates that co-insures placements on the lead insurer’s terms and price, will take up to 35% of eligible placements from Jan 1, up from 28.5%. The 1.5% premium discount is unchanged but now applies to the larger share, where local laws permit. The facility has placed more than $5 billion of gross written premium since 2016.

Regulators, insurers urged to cooperate on litigation costs

Business Insurance · Nov 3 · APCIA annual meeting panel (regulator and former-regulator views)
At the APCIA annual meeting, Louisiana Insurance Commissioner Tim Temple said the costs of what the industry calls legal-system abuse need to be quantified, urging insurers to share more data. Former Wisconsin commissioner Mark Afable also backed data sharing and said the industry must counter plaintiff-attorney advertising. The article gives no cost figures.

High court blocks insurer’s recovery of fatality settlement

Business Insurance · Nov 3 · Virginia Supreme Court answer to a 4th Circuit question
The Virginia Supreme Court held that contractors working on a communications tower were exempt from the state’s Overhead High Voltage Line Safety Act, so they had no duty to reimburse an electric cooperative for an $800,000 settlement paid over a worker’s death. The cooperative’s insurer, Federated Rural Electric Management, had sued to recover the amount. The court did not decide whether a settlement without a liability finding could otherwise qualify for reimbursement.

Pennsylvania Public Adjuster Law Adds Cap on Fees, Bans Ties With Contractors

Claims Journal · Nov 4 · signed into law; effective date not stated
Gov. Josh Shapiro has signed House Bill 1972, capping public adjuster fees at 10% for catastrophic claims and 20% for other claims, where prior law had no cap. Adjusters may no longer hold a financial interest in repair, restoration, demolition or salvage work on the same claim, and contractor kickbacks are banned.

SafePoint secures its largest catastrophe bond ever, $650m Nature Coast Re 2026-2

Artemis · Nov 4
SafePoint’s Nature Coast Re 2026-2 cat bond priced at $650 million, up 160% from a $250 million target, giving four years of indemnity, per-occurrence US named-storm reinsurance from Dec 1. Secondary Perils Now Drive Catastrophe Losses, Squeezing Earnings and Capital

Risk & Insurance, citing Marsh Re (reinsurance broker research) · Nov 3
Marsh Re research says hail, wildfire, winter storm and inland flood now drive most North American insured catastrophe losses in most years. US insured severe convective storm losses have exceeded $30 billion every year since 2020, topping $40 billion in four of them, with about $26 billion recorded through August 2026. Reinsurers’ share of total industry catastrophe losses fell to 11% in the first nine months of 2025, from 21% to 24% in 2017, 2019 and 2021.

Firms face growing disruption from geopolitical conflict

CIR Magazine, citing Healix’s 2027 Risk Radar Report (vendor survey) · Nov 4
Healix found that 92% of surveyed global organisations operate in, or send staff to, areas of geopolitical instability, and almost two-thirds reported operational impacts from conflict over the past two years. In the past year 48% experienced travel disruption, 36% employee safety incidents and 32% evacuations or relocations. Respondents expect travel disruption (18%), cyber threats (16%), energy prices (15%) and supply-chain disruption (14%) to be the top impacts over the next 12 months.

Commercial Auto Underwriting Results Show Signs of Long-Awaited Improvement: AM Best

Risk & Insurance, citing AM Best (rating agency) · Nov 4
AM Best says US commercial auto’s net underwriting loss narrowed to $2.2 billion in 2025 from $4.9 billion, its best result since 2021, on net premiums written up 6% to $65.8 billion. Auto liability still lost more than $4.6 billion at a 108.6 combined ratio, with average loss per claim rising to $31,537 from $28,771. Q2 2026 rate increases were the lowest in more than ten years.

Older Workers Show 21% Higher Medical Utilization, Driven by Comorbidities and Diagnoses, NCCI Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing NCCI (rating and data organisation) · Nov 2 · still relevant
NCCI says workers aged 65 and over made up 8% of lost-time workers’ compensation claims in accident year 2025, double the 4% share in 2012. Their medical utilization runs about 21% above that of workers aged 16 to 64, a gap NCCI attributes mainly to diagnosed conditions and treated comorbidities. NCCI measures utilization as the intensity of medical services used, not cost.

EMEA reinsurers face lower profits through 2027: S&P

Business Insurance, citing S&P Global Ratings via Reinsurance News · Nov 4 · rating-agency outlook
S&P expects reinsurance pricing to soften further and large reinsurers’ profitability to decline in 2026 and 2027, citing ample capacity from traditional reinsurers and alternative capital. It still expects sector profits to cover the cost of capital, provided catastrophe and large man-made losses stay within budgets. The article gives no specific figures.

Insured windstorm losses in the hundreds of millions of euros: Aon

Business Insurance, citing Aon (broker estimate) · Nov 4 · event of Oct 22–25
Aon estimates insured losses in the hundreds of millions of euros from windstorms Austen and Boelo, which struck Western Europe from Oct 22 to 25. France is expected to bear the largest share, with notable losses also in the UK and Germany.

Cyberattackers Drain Money From French Bank Credit Mutuel Arkea

Claims Journal (Bloomberg) · Nov 4 · amount not disclosed
French lender Crédit Mutuel Arkéa says it was the victim of a cyberattack that enabled fraudulent transactions after hackers gained access to its systems. The bank declined to say how much was taken, has suspended some transactions and is working with authorities. It says the situation is under control and clients will not suffer a financial loss.

Premia acquires Luxembourg reinsurer

Business Insurance · Nov 4 · completed Oct 30 · terms undisclosed
Bermuda-based Premia Holdings has completed its acquisition of Ramius Enterprise Luxembourg Holdco and its subsidiaries, including Cowen Reinsurance SA, in a deal with TD Bank Group. The transaction closed Oct 30 after approval from Luxembourg’s insurance regulator, and terms were not disclosed.
