Full Senate passes measure to reauthorize federal terrorism insurance backstop

Insurance Journal · Sep 29 · Senate-passed, House reconciliation pending
The Senate passed, by unanimous consent, legislation to reauthorize the federal terrorism insurance backstop through 2034, extending it well past its current December 31, 2027 expiration. The House passed its own version in June 2026, and because the two bills differ, lawmakers must reconcile them before sending a final measure to the president. Industry groups are hoping for enactment by the end of 2026.

Chubb must pay $30 million in coal silo collapse coverage fight

Business Insurance · Sep 29
A federal jury in West Virginia ordered Chubb's Federal Insurance Company unit to pay Ramaco Resources $30 million ($27.5 million for economic loss plus additional damages) after rejecting Federal's position that a 2018 coal silo hopper collapse was caused by corrosion, which its policy excluded. Ramaco had argued a coal clog caused the failure and sued in 2019; an earlier $7.6 million award was reinstated on appeal in 2023 with a new trial ordered on further damages under West Virginia's Hayseeds doctrine. The case has now run more than seven years since the original loss.

AM Best: US home insurers in 2025 book first underwriting profit in seven years

Insurance Journal, citing AM Best · Sep 29
AM Best found US home insurers booked a $16.5 billion underwriting profit in 2025, their first gain since 2019, with the combined ratio improving to 84.5 from 90.7 in 2024, aided by $4.1 billion of favorable reserve development. AM Best attributed the turnaround to insurers better matching rate to risk, a relatively calm 2025 hurricane season, and softening reinsurance costs. Seven of the prior eight years had produced underwriting losses for the line.

Nuclear plant settles cancer, contamination lawsuits for $80 million

Insurance Journal · Sep 30 · settlement, no admission of wrongdoing
Solstice Advanced Materials agreed to an $80 million settlement over claims that its Metropolis, Illinois uranium conversion plant released radioactive contamination for decades, sickening residents and contaminating property; the company denied wrongdoing but settled to avoid prolonged litigation. The deal covers property-damage claims from the town and county, a proposed resident class action within three miles of the plant, and individual illness claims. State data cited in the litigation found brain cancer incidence in surrounding Massac County 45% above the state average and lung cancer 52% above average.

Zebra printer power supplies recalled over fire risk

CIR Magazine · Sep 29 · UK recall expansion
A UK product-safety recall has expanded to cover Zebra desktop and card printer power supplies made by FSP Group between October 2006 and December 2012, after an improperly mixed flame-retardant compound in the DC power connectors was found to risk overheating when exposed to moisture over time. The recall spans multiple printer ranges and was flagged as particularly relevant to healthcare settings where an electrical failure could disrupt operations. It expands an original 2016 recall to catch additional units from the same manufacturing window.

Where construction insurance demand is moving heading into 2027

Insurance Business America, citing QBE · Sep 29
QBE's construction chief Ryan Powers said 2027 construction insurance demand growth will be "highly selective," concentrated in data centers, public infrastructure, battery plants, semiconductor facilities and advanced manufacturing, while office, hospitality and single-family residential construction stay weak. Labor shortages, tariff-driven supply chain disruption, and litigation-funding-linked claim severity remain headwinds, compounded by a softening property market. Powers flagged AI adoption in design and site safety as a growing 2027 theme, with human oversight of AI-generated designs still essential.

Unrated reinsurers creep into $30-billion US fronting market

Insurance Business America, citing AM Best · Sep 30
AM Best warned that unrated and unauthorized reinsurers — offshore entities, unauthorized captives, or unrated carriers — are increasingly backing the roughly $30 billion US fronting market, leaving fronting carriers on the hook if that reinsurance fails to pay. The fronting segment has grown from $1.8 billion to nearly $20 billion in direct written premium since 2015, but AM Best tracks only 16 rated organizations among an estimated 30 total fronting operators. Adverse loss development over seven straight accident years suggests early loss projections in the segment have been too optimistic.
