Soft insurance market to continue through 2027: Swiss Re

Business Insurance, citing Swiss Re Institute (reinsurer’s research arm) · Nov 17 · reinsurer forecast
A Swiss Re Institute report released November 17 expects global property/casualty pricing to stay soft through 2027, citing abundant capital, strong investment returns and lower reinsurance costs. It forecasts underwriting margins falling from 4.4% of earned premiums in 2026 to 1.7% in 2027 and barely above zero in 2028, with claims costs rising, particularly for construction and medical treatment. Data centers, energy infrastructure and advanced manufacturing are named as drivers of new demand for commercial property, engineering, liability and specialty cover.

Insurance loss estimates for Microsoft outage range from $100M to $700M: reports

Insurance Journal, citing CyberCube and Acrisure (cyber modeler and broker) and Morningstar DBRS (rating agency) · Nov 16 · preliminary estimates; new figures since Monday’s edition
Estimates of global cyber insured losses from the November 12–14 Microsoft outage now range from $100 million to $700 million. CyberCube says losses are most likely below $100 million, with a conservative upper limit of $350 million, while Morningstar DBRS estimates $300 million to $500 million and Acrisure $700 million. Morningstar DBRS, quoting CyberCube, put insured losses from the 2024 CrowdStrike outage at $400 million to $1.5 billion.

Aon completes $17 billion acquisition of USI

Insurance Journal · Nov 17 · deal completed
Aon completed its $17 billion acquisition of USI Insurance Services from private equity firm KKR, a deal first announced in late August. USI, with about $3 billion in annual revenue and more than 10,500 employees, will operate as “USI, an Aon company,” and former USI CEO Mike Sicard now leads Aon’s middle-market business across Aon, NFP and USI. Aon expects about $395 million in annual run-rate net adjusted EBITDA from revenue and cost synergies.

L’Oreal US settles hair relaxer cancer cases during first trial

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · Nov 17 · settled Nov 16; terms confidential, no liability finding
L’Oreal’s US subsidiary and other companies settled, for a confidential sum, the claims of four women who alleged chemical hair straighteners caused their gynecologic cancers, in the first such cases to reach trial, in Cook County, Illinois. More than 12,700 cases are centralized before a federal judge in Chicago, with others in state court. L’Oreal says its products are safe and that a 2022 NIH study did not establish a causal link.

Swiss Re unit must pay $129M on Duke coal ash reinsurance: judge

Business Insurance · Nov 16 · federal bench-trial ruling; appeal status not reported
After a nine-day bench trial, a federal judge in the Southern District of New York found that Swiss Reinsurance America Corp. breached six 1980s reinsurance certificates and awarded Fairfax subsidiary TIG Insurance $114.3 million in damages and $15.5 million in expenses, plus interest from November 2021. The underlying excess policies covered utilities including Duke Power, whose 2016 coal ash groundwater claims settled in 2021. The judge rejected the reinsurer’s argument that its exposure was limited to credit risk and found the 57% allocation of the settlement to these policies reasonable and made in good faith.

Asbestos coverage triggered by exposure

Business Insurance · Nov 16 · Fifth Circuit appeals ruling
The Fifth Circuit held in Interstate Fire & Casualty Co. v. Murco Wall Products that excess policies issued to the former wall-products maker by Allianz subsidiary Interstate and by Canal Insurance are triggered by asbestos exposure during the policy period, not by when illness appears. It reversed the lower court on defense costs, ruling that because the policies reimburse only “ultimate net loss,” proven facts rather than lawsuit allegations must establish a covered occurrence. In one underlying suit the proven facts did not, so the insurers owed no defense-cost reimbursement there.

Brokers need to be extremely careful when changing casualty towers: Hartford E&S leader

Insurance Business America, citing Navigators/The Hartford (insurer) and the Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers (trade body) · Nov 17 · insurer executive’s views
Lynanne St. Denis, head of wholesale property and casualty at Navigators, a brand of The Hartford, says brokers moving a primary casualty policy from E&S to an admitted carrier must check that its definitions, exclusions and sublimits align with the umbrella and excess layers. She says premises and products liability and simpler construction risks are moving back to admitted carriers, while loss trends tied to legal system abuse and nuclear verdicts have not changed. Council data cited in the article show umbrella premiums rose 5.3% in the second quarter, the 35th straight quarterly increase.

Artemis (publisher’s own deal tracking) · Nov 17
Settled 2026 catastrophe bond issuance tracked by Artemis passed $20 billion as of November 17, a week ahead of 2025’s pace, and the outstanding market exceeded $66 billion for the first time, up $4.69 billion this year. NormanMax parametric Syndicate 3939 more than doubles GWP, gets $65m capacity approved for 2027

Artemis · Nov 17 · syndicate’s own reported results
NormanMax Syndicate 3939, described as the first pure parametric syndicate at Lloyd’s, wrote $53.8 million of gross premium in the nine months to September 30, against $23.8 million for all of 2025. Lloyd’s approved raising its 2027 capacity to $65 million from $50 million for 2026, and the syndicate reported a nine-month GAAP profit of $3.2 million at an 87.3% combined ratio. Its weather business spans the US, Europe and Australia, with non-catastrophe weather now a significant part of the portfolio.

Honda faces new investigation over stop-start failures

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · Nov 17 · regulator investigation, not a recall or finding
NHTSA opened an investigation into more than 1 million 2024–2026 Honda Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline and Pilot and Acura MDX vehicles after about 700 complaints that engines failed to restart after the automatic stop-start system shut them off. A 2022 probe covering 2.2 million vehicles was upgraded last year after more than 1,300 complaints and reports of four crashes and two injuries, and remains open. Honda issued 2023 service bulletins for a fix, extended warranty coverage on affected parts to 10 years with unlimited mileage, and says it will cooperate.

Flood threats grow beyond traditional high-risk zones

Business Insurance, citing the GAO (using First Street data) and NOAA · Nov 17
An October GAO report, citing First Street data, found about 12.8 million US residential and commercial properties outside FEMA high-risk zones face high flood risk, with heavy rainfall the primary risk for 62% of them. The National Flood Insurance Program caps commercial cover at $500,000 each for buildings and contents and does not cover business interruption. NOAA’s October winter outlook flagged a potentially very strong El Niño that could bring wetter-than-average conditions from California to Florida.

Top four US airlines face $345M hit from FAA ground stop, Bernstein estimates

Business Insurance, via Reuters, citing Bernstein (analyst firm) · Nov 16 · analyst estimate; cause still under investigation
Bernstein estimates American, Delta, United and Southwest face a combined $345 million pretax earnings hit from the weekend’s nationwide ground stop, within a range of roughly $270 million to $430 million. More than 18,500 US flights had been canceled as of Monday morning, based on FlightAware data. Officials suggested on Saturday that a single router installed by L3Harris may have caused the disruption.

AI misuse among staff rivals phishing as security threat

CIR, citing IO (security vendor) survey · Nov 17 · vendor survey of UK and US cybersecurity managers
In IO’s 2026 State of Information Security survey, 25% of UK and US cybersecurity managers who had an incident in the past year traced their most significant incident or near-miss to employee misuse of AI, against 24% citing phishing. The most common employee security mistake, cited by 35%, was using generative AI without permission or guidance, and 22% said staff had shared sensitive data with public AI tools.

Cargo ship strikes bridge between New Jersey and Philadelphia

Insurance Journal, via AP · Nov 16 · collision Nov 12; Coast Guard inspecting
The 591-foot Singapore-flagged cargo vessel Strategic Alliance struck the Tacony-Palmyra Bridge between Palmyra, New Jersey, and Philadelphia at about 1:15 p.m. on November 12 while the drawbridge was open, leaving it unable to close for a time. No injuries were reported, and the bridge reopened to traffic that evening with a lane closure. A US Coast Guard inspection team is on site, and the article does not describe structural damage.
