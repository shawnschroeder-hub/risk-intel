Swiss Re: AI data centers and renewables could generate $200bn in new insurance premiums by 2030

Risk & Insurance, citing Swiss Re Institute · Sep 18
Swiss Re Institute's "Time to Build" report estimates AI data-center and renewable-energy investment could generate roughly $200bn in cumulative commercial property and casualty premiums through 2030, split about $91bn from data centers and $111bn from renewables. Property insurance alone is projected to account for $49bn of the data-center premium pool. The figures represent capacity insurers are building for the capex wave, not confirmed bound business.

Sentry survey: 88% of US businesses struggling to manage geopolitical risk

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS), citing a Sentry Insurance survey · Sep 17
A Sentry Insurance survey found 88% of US businesses report difficulty accurately managing risk amid rapid geopolitical developments, and 61% of leaders said international conflicts have already hit them through supply-chain disruption or higher operating costs. 98% of executives surveyed say they are reassessing long-term planning and risk strategy in response.

Business email compromise drove ~$3bn of US cybercrime losses in 2025, dwarfing ransomware

Insurance Business America, citing an Opstream analysis of FBI data · Sep 22
An Opstream analysis of FBI data found business email compromise caused roughly $3bn in US losses in 2025 across nearly 24,800 reported cases — more than seven times the combined losses from ransomware, data breaches and IP theft. Funds-transfer fraud, not ransomware, remains the largest crime-loss category for US businesses.

Swiss Re: reinsurance demand rising as catastrophe and liability losses both grow

Beinsure, citing Swiss Re Institute and Swiss Re P&C Re executives · Sep 18
Swiss Re says insured natural-catastrophe losses are growing 5-7% annually while commercial liability losses hit $174bn in 2025, edging past that year's $120bn of global insured natural-catastrophe losses. Executives point to the projected $6tn of data-center investment through 2030 as a further demand driver, estimating a $91bn premium opportunity tied to that buildout.

214 companies, including manufacturers, press for mandatory disclosure of litigation funders

IPWatchdog · Sep 15
A coalition of 214 companies and organizations — including manufacturers, insurers and major industrial and tech firms — sent a letter September 14 urging the Advisory Committee on Civil Rules to require disclosure of nonparty litigation funders in federal civil cases. The letter argues courts and defendants currently have no way to identify funders backing a case, letting undisclosed funding influence claims severity and settlement leverage. The push follows a year in which nuclear verdicts (jury awards of $10m+) totaled 149 cases and $25.1bn nationally in 2025 per Litigation Sentinel tracking, up 52% from 2024.

Hurricane Polo puts Mexico's $175m parametric catastrophe bond trigger in focus

Artemis.bm · Sep 22
Hurricane Polo rapidly intensified into a Category 4 storm off Mexico's Pacific coast, bringing the $175m IBRD CAR Mexico 2024 parametric catastrophe bond into focus. The bond pays out 25-100% of principal only if the storm's central pressure falls to 937mb or lower while passing through a defined coastal box. Current model runs suggest Polo is likely to stay offshore at peak intensity, reducing near-term trigger risk.

Parametric insurance market forecast to more than double to $46bn by 2035

SNS Insider research, via GlobeNewswire · Sep 15
SNS Insider projects the global parametric insurance market to grow from an estimated $21.1bn in 2025 to $46.08bn by 2035, a 10.1% compound annual growth rate. Warehouse operators urged to overhaul lithium-ion battery storage after thermal-runaway risk

TT Club is urging warehouse and logistics operators to raise lithium-ion battery storage standards, calling thermal runaway the principal hazard as battery volumes rise with the energy transition. Ambiguous exclusion wording sinks insurer's bid to deny listeria outbreak coverage

Business Insurance · Sep 21
A federal judge in Oregon ruled against Scottsdale Insurance's attempt to deny excess liability coverage to Bruce Packing after a 2024 listeria contamination at its Oklahoma facility triggered a recall and bodily-injury suits. The policy's "Communicable Disease Exclusion" barred coverage for any "infectious and/or contagious disease," and the court found the ambiguous "and/or" construction had to be read against the insurer since listeria is infectious but not contagious. The ruling is a reminder that recall and contamination exclusion wording gets read narrowly against the drafter.

Surveillance video backs comp award, defeats insurer's intentional-injury defense

Business Insurance · Sep 21 (ruling Sep 17)
A New York appellate court upheld workers' compensation benefits for a retail manager injured when a dropped box struck her leg and foot. Surveillance footage corroborated her account of the accident, and the court found the insurer's defense — that she deliberately injured herself — unsupported by the video evidence.

US-Canada tariffs projected to add up to $61bn to auto claim costs, spilling into property lines

Beinsure, citing APCIA and Verisk · Aug 25 · older but still relevant
The American Property Casualty Insurance Association estimates that 50% US tariffs on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods, plus Canada's retaliatory measures, could add $30bn-$61bn to personal auto claim costs over 12 months. Verisk separately reports reconstruction costs rose 3.6% between April 2025 and April 2026, meaning the same tariff-driven material inflation is also pushing up property and manufacturing-equipment claims costs.

Generative-AI exclusions spread rapidly across contractor liability policies

Beinsure · Aug 26 · older but still relevant
Carriers have filed over 4,000 state-level generative-AI exclusions on contractor policies since mid-2025, built from six ISO standard forms that strip coverage for bodily injury, property damage or advertising injury where AI contributed to the claim — even if AI touched only a small part of the project. Unlike cyber, where standalone products emerged after exclusions hit, no admitted standalone generative-AI product yet fills the resulting coverage gap for contractors.
