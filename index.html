Commercial insurance rates keep tumbling: Marsh

Business Insurance, citing Marsh McLennan’s global index (broker) · Nov 10 · global figures; no US breakdown given
Global commercial insurance rates fell 5% in the third quarter, the ninth straight quarterly decline, according to Marsh McLennan. Property rates fell 10%, cyber 4% and financial and professional lines 3%, with India, the Middle East and Africa down 14% and the Pacific down 12%. The article attributes the decline mainly to insurers’ strong profitability, surplus capital and lower reinsurance costs.

Ivans: US Commercial Renewal Rate Increases Slowed in October

Insurance Journal, citing the Ivans Index (Applied Systems unit; agency and carrier transaction data) · Nov 10 · US data
US commercial renewal rate increases slowed in October for most lines: commercial auto 2.80% (from 3.29%), business owners policies 5.21%, general liability 3.10% and umbrella 6.08%. Commercial property was the exception, rising to 5.37% from 5.11%, while workers’ compensation fell further to −1.45%. Commercial property averaged 5.65% in the third quarter, down from 6.40% in the second quarter and 7.64% a year earlier.

Reinsurance renewal discussions shaped by abundant capital, loss concerns

Business Insurance · Nov 10 · reinsurance broker views ahead of January 1 renewals
Reinsurance brokers expect property reinsurance rates to fall again at January 1 renewals, with Howden Re saying rates fell slightly more than 15% on a risk-adjusted basis at both the January and midyear 2026 renewals. Casualty reinsurance rates are expected to stay relatively flat, and Gallagher Re said reinsurers remain worried about the US litigation environment. Brokers said 2027 talks may extend to program structure, including multi-event, aggregate and multi-year covers.

Insured catastrophe losses surpass $100bn in 2026. No longer enough to move the needle: Bowen, Gallagher Re

Artemis, citing Gallagher Re (reinsurance broker) · Nov 10 · broker estimate
Gallagher Re’s chief science officer said global insured catastrophe losses have passed $100 billion in 2026, the tenth straight year above that level on an inflation-adjusted basis and the seventh in nominal terms. Natural catastrophe insured losses for January to September were $92 billion, the lowest nine-month total since 2019 and 29% below the 10-year average. He said the threshold is now notable but no longer enough on its own to move the market.

Court upholds $19.5M judgment against Travelers in Scholastic cover fight

Business Insurance · Nov 9 · 2nd Circuit; district court judgment affirmed
The Second Circuit affirmed a judgment of nearly $19.5 million, including $5.2 million in prejudgment interest, against Travelers unit St. Paul Fire and Marine in a coverage fight with publisher Scholastic. The court rejected the insurer’s argument that the underlying software dispute with Vanderbilt University was an uncovered contract claim, holding that developing and selling educational products are core professional services. It also held that, once the AIG primary policy’s $10 million limit was paid, Travelers’ policy language obliged it to pick up covered defense costs as well as settlement costs.

Terror Suit Against Drugmakers Draws US Supreme Court Inquiry

Claims Journal (Bloomberg) · Nov 10 · allegations only; Supreme Court has not decided whether to hear the case
The US Supreme Court asked the Solicitor General for the administration’s views on an appeal by units of AstraZeneca, Roche, GE HealthCare, Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer and Viatris in a suit under the Anti-Terrorism Act. Plaintiffs allege the companies paid bribes from 2004 to 2013 for Iraqi Ministry of Health contracts that funded a militant group, and a federal appeals court let the suit proceed. The companies argue the allegations do not show a close enough link to specific attacks.

Munich Re teams with Wawanesa & WWF-Canada to launch parametric river flood solution

Artemis · Nov 10 · company announcement
Munich Re, Wawanesa and WWF-Canada launched what they describe as a first-of-its-kind parametric river flood solution in Canada, with Marsh Re providing climate-peril expertise. Wawanesa pays when independently measured river levels in the Wolastoq (Saint John River) watershed meet predetermined conditions, up to $1 million a year and $3 million over three years. The funds support post-flood assessment and restoration work before conventional aid arrives.

Global Parametrics to design Peru’s first parametric earthquake insurance solution

Artemis · Nov 9 · in development; pilot planned, no launch date
Global Parametrics, part of CelsiusPro Group, is developing Peru’s first parametric earthquake insurance product, paying livelihood support to low-income Lima households after a qualifying quake. The pilot targets 5,000 policyholders covering about 17,000 household members, scaling to up to 20,000 policyholders in its second year. It is funded by a Natural Disaster Fund technical assistance grant.

France blames Iran for fridge hacks as brokers confront cyber-physical exposures

Insurance Business America · Nov 9 · development of a story carried Nov 2 and Nov 6; government attribution
France’s foreign minister said Iran’s Revolutionary Guards were responsible for the October 11–12 attacks on refrigeration temperature controllers at about 700 retail sites, which caused substantial inventory losses and at least 15 fires, and said France would pursue European sanctions. Affected sites included major grocers, a slaughterhouse and cooperative food-processing factories, and some units reportedly ran obsolete or outdated firmware. A Lockton cyber executive advised mapping connected equipment against the consequences of losing control of it and reviewing wording and limits across cyber, property and liability.

NHTSA Opens Probe Into 1.2 Million Ford Vehicles Over Airbag Deployment

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Nov 10 · preliminary evaluation, not a recall
NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation covering more than 1.2 million 2021–2026 Ford Explorer vehicles, including Police Interceptors, over accidental deployment of seat-mounted side airbags. The agency has received eight complaints and four early warning reports alleging burn injuries, including second- and third-degree burns. A preliminary evaluation is the first stage of an investigation, and no recall has been reported.

Workers’ Compensation Claims Triggered Probes of Walmart for Child Labor Violations

Insurance Journal · Nov 10 · state settlement
Pennsylvania announced a $7.8 million settlement with Walmart under the state’s Child Labor Act, which the state called its largest child labor enforcement action. The investigation began after the state’s Bureau of Workers’ Compensation referred claims involving minors injured at Walmart stores, and ten audits at eight stores found an average of 65 violations each, including excessive hours, missed breaks and missing work permits. Walmart said it has resolved the matter and did not say whether it agrees with the findings.

Claims adjuster shortage puts workers’ comp renewals at risk

Insurance Business America, citing Glassdoor, the BLS and Sedgwick · Nov 10 · industry data
Claims adjuster job postings are down about 55% from their post-pandemic peak, and junior postings about 50% since early 2024, according to Glassdoor research. The BLS projects employment of claims adjusters, appraisers, examiners and investigators to fall 6% from 2025 to 2035, while still forecasting about 21,600 openings a year as workers leave. Sedgwick’s 2026 loss adjusting report names rapid retirement of experienced claims staff as one of eight major pressures on claims operations.

Nvidia-Backed Fireworks AI Notifies Customers of Its Second Hack in Weeks

Insurance Journal (Bloomberg) · Nov 10 · company disclosure
Fireworks AI, which runs AI models for more than 10,000 business customers, notified customers that their data was accessed after an unauthorized party breached an external cloud provider hosting its machines on October 24. It is the company’s second known incident in weeks, after an early-October breach using internal credentials exposed account and billing information and, for some accounts, secrets and datasets. The company has not said who was responsible for the latest incident.

Cowbell launches standalone AI agent liability policy for SMEs

Insurance Business America · Nov 10 · product announcement; launches January 2027
Cyber insurer Cowbell plans to launch a standalone AI liability policy for small and mid-sized businesses in January 2027, sold through wholesale brokers on a non-admitted basis. Cover is triggered by an “AI incident,” such as an AI agent exceeding its authorized scope, exposing data or producing unsafe outputs, and combines first-party losses with third-party liability from the same event. Limits, retentions and premiums were not disclosed.

Riot Risks in Europe Lead Insurers to Start Capping Coverage

Claims Journal (Bloomberg), citing Morningstar DBRS (rating agency) · Nov 10 · loss estimate may be revised
Insurers have begun capping riot coverage, raising deductibles and in some high-risk locations eliminating cover after protests in France, Germany, Belgium and Portugal, according to Morningstar DBRS. DBRS puts insured losses from the French protests below €1.2 billion, possibly to be revised higher, against about €730 million for France’s June 2023 riots. A new French state-backed fund for mandatory riot coverage in property policies is expected to take effect in January, funded by insurer contributions and a 0.8% premium increase.
