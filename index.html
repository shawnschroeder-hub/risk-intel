Senate Banking Committee advances TRIA reauthorization 24-0Committee-approved, not enacted

Insurance Journal · Sep 17
The Senate Banking Committee voted 24-0 to advance the Terrorism Risk Insurance Program Reauthorization Act (S. 4395), which would extend the federal terrorism backstop from its current December 31, 2027 expiration through 2037. The bill now goes to the full Senate floor; the House passed similar legislation in June 2026. Industry groups including APCIA, NAMIC and the Reinsurance Association of America back the extension.

Moody's survey: property reinsurance pricing likely falls another 7.5-15% in 2027

Artemis, citing Moody's Ratings · Sep 16
Moody's annual survey of reinsurance buyers, released ahead of the Monte Carlo Rendez-vous, found 86% of respondents expect property reinsurance prices to fall 7.5% to 15% in 2027, with a meaningful number expecting steeper declines. Respondents underestimated 2026's softening too: only 74% forecast declines for this year, yet double-digit price drops materialized. The survey measures reinsurer-level pricing, not primary property rates.

Falls and machinery incidents dominate OSHA's most-cited hazards

Business Insurance, citing a National Safety Council analysis · Sep 17
A National Safety Council analysis tied to OSHA's most frequently cited standards found falls caused 844 workplace deaths in 2024, including 666 falls to a lower level. Machinery struck-by and caught-in incidents added another 213 deaths in 2024 and nearly 85,000 lost-time or restricted-duty cases in 2023-2024, with powered equipment such as forklifts responsible for 84 of those deaths. The same analysis flagged 65,300 respiratory-illness cases and over 95,000 eye and face injuries tied to the same standards.

California comp medical payments jump after pandemic-era slowdown

Business Insurance, citing the California Workers' Compensation Institute · Sep 17
A CWCI study found medical payments on 2024 workers' comp claims rose 15.1% at six months and 9.4% at twelve months versus the prior accident year, reversing a pandemic-period slowdown. Preliminary data for 2025 claims shows a 6.1% decline in six-month medical payments. Construction claims ran highest of any industry studied, averaging $49,947 at 24 months, about 57% above the statewide average of $31,892.

Power sector build-out raises the resilience bar, insurers say

CIR Magazine, citing Willis Natural Resources · Sep 15
Electrification, AI data-center demand and advanced manufacturing expansion are pushing power companies to raise resilience standards, according to Willis Natural Resources' global head of power and renewable energy, Rob Hale. Underwriters are giving heightened scrutiny to wildfire exposure, grid resilience, supply-chain vulnerability and failure-to-supply risk even as market capacity is currently abundant and rates are falling. Hale frames the soft market as a window to renegotiate program structure rather than just cut cost.

RAND: most insurers stay silent on AI losses, and that is the real coverage gap

Insurance Business America, citing a RAND Corporation report · Sep 18
A RAND Corporation report, "The Insurability of Artificial Intelligence," found that while some carriers explicitly cover or exclude AI losses, most policies simply stay silent, leaving coverage determined only after a claim is filed. A single AI incident, such as a chatbot giving bad financial advice, can plausibly be characterized as a professional error, a cyber incident, a product defect or a general-liability claim, each triggering a different policy. RAND counts AI-related exposure touching at least 11 insurance lines and notes carriers including Berkley and Verisk have begun filing broad AI exclusions in specialty lines.

Casualty sidecar capital keeps growing; Ledger flags a new discipline problem

Artemis, citing Ledger Investing · Sep 18
Casualty sidecar capital reached roughly $1.7bn as of September 30, 2025, while life and annuity sidecar reserves grew from about $55bn in 2023 to more than $90bn in 2025. Ledger Investing says newer "asset-driven" sidecars add investment-portfolio returns as a second income source alongside underwriting, and both sides need the same discipline. Ledger recommends transactions explain upfront how underwriting performance, asset valuations, collateral and capital-replacement cycles interact.

Bermuda regulator to judge new parametric insurer license by substance, not contract formFramework proposed

Artemis, citing the Bermuda Monetary Authority · Sep 17
The BMA said its new Parametric Special Purpose Insurer (PSPI) regime will prioritize the substance of a risk transfer over its contractual form, allowing structures set up as reinsurance, derivatives or swaps to qualify if they meet statutory requirements. Insurers already writing parametric business under other licenses can continue without relicensing. The BMA plans to amend Bermuda's Insurance Act 1978 in Q4 2026, with full guidance published at that time.

Hackers hide malware instructions on blockchains; cases up 440%

Insurance Journal, citing Chainalysis · Sep 18
Chainalysis found blockchain-based malware "dead drops," hidden instructions such as the location of a command server, rose 440% in under a year, averaging 11 cases a day versus about 2 a day before mid-2025. The firm links the spike to unrestricted, open-source Chinese AI models capable of generating malicious code, and says state-backed groups tied to North Korea and Iran now account for most of the activity. Separately, TRM Labs found overall crypto hacks rose about 150% to 207 incidents in the first half of 2026.
