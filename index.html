Casualty loss costs outpace pricing as commercial market splinters

Insurance Business America, citing Lockton's Q3 2026 market update (broker) · Oct 2 · broker data
Lockton reports U.S. casualty loss costs rising 6% to 7% a year for primary coverage and 9.5% to 12% for excess, outpacing rate increases. In Q2, property rates fell 10.5%, while excess casualty rose 8.2%, lead umbrella 6.2% and general liability 1.8%. Employment practices liability median rates rose 29% in Q2, the sharpest hardening of any line.

US states challenge Corteva spinoff as bid to evade PFAS liabilities

Insurance Journal (Reuters) · Oct 2 · lawsuit filed; allegations, not findings
Fifteen states and Guam sued Corteva in Indiana state court, alleging the spinoff of its seed business into Vylor was designed to move valuable assets away from billions in potential PFAS liabilities. The spinoff was completed Thursday, a day after a South Carolina federal judge rejected California's emergency request to block it. Corteva, formed from the Dow–DuPont merger, faces thousands of lawsuits over DuPont's PFAS production.

US P/C insurers see fewer ratings downgrades in H1 2026 on improved performance

Insurance Journal, citing AM Best (rating agency) · Oct 2
AM Best says downgrades fell to 3.8% of U.S. property/casualty rating actions in the first half of 2026, from 6.1% a year earlier, while upgrades rose to 8.2% from 5.5%. It credits rate increases, underwriting discipline and a relatively benign claims year. As of June 30, 82.3% of commercial-lines writers carried stable outlooks.

Motor vehicle tort filings hit 30-year high as lawsuits outpace accidents

Insurance Business America, citing a Triple-I and Casualty Actuarial Society white paper (industry bodies) · Oct 2
Federal motor vehicle tort filings reached 6,809 in 2024, the highest since 1995, growing 5% a year from 2014 to 2024 while overall federal civil cases fell. The average plaintiff award rose from $377,000 in 1995–2004 to $681,000 in 2015–2024. The paper estimates $43.84 billion of excess litigation value across federal and state courts over 2015–2024, and commercial auto posted a $4.9 billion underwriting loss in 2024.

Zurich unit freed from covering NFL concussion settlement

Business Insurance · Oct 2 · NY appellate ruling
New York's Appellate Division, First Department unanimously held that Zurich unit American Guarantee and Liability has no duty to reimburse the NFL under its 2001–02 excess policy toward the roughly $1 billion concussion settlement. The panel found each player's injury was at least one separate occurrence, so no single claim reached the $51 million needed to trigger the excess layer. The ruling was less favorable for primary insurer TIG, a Fairfax unit.

Insurer prevails in coverage dispute over carpenter's death

Business Insurance · Oct 2 · Illinois appellate ruling
The Illinois Appellate Court ruled Mt. Hawley Insurance has no duty to indemnify Stone Development Group for a $2.65 million agreed judgment over a carpenter killed in a 2017 fall at a Waukegan hotel project. The court found the worker was an employee, so the policy's employee injury exclusion applied. Stone Development had no workers' comp coverage at the time of the accident.

Why private companies may be underestimating D&O risk

Insurance Business America, citing Travelers (insurer view) · Oct 2
Travelers says private-company D&O claims come mainly from creditor actions, regulatory investigations and investor suits, plus disputes with customers, competitors and suppliers. It points to U.S. business bankruptcy filings rising 7.1% to 24,737 in 2025 as a driver of insolvency-related claims. Smaller private companies are described as the most underinsured segment.

Lyft to pay $272.5 million to settle California misclassification claims

Business Insurance · Oct 1 · settlement announced
Lyft agreed to pay $272.5 million plus interest to settle California claims that it misclassified drivers as independent contractors from 2016 to 2020, denying them minimum wage, overtime, sick leave and workers' comp coverage. The state Labor Commissioner's Office called it the largest wage-and-hour settlement in California history, with 87% of the funds going to drivers. The settlement does not require Lyft to reclassify drivers going forward.

Almost 75% of companies say AI-related job cuts cost more than they saved

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS), citing a Careerminds survey (vendor) · Oct 1 · survey of HR leaders
In a Careerminds survey, nearly 75% of HR leaders said AI-driven job cuts ultimately cost more than they saved, and 54.6% said AI systems needed far more human oversight than expected. A third of organizations rehired more than half of the roles they had cut. 28.1% said remaining staff lacked the expertise to fill the gaps.
