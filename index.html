Small, medium enterprises lead cyber claimsClaim counts, not rates

Business Insurance, citing NetDiligence (cyber-claims data firm) · Sep 16
NetDiligence's study of 10,309 cyber claims from incidents in 2021–2025 found companies under $2bn of revenue made 97% of claims, totalling $2.5bn. Companies with $2bn or more made 3% of claims but accounted for $3.2bn, or 56% of costs, and 28% of their claims exceeded $1m. Ransomware caused 67% of losses and business email compromise 9%.

Lloyd's market posts third straight year of 20%-plus returns as softening cycle looms

Risk & Insurance, citing AM Best (rating agency) · Sep 16
AM Best's review of the Lloyd's market reports a 22.0% return on capital and £10.6bn ($14.4bn) of pre-tax profit in 2025, the third straight year above 20%. The ten largest syndicates wrote 37% of gross written premium. AM Best warns that softening conditions and pricing pressure across many lines are likely to push the attritional loss ratio higher in coming years.

What Beazley's AI cyber endorsement does and doesn't coverSubject to policy terms

Insurance Business America · Sep 17
Beazley has introduced an AI Clarifying Endorsement confirming that an attacker's use of AI tools, such as autonomous phishing or accelerated intrusion, does not by itself take an attack outside its existing cyber cover. The endorsement does not address whether a policyholder's own use of AI — chatbots, internal models or third-party AI vendors — is covered. A Beazley spokesperson said it has not reduced coverage for AI cyber risk, after earlier reports that it was drafting language to cap AI-related payouts.

US tracking cyber threats against nearly 20 ships worldwideOfficials unnamed

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · Sep 17
The Coast Guard, FBI and Department of Homeland Security are tracking about 20 commercial vessels facing cyber threats, and the Coast Guard has asked for advance notice if they plan to enter US ports. The Coast Guard boarded two foreign-flagged vessels in the Gulf of Mexico on 21 and 24 August after detecting compromised networks, reporting no operational disruption, danger to crews or environmental impact. A CISA official said hackers did not appear to have gained control of the ships themselves.

BMA to prioritise substance of parametric risk transfer over contractual form for new PSPINot yet final

Artemis · Sep 17
The Bermuda Monetary Authority has published its response to January's consultation on a proposed Parametric Special Purpose Insurer class, saying it will prioritise the substance of risk transfer over contractual form. Under the proposal, fully collateralised contracts could be structured as reinsurance, derivatives or swaps if they meet statutory requirements, and existing parametric business can continue under current licences. An amendment to the Insurance Act 1978 is to be drafted, and guidance published, in Q4 2026.

Fall protection tops OSHA violations as citations declinePreliminary figures

Business Insurance, citing National Safety Council · Sep 15
Fall protection was OSHA's most frequently cited standard for a 16th consecutive year in preliminary fiscal 2026 figures, with 4,041 citations, down 31.7%. Hazard communication (1,961), lockout/tagout (1,863), scaffolding (1,725) and ladders (1,659) followed. Citations across the top ten fell 24.8% to 17,701 from 23,537 in fiscal 2025.

Misclassified comp risks bring premium surprises, distort loss analysisClass codes

Business Insurance · Sep 16
Among warehouse-classification workers' compensation policies inspected in 2025, 56% required a change of governing classification, and 78% of those moved to retail or wholesale codes. NCCI says a wrong classification can leave premium out of line with actual risk, and an Aon casualty leader said corrections can bring significant premium adjustments and affect experience modification ratings used for contract eligibility. The article attributes the problem to classifying by a facility's appearance rather than its actual operations.
