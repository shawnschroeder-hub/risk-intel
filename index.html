ISO's generative AI exclusion is already on thousands of CGL policiesFiling records

Insurance Business America, citing state filings and a Reed Smith white paper · Sep 16
ISO's generative-AI exclusion endorsements, led by form CG 40 47 with a January 2026 edition date, exclude general liability claims for bodily injury, property damage and personal and advertising injury arising from generative AI. The report's review of state filings found 2,369 generative-AI exclusion records across 49 states by mid-2026, and says W.R. Berkley, Chubb, Travelers, Berkshire Hathaway and AIG were adopting the forms by April. It found no admitted standalone product designed to fill the gap.

Property reinsurance prices most likely to fall 7.5% to 15% in 2027: Moody's surveySurvey, not realised prices

Artemis, citing Moody's Ratings (rating-agency survey) · Sep 16
In Moody's Ratings' survey, the most common expectation for property reinsurance pricing at the 1 January 2027 renewals was a decline of 7.5% to 15%, with 86% of respondents anticipating decreases. Moody's says reinsurers can still earn attractive risk-adjusted returns at lower prices, which is sustaining competition. It cautions that expectations would likely shift if a major catastrophe occurs before the January renewals.

Cat 5 Florida hurricanes could drive $300bn plus insurance market loss: Swiss ReModelled scenario

Artemis, citing Swiss Re Institute (reinsurer research) · Sep 16
Swiss Re Institute estimates a Category 5 hurricane striking Miami or Tampa Bay could cause insured market losses of more than $300bn at today's exposures. A repeat of the 1926 Great Miami Hurricane would exceed $200bn, and a repeat of 1992's Hurricane Andrew would approach $100bn. Swiss Re attributes the scale to population growth and asset accumulation in exposed areas.

Insurance companies join letter to push TPLF disclosure in federal courtsNo nationwide rule yet

Insurance Journal · Sep 15
Allstate, AIG, Chubb, State Farm, Liberty Mutual, Nationwide, Travelers, AXA XL, FM and USAA were among roughly 30 insurers in the 200-plus companies asking the Advisory Committee on Civil Rules for a federal litigation-funding disclosure rule, alongside technology firms and retailers. The committee meets on 21 October. The report cites consulting firm EY estimating that third-party litigation funding could cost insurers up to $50bn over five years.

Electrolux claims insurer froze it out of its own policy after house fireComplaint, no ruling

Insurance Business America · Sep 16
Electrolux Consumer Products sued AmGUARD Insurance in the Middle District of Florida on 14 September, alleging the carrier breached duties owed to it as an additional insured on a servicer's general liability policy after a house fire involving a Frigidaire freezer allegedly caused more than $1.3m of homeowner losses. Electrolux alleges AmGUARD refused independent counsel despite a conflict from joint representation with the servicer and withheld policy limits at mediation, leaving Electrolux to fund its own settlement. It seeks $273,764 in defence fees plus damages for breach of contract and bad faith.

Abbott to pay $385 million to end claims over infant formulaNo admission of liability

Insurance Journal · Sep 16
Abbott will pay $385m — $348.7m federal and $36.3m to states — to resolve False Claims Act allegations that government programmes bought powdered infant formula made in violation of safety standards, centred on its Sturgis, Michigan plant, where FDA inspectors found unsanitary conditions in 2022. Three employee whistleblowers who brought the case will share $69m, and Abbott says the settlement is not an admission of fault or liability. Abbott separately agreed in August to pay $670m over lawsuits alleging it concealed bowel-disease risks from preterm infant formula.
