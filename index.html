Nine-month insured nat cat losses least since 2019

Business Insurance, citing a preliminary Aon report (broker data) · Oct 29
Aon estimates global insured natural catastrophe losses for the nine months to Sept. 30 at at least $91 billion, the lowest nine-month total since 2019 and well below the five-year average of $135 billion. Severe convective storm drove $55 billion of the total, and the US accounted for $56 billion. With fourth-quarter losses averaging $16 billion, Aon says the full-year total will likely exceed $100 billion.

Rate increases moderate in Q3: Ivans

Business Insurance, citing Ivans (an Applied Systems unit) premium renewal index · Ivans report dated Oct 28 · US small-commercial renewal data
Ivans’ third-quarter index shows commercial property renewal rates up 5.65%, against 6.40% in the second quarter. General liability rose 4.12% (from 5.44%), umbrella 6.88% (from 7.96%) and commercial auto 3.63% (from 4.93%). Workers’ compensation rates fell 1.17%, after a 1.37% decline in the second quarter.

Arch profits fall as Iran war adds to catastrophe losses

Business Insurance, citing Arch Capital’s Q3 results · Oct 28
Arch Capital Group’s third-quarter net income fell 23.5% to $1.03 billion, and net premiums written fell 3% to $3.85 billion. Pretax catastrophe losses rose to $234 million from $72 million a year earlier. Its combined ratio worsened to 82.5% from 79.8%.

Cyber insurers face potential PHP loss wave: A.M. Best

Business Insurance, citing A.M. Best via Reinsurance News (rating agency view) · Oct 29 · development of the PHP flaw story carried Oct 22 and Oct 26
A.M. Best says the PHP security flaws could produce broad cyber insurance losses that surface gradually, partly because attackers may delay exploiting known bugs and disputes may stretch out claims. It adds that directors and officers coverage could also be hit if companies face regulatory scrutiny or lawsuits over disclosure and patching. The report gives no loss estimate.

Trump Administration Sued Over Rollback of Corporate Ownership Reporting Rules

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · Oct 29 · lawsuit filed; no ruling
Twenty-one states and the District of Columbia sued the Treasury Department and FinCEN in federal court in Oregon over an August 2026 final rule removing beneficial ownership reporting duties for US companies under the Corporate Transparency Act. The states say the rule exempts more than 99.9% of companies originally required to disclose their owners. They argue the executive branch cannot repeal a law by rulemaking.

Parametric insurance fills gaps in builder’s risk coverage: panel

Business Insurance, reporting from IRMI’s Construction Risk Conference · Oct 28 · adviser panel views
Three advisers, none representing parametric insurers, said parametric policies can pay when weather halts work or makes it unsafe, such as extreme heat or cold, high winds or precipitation. Traditional builder’s risk does not respond to those delays, and parametric payouts can be used for any purpose. One panellist said contractors are currently “absorbing” weather losses.

Captive owners find new ways to finance risks, manage losses

Business Insurance, reporting from the Vermont Captive Insurance Association’s Chicago summit · Oct 29
Captive owners described using their captives for loss prevention, capital management and services to business partners, and some are converting single-parent captives into sponsored captives with segregated cells. Kite Realty Group uses captive incident data, including losses that never become claims, to justify maintenance and security spending. RSM US matches its captive’s investments to professional liability claims that can take 20 years or more to resolve.

Property insurance is getting cheaper. Why are some buyers retaining more risk?

Insurance Business America, citing FM and Vermont regulator data · Oct 29
Vermont, the largest US captive domicile, licensed 51 new captives in 2025, up from 41 in 2024, and ended the year with 707 licensed captives. FM chief underwriting officer Laurel Rudnick said many alternative risk arrangements run on a multi-year basis rather than as short-term fixes.

Cat bond terms may loosen next year, but discipline won’t break: Perrot, Marsh Securities

Artemis, citing an AM Best ILS webinar · Oct 29 · market participant views
Marsh Securities’ Quentin Perrot expects catastrophe bond terms to soften in 2027 while structures stay conservative, with high deductibles and modelled perils. Leadenhall’s Bart Thomas expects rate cuts in the low double digits. Pricing multiples have fallen from roughly 5 times expected loss in 2023 to about 2.5 times earlier this year.

California Earthquake Authority seeks $600m reinsurance with Ursa Re 2026-1 cat bond

Artemis · Oct 29 · deal in marketing; terms are price guidance
The California Earthquake Authority is seeking $600 million of fully collateralised earthquake reinsurance, its second cat bond this year after a $425 million deal in June. A $350 million tranche attaches at $4.784 billion of losses with price guidance of 4% to 4.5%. A $250 million tranche attaches at $1.5 billion with guidance of 8.5% to 9.5%.

Workers Say A.I. Helps Write Safety Documents, But Few Know Who’s Accountable for Errors

Risk & Insurance, citing a Tradesafe survey (safety equipment vendor) · Oct 28 · date from the article’s own page
Tradesafe surveyed 514 US workers in manufacturing, construction, warehousing, logistics and the skilled trades. Among those who knew how safety paperwork is produced, 51% said their employer uses generative AI for it. Only 18% pointed to a formal policy assigning responsibility for AI errors.

Medical inflation edges up in comp, with further hikes expected: NCCI

Business Insurance, citing NCCI (rating bureau data) · Oct 28
NCCI’s workers’ comp weighted medical price index rose 1.2% year over year in September, up from 1% in July and 1.1% in August. NCCI expects annual medical price growth of 2% to 2.5% over the next several quarters. Hospital inpatient prices rose 4.1% while drug prices fell 3.4%.

Energy Insurance Market Splits as Property Softens and Casualty Pricing Flattens

Risk & Insurance, citing Amwins’ energy state of the market report (broker report) · Oct 29 · date from the article’s own page
Amwins reports that energy property pricing is softening while casualty pricing has flattened. Downstream energy property insurers faced about $6.8 billion in gross losses in 2025 against roughly $3.25 billion to $3.5 billion in premium.

Trucking Companies Confront Mounting Diesel, Insurance, Debt Costs

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg · Oct 28
Bloomberg counts 221 US trucking companies that have filed for bankruptcy so far this year. About two-thirds of small carriers report flat or lower revenue than a year earlier, per a Truckstop and Bloomberg Intelligence survey. Highway’s Freight Fraud Index estimates freight fraud costs the industry $18 million a day.

Protecting Trade Secrets from Generative AI-Fueled Risks

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS), by attorneys at Parker Poe · Oct 29 · law firm commentary
The authors argue that employee use of public or unapproved generative AI tools can undermine a company’s claim to have taken “reasonable measures” to protect trade secrets. They cite a January 2026 Northern District of California decision that dismissed a pro se trade secret claim because the ideas were developed through ChatGPT. They recommend written AI-use policies, approved enterprise tools, training and documented controls.

Cyber crisis exercises expose gaps in resilience

CIR Magazine, citing Databarracks’ Anatomy of a Crisis wargames (vendor research) · Oct 29
Databarracks analysed 2,117 scored responses from 303 participants at 147 organisations in cyber crisis wargames. While 66% of containment responses recommended isolating affected systems, only 7% explained how to prioritise shutdown versus keeping services running. Just 20% mentioned preserving forensic evidence before wiping or rebuilding systems.
