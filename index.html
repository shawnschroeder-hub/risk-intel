Hurricane Isaias insurance industry loss estimated in $2bn – $4bn range: Euler ILS Partners

Artemis, citing Euler ILS Partners, Cotality and AccuWeather · Oct 13 · initial estimates; update to Monday's item
Swiss ILS manager Euler ILS Partners gave an initial insured industry loss estimate of $2 billion to $4 billion for Hurricane Isaias, which made landfall near Destin, Florida, as a Category 2 storm with 105 mph winds. Cotality separately estimates insured wind and storm-surge losses of $2 billion to $3 billion, mostly in the Florida Panhandle and southern Alabama. Euler says early estimates may be revised significantly, and AccuWeather's $16 billion to $20 billion figure is total damage and economic loss, not insured loss.

Hurricane Isaias unlikely to affect US reinsurer ratings – Fitch Ratings

Beinsure, citing Fitch Ratings (rating-agency view) · Oct 13
Fitch estimates Isaias could generate insured losses in the mid-single-digit billions and does not expect them to affect the ratings of major U.S. property and casualty insurers and reinsurers, with the impact falling mainly on 2026 profitability. It does not expect Isaias to exhaust catastrophe reinsurance for most Florida specialist insurers, but flags smaller, unrated and newer carriers as potential vulnerabilities.

Commercial insurance pricing set to ease further

Business Insurance, citing broker and carrier executives at the CIAB Insurance Leadership Forum · Oct 13 · executive views; forum held before Isaias landfall
EPIC's Marc Kunney said property rate reductions of about 20% are still being achieved on relatively straightforward risks, with decreases of 5% or more on harder classes, and Aon's Ryan Barber expects property reductions to likely continue through 2027. Primary general liability rates are rising by low single digits, while Willis's Pat Donnelly said North American excess liability rates continue to rise by double digits. Coalition's Shawn Ram said U.S. cyber rate decreases have moderated to the mid-single digits this year.

Cyber's soft market is masking a coverage squeeze

Insurance Business America, citing Marsh (broker index), Amwins (wholesale broker) and Verizon · Oct 13 · broker views
Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index shows U.S. cyber rates fell 2% in Q2 2026, and global cyber pricing fell 4%, a twelfth straight quarterly decline. Amwins' Megan North says some insurers are adding widespread-event exclusions and restricting or sublimiting dependent business interruption cover, and are limiting capacity on risks they flag rather than raising prices. Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report found third parties involved in 48% of breaches.

$4.75M compounding judgment falls through every coverage safety net

Insurance Business America · Oct 13 (ruling Oct 9) · Alabama Supreme Court; opinion subject to formal revision
The Supreme Court of Alabama affirmed summary judgments leaving a Tuscaloosa compounding pharmacy with no coverage for a $4.75 million settlement after a technician measured belladonna in grams instead of milligrams, poisoning a customer. The court held Nationwide's general liability professional-services exclusion applied to every claim, including product-liability theories, because all arose from the compounding act. It also barred the pharmacy's negligent-procurement claim against its agency under Alabama's duty-to-read doctrine, because the pharmacy had not read a replacement policy that lacked an exception in its prior Zurich policy.

Six Flags faces mounting liability pressure after Georgia death

Insurance Business America · Oct 13 · police investigation; cause unconfirmed
One person died and several were injured on the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia on Oct. 12, and Cobb County Police are investigating. Six Flags temporarily closed similar rides at its other parks as a precaution. The company already faces suits alleging more than 100 brain injuries linked to the now-retired X2 ride at Six Flags Magic Mountain in California.

Liberty Mutual Re makes sensor-triggered parametric earthquake payout in Mexico

Artemis · Oct 13 · payout amount not disclosed
Liberty Mutual Re's ShakeNet Parametric cover for the Mexican federal government's earthquake and volcanic risk program was triggered by the magnitude 7.3 quake that struck Mexico's southern coast on July 17, within weeks of the policy's inception. The trigger relies on Safehub's sensor network, which recorded shaking within 80 km of the epicenter, together with the Global Earthquake Model's OpenQuake engine.

Evolving captives becoming strategic platforms for attracting third-party capital: Aon

Artemis, citing an Aon article (broker view) · Oct 13
Aon says captives are moving beyond risk retention to become platforms for accessing capital, with some owners partnering with outside investors to support larger retentions. It links the shift to insurers deploying capital more selectively, sometimes cutting line sizes, while natural catastrophe, cyber and litigation exposures grow.

Amwins offers one program for battery storage and EV charging portfolios

Insurance Business America · Oct 13 · product launch; capacity not disclosed
Amwins Global Risks launched a commercial and industrial facility that groups distributed battery energy storage and EV charging assets under one program, with deductibles set site by site. It covers construction and operational phases, including delay in start-up and business interruption, with the EV charging piece aimed mainly at the U.S.

Hackers overheat fridges at hundreds of French stores

Business Insurance, citing Reuters · Oct 13 · under investigation; no claim of responsibility
Hackers overnight raised refrigerator and freezer temperatures as high as 122°F at hundreds of French supermarkets, causing food losses and fires. The units run Danfoss software that lets stores manage refrigeration remotely, and Danfoss says the affected units were on outdated firmware despite long-standing advice to install updates fixing flaws that let attackers bypass authentication.

Contractor Faces $255K Fine for 20-Year-Old Worker's Death in Trench Collapse

Insurance Journal · Oct 13 · proposed penalties, not final
OSHA proposed $254,884 in penalties against Pennsylvania contractor J.D. Wagoner Excavating after a 20-year-old worker died when the wall of a roughly 12-foot-deep trench collapsed during storm-sewer work. The citations include one willful violation for failing to protect workers from cave-ins and two repeat violations, including not assigning a competent person to inspect the trench daily. The company has 15 business days to contest.

Younger and Older Workers Drive Shift in Workers' Comp Claims Mix, WCRI Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing the Workers Compensation Research Institute · Oct 12
A WCRI flash report covering 31 states found workers aged 20 to 24 and 65 and older made up 17% of work injuries in 2024, up from 15% in 2019, while the 45-to-64 share fell from 38.2% to 35.5%. Manufacturing is one of five industries that together account for nearly 75% of work injuries, and 54% of injuries in 2022–2024 occurred in workers' first two years on the job.

AI tops AXA's risk ranking, and clients want to know if their cyber policy covers it

Insurance Business America, citing AXA's Future Risks Report (insurer survey) · Oct 13
AXA's Future Risks Report, based on 4,345 experts in 62 countries surveyed by Ipsos, ranks AI and big data as the top risk, ahead of climate change for only the second time in the survey's 13 years. Of experts who named cyber a top risk, 90% called it rapidly emerging or already entrenched, with cyberwarfare against critical infrastructure the leading concern. Energy risks rose to fifth place, reversing three years of decline.

Trump's Russian diesel deal creates sanctions minefield for marine insurers

Insurance Business America, citing OFAC, a sanctions lawyer and the Associated Press · Oct 13 · U.S. license issued; legality questioned in Congress
OFAC issued General License 135 on Oct. 9, authorizing specified transactions in Russian-origin diesel through April 7, 2027, after President Trump agreed with Vladimir Putin to allow Russian diesel shipments. UK and EU restrictions on maritime transport and insurance for Russian petroleum products still apply, so a placement touching those markets needs a license from each, according to sanctions lawyer Mahmoud Abuwasel. Lawmakers from both parties have questioned whether the arrangement fits a 2022 law banning Russian energy imports, the Associated Press reports.

Shipping and insurance costs for energy flows surge

Business Insurance, citing Reuters and an OPEC statement · Oct 13 · producer-group statement
Ministers of the OPEC+ Declaration of Cooperation said shipping and insurance costs for crude and petroleum products have risen to unprecedented levels, exceeding one-third of the commodity's value. Meeting in Riyadh, they said the costs are impeding oil flows and adding to inflationary pressure, and stressed protecting international maritime routes.

Drone attacks damage cloud services data centers

Business Insurance, citing BleepingComputer · Oct 13
Drone attacks damaged all three data centers operated by Russian cloud provider Yandex Cloud, which shut down two and partially disabled the third. It is unclear whether any data was irreversibly lost.
