Aon raises $13.5 billion from bond sale to fund USI takeoverAgreed, not completed

Insurance Journal · Sep 15
Aon sold $13.5bn of high-grade bonds across seven tranches maturing in three to 30 years, drawing roughly $65bn in orders, to fund its all-cash $17bn purchase of USI Insurance Services from KKR. The purchase is expected to close by year-end 2026, and all notes except the 30-year tranche must be redeemed at 101 cents if it has not completed by 1 December 2027. Fitch placed Aon's ratings on Rating Watch Negative, saying the acquisition will "materially increase credit risk for at least the next two years."

Baldwin Group to go private after $7.7 billion investment dealCloses Q1 2027

Insurance Journal · Sep 14
Sequence Holdings and DFO Management, which manages investments for Dell founder Michael Dell, agreed to buy a majority interest in brokerage Baldwin Group for $7.7bn, comprising about $4.6bn of equity and $3.1bn of net debt assumed or refinanced. Public shareholders receive $32.50 per share in cash. Eligible employees roll equity into a significant minority stake in the new parent, of which Baldwin will be a wholly owned subsidiary.

Appeals court throws a wrench into part of Georgia's 2025 tort-reform lawAppeal possible

Insurance Journal · Sep 15
The Georgia Court of Appeals held that evidence about the extent of a plaintiff's injuries may be presented during the fault phase of a trifurcated trial, reasoning that proving fault requires proving both injuries and proximate causation. Defence attorneys quoted in the report argue this cuts against the 2025 tort-reform law's intent by allowing injury testimony to reach the liability determination. The decision stands pending any appeal to the Georgia Supreme Court.

AI data centre boom is concentrating risks brokers haven't fully mappedBroker research

Insurance Business America, citing Howden (broker research) · Sep 15
Howden reports that 20 US locations account for roughly 80% of data centre floor space hit by severe tornadoes and hailstorms over the past decade, and that 64% of US capacity under construction in 2026 sits outside traditional hubs in more weather-exposed interior markets. Major data centre lawsuits and arbitrations rose from four in all of 2021 to 14 in the first half of 2026, with cooling-system noise disputes accounting for seven of the 14. Howden also finds data centre infrastructure within 10–15km of active conflict zones grew more in 2025 than in the previous five years combined.

214 companies urge a federal rule requiring disclosure of litigation-funding agreementsNo federal rule yet

Unified Patents · Sep 14 · advocacy letter
A letter signed by 214 companies across manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, energy and other sectors asks the Advisory Committee on Civil Rules to propose a uniform amendment requiring disclosure of third-party litigation funding agreements and the identity of any non-party funder in federal civil cases. The Advisory Committee is scheduled to discuss next steps on 21 October, and any amendment would then run the full federal rulemaking process. No uniform federal rule exists today, although some federal district courts and nearly a dozen states impose narrower disclosure requirements.

Casualty sidecar capital surpasses $2bn as specialised investor base drives expansionRating-agency research

Artemis, citing AM Best (rating agency research) · Sep 15
AM Best reports more than $2bn of disclosed casualty sidecar capital since 2024, and separately estimates the broader property and casualty sidecar market at $17–19bn. It attributes investor appetite to casualty's long-duration liabilities and the investment income earned on supporting assets at current rates. AM Best also cautions that those assets are commonly credit instruments, so reserve deterioration and pressure on credit-sensitive collateral can arrive together.

CCRIF lifts parametric risk pool coverage another 14.5% to $1.8bn for current policy year

Artemis · Sep 15
The Caribbean parametric risk pool CCRIF has raised coverage for the current policy year to $1.8bn, up 14.5% from the $1.57bn reported in June and 25% above last year's $1.44bn. Membership reached 39 with four new joiners, all utilities, including Jamaica Public Service Company and the National Water Commission of Jamaica. CCRIF says it has made 82 payouts totalling $483m since 2007, each within 14 days of a qualifying event.

US employer healthcare costs set to rise 9.5% in 2027Aug 26 · older but critical

Beinsure, citing Aon (broker research) · Aug 26 · projection, before cost-management measures
Aon's Health Value Initiative database, covering more than 1,100 US employers, 7.9 million employees and $135bn of 2026 health spending, projects a 9.5% cost increase in 2027, taking average total plan cost per employee above $19,000. Employer spending per employee rose 8.8% in 2026 to $14,432, and total plan cost rose 8.3% to $17,562, so the projected figure is the total rather than the employer-funded share. Aon notes the projection is before plan-design and other cost-management measures that typically offset part of the increase.
