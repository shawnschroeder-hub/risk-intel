Global disaster losses dip below average as Venezuela quake and heatwaves signal rising risk

Risk & Insurance, citing Munich Re · report dated Jul 30 · still relevant
Munich Re puts H1 2026 natural catastrophe losses at $112bn economic and $44bn insured — a 60% protection gap. The June 24 Venezuela earthquake was the most destructive single event at roughly $30bn total but under $1bn insured, and Munich Re's chief climate scientist warns of a "dangerous mix" as warming meets a developing Super El Niño in the second half. Why it matters: a below-average half-year is why capacity stays plentiful — but the 60% gap and the El Niño warning are the reasons not to read it as permanent.

Florida appeals court upholds $103M verdict against Ford, affirming the product-defect presumption

Insurance Journal · Aug 4 · original jury verdict 2024
Florida's Fifth District Court of Appeal affirmed a $103m award over a 2016 Mustang, supercharged by Roush Performance, that exploded in 2017 with the owner inside; the exact cause was never determined. The court upheld the Cassisi doctrine — that a product malfunctioning under normal use may be presumed defective at the time of sale — and rejected Ford's argument that it had been applied too broadly. Why it matters: the manufacturer's story of the day. A presumption of defect from malfunction alone, with no proven cause, is precisely the mechanism that turns a contested product claim into a nine-figure one.

NHTSA determines certain older Ford vehicles pose "unreasonable safety risks"

Insurance Journal · Aug 3
The safety regulator determined that certain older Ford models present unreasonable safety risks. Why it matters: read alongside the verdict above, it shows the two tracks running at once — a regulatory finding and a civil judgment against the same manufacturer. A regulator's determination is the kind of document that later appears in a plaintiff's exhibit list.
