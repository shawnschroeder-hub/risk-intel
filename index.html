Anthropic’s IPO filing admits a worst-case AI disaster could be too big for insurers to cover

Insurance Business America, citing Anthropic’s Form S-1 and WTW · Oct 27 · company risk-factor disclosure; IPO not yet priced
Anthropic’s Form S-1, filed with the SEC on Monday for a planned Nasdaq listing, says a catastrophic AI-related incident could cause damage beyond what any single insurer, or possibly the whole market, can underwrite, and that some AI liabilities may not be insurable on commercially reasonable terms. The filing also says its business interruption cover may not respond to a serious outage at its computing providers, and that insurance did not fully cover its $1.5 billion Bartz copyright settlement. WTW’s Andrew Hill said it “cannot be assumed that all AI exposures will be insurable,” while calling the fundamentals for insuring AI risk still robust.

Specialty market has given back 65% of hard-market rate gains

Insurance Business America, citing WTW’s Specialty Insurance Marketplace Survey (broker analysis of insurer-supplied data) · Oct 27
WTW estimates about 65% of the specialty rate gains built since 2017 had unwound by July 2026, after rates rose roughly 50% net of claims and exposure trend to the 2023 peak. July 1 renewals fell 6% gross and 9% net of trend, with property down 17% net of trend, the steepest decline. Nominal casualty increases of 6% were offset by an estimated 8% claims trend, an effective 2% real reduction.

Workers’ comp losses push Hartford’s mid-market combined ratio to near-breakeven

Insurance Business America, citing The Hartford’s Q3 2026 results · Oct 27 · insurer earnings
The Hartford’s middle and large business underlying combined ratio rose to 99.5 in Q3 2026 from 91.4 a year earlier, driven mainly by a higher workers’ compensation loss ratio, along with higher non-catastrophe property losses. Small business improved to 85.2 from 89.8, and business insurance written premiums grew 6% to $3.8 billion. The article also cites NCCI figures putting private carriers’ workers’ comp accident-year combined ratio at 102%.

Ransomware Attacks Rise 50% in Q3, Says NordLayer

Insurance Journal, citing NordLayer (security vendor data) · Oct 27
NordLayer recorded 2,894 ransomware attacks in Q3 2026 against 1,933 a year earlier, a rise of about 50%, and 8.6% more than in Q2. It says small and mid-sized manufacturers, with up to 200 employees and under $25 million in revenue, remain the most targeted, and North America accounted for almost half of all attacks. Some groups now skip encryption and simply steal data.

Safety Company Acknowledges Hack 7 Months After Breach

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · Oct 26 · company acknowledgment dated Oct 23
Safety company Navigate360 acknowledged on Oct. 23 that hackers copied data from its P3 Global Intel tip platform, about seven months after the breach was first publicized in March. Its statement says names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers and other sensitive information may have been exposed. Officials in Nevada and Canada said the company gave them only partial information despite repeated requests.

Court Rejects Exxon Appeal of $815M Award to Mechanic Over Benzene Exposure

Claims Journal · Oct 27 · appellate ruling dated Oct 15; further review not reported
A three-judge Superior Court of Pennsylvania panel upheld a Philadelphia jury’s May 2024 verdict for a former Mobil station mechanic who attributes his acute myeloid leukemia to benzene in ExxonMobil gasoline. The judgment totals about $815.5 million, including $725 million for pain and suffering and about $90 million in delay damages. The court rejected Exxon’s argument that the award was excessive and declined to order a new trial; the article does not say whether Exxon will seek further review.

Insurers lean on third-party capital, strategic partnerships to manage operating models: BlackRock

Artemis, citing BlackRock (asset manager survey) · Oct 27
BlackRock’s 15th annual global insurance survey of 463 insurers in 31 markets found 82% work with external partners in at least two business functions, and 57% use partnerships to expand capital-related activities. Some 53% plan to increase their use of captive onshore reinsurance. Respondents named capital efficiency (66%) as the main motivation and regulatory, solvency and rating-agency constraints (60%) as the top barrier.

2

Modern warehouses test limits of fire protection

Business Insurance, quoting Zurich Resilience Solutions, FM and Global Risk Consultants · Oct 27
Engineers quoted by Business Insurance say warehouse technology has outpaced the testing of sprinkler protection, and FM says dense storage, including automated retrieval racks, can block sprinkler water from reaching a fire. Global Risk Consultants says sprinkler upgrades can cost several hundred thousand dollars per system, and more than $1 million if larger fire pumps or water tanks are needed. Underwriters are asking more detailed questions about stored goods, especially lithium-ion batteries.

A Risk Management Checklist for Workplace Safety in Extreme Heat

Risk Management Magazine (RIMS) · Oct 27 · practitioner guidance by a workers’ comp defense attorney
The article cites OSHA estimates of more than 550 worker deaths and over 24,000 work-related heat illnesses and injuries each year. It describes no specific federal heat standard: federal protection rests on OSHA’s General Duty Clause, while Washington, Minnesota, California, Oregon and Colorado have their own heat laws. The checklist covers a written heat illness prevention plan, shade and paid rest, annual training and fleet air-conditioning checks.

Insurers warn over AI-enabled cyber attacks

CIR Magazine, citing KPMG’s UK Sentiment Survey (adviser survey) · Oct 26 · UK insurers
In KPMG’s survey of more than 100 UK insurance leaders, 30% named AI-enabled cyber attacks as the risk the industry is least prepared for, ahead of climate catastrophes and technology system failures. Some 60% said strengthening cyber resilience should be a priority over the next 12 months. The largest share, 32%, cited limited industry cooperation as the biggest barrier to protecting clients from systemic cyber risk.

EU sets out climate resilience framework

CIR Magazine, citing the European Commission · Oct 27 · framework presented; accompanying law is a proposal, not adopted
The European Commission presented an EU Framework for Climate Resilience, including a proposed law that would set a common reference trajectory for assessing future climate risk and require one strategic adaptation plan per member state. The Commission estimates climate-related economic losses of about €200 billion from 2022 to 2025 and adaptation investment needs of about €70 billion a year. It also plans a “European climate viewer” map of projected hazards such as heat, drought, wildfire and flooding.

Synthetik and Marsh Re extend violence modelling agreement

CIR Magazine · Oct 26 · vendor and broker announcement
Synthetik Insurance Technologies renewed its agreement with Marsh Re for three more years, covering multi-peril consulting and use of CityScape, an explosion modelling tool focused on dense urban areas. Marsh Re will use it to assess terrorism and political violence accumulations, estimate probable maximum loss and help structure and place reinsurance programmes.
