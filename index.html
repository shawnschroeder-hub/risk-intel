Lloyd's £1.4bn Iran war loss estimate is largely based on exposures, CFO saysPreliminary

Insurance Business America · Sep 4
Lloyd's CFO Jim Bichard said the market's preliminary £1.4bn Iran-related war loss estimate is "still heavily based on exposures and estimates as opposed to really hard numbers," with limited detailed claims information yet available from the region. The losses span marine war as well as political violence and terrorism from physical damage on land, not marine business alone. Bichard said the estimate could change materially as claims develop.

EQT lands $2 billion swoop for McGill and PartnersAgreed, not closed

Insurance Business America · Sep 4
EQT has agreed to acquire a majority stake in McGill and Partners, the London-based specialty broker, from Warburg Pincus, in a deal reported to value the business at $2.0bn; McGill's management retains a stake and the transaction is not yet closed. It follows Aon's separately announced $17bn agreement to acquire USI Insurance Services from KKR the same week.

Fitch keeps deteriorating outlook on reinsurance despite sector's very strong capital position

Artemis, citing Fitch (rating agency) · Sep 4
Fitch maintained a deteriorating outlook for global reinsurance through 2027, citing softening property pricing, intensifying competition and rising claims costs from economic, social and medical inflation alongside climate change and emerging liabilities such as geopolitics and AI. It expects margin and revenue erosion not to materially weaken the sector's very strong capital position, which reached new highs in H1 2026. Fitch estimates roughly $100bn of major losses would be needed to materially shift market dynamics.

Late August western Europe SCS and hail to drive ~EUR 2.5bn insured loss: Gallagher Re

Artemis, citing Gallagher Re (broker research) · Sep 4
Gallagher Re estimates two late-August weather systems, named Quiria and Raphaela, caused roughly EUR 2.5bn of insured losses in France, Switzerland and Italy, with hailstones exceeding 10cm in Italy. Switzerland's share is put at EUR 0.75–1.25bn, Italy's at EUR 0.5–1bn and France's at EUR 0.5–0.75bn. Damage extended to residential and commercial property, vehicles and agricultural operations.

Citizens cuts policy count to 270,000 amid non-renewal push

Insurance Business America · Sep 4
Florida's state-backed insurer of last resort, Citizens, has shrunk from a September 2023 peak of 1.4 million policies to 270,000, including a recent round of 45,000 non-renewal notices; Citizens attributes the decline to private-market takeouts following the 2022–2023 legislative reforms. Citizens says private-market premiums are often higher than its own rates even as competition improves. Affected policyholders have approximately one year to secure replacement coverage, and an eligible private offer within 20% of the Citizens premium can make a policyholder ineligible to renew with Citizens.

California workers' comp medical costs rose 5% in 2025

Risk & Insurance, citing WCIRB · Sep 4
The Workers' Compensation Insurance Rating Bureau of California reports medical costs per claim rose 5% in 2025, driven by a 4% rise in average price per transaction and 1% higher utilization. Medical-legal service costs are up 47% cumulatively since 2021 and now represent 17% of total medical payments, while medical equipment and other services rose 55% cumulatively since 2020 to 13% of payments. Medical cost per claim grew 8% in Southern California in 2025 and was relatively stable in Northern California.

NHTSA opens probe into nearly 1,000 Tesla Cybercabs

Insurance Journal · Sep 4
NHTSA opened an audit into how Tesla determined its two-seater Cybercab — which lacks a steering wheel, pedals and mirrors — complies with federal motor vehicle safety standards, covering close to 1,000 planned Cybercabs. The probe follows Tesla's deployment of the vehicle in Austin, Texas, where Tesla had registered 420 autonomous vehicles, including 45 Cybercabs, by Friday morning. No specific triggering incident was identified in the reporting.

Pharma firms fear climate supply chain risk

CIR Magazine, citing BSI (standards body) · Sep 4
BSI's 2026 G7 "temperature check" report finds 87% of surveyed pharmaceutical companies across G7 nations expect climate-related supply chain disruption to increase, and 88% are concerned about future costs and resilience. Separately, 84% view net zero as important to their business and 37% plan to increase net zero investment over the next year. BSI published its PAS 2090 lifecycle-assessment specification for pharmaceuticals at the end of 2025.

Thomson Reuters detects cyber incident, says unauthorized party accessed filesStill relevant

Insurance Journal · Sep 3
Thomson Reuters disclosed that its West Publishing C-Track case management platform was accessed by an unauthorized party in March, with detection on June 30 affecting C-Track files tied to 11 US states, the US Virgin Islands and Canada. The accessed files may have contained names and other personal information of individuals in court proceedings, and the company says its review of exactly what was affected is continuing. Thomson Reuters says C-Track remains fully operational and it has engaged external cybersecurity experts.

Moody's keeps stable reinsurance outlook. ILS capital influential, with room to grow

Artemis, citing Moody's (rating agency) · Sep 4
Moody's kept a stable outlook on global reinsurance despite softening property catastrophe pricing, citing robust balance sheets and strong solvency after consecutive profitable years. It reports alternative reinsurance capital and ILS grew 18% in 2025 versus 8% for traditional reinsurance, now representing 18% of the total reinsurance market, with the sidecar market reaching a record $23bn in 2026. Moody's says ILS instruments remain underrepresented in institutional investment portfolios.

Strong ILS demand and continued growth expected at year-end and in 2027: Pennay, AonInsurer's own account

Artemis · Sep 4
Aon Securities CEO Richard Pennay said the ILS market is at its strongest point in its 20-plus-year history, with total capacity of $145bn growing at 13.5% annually over the last three years versus a 6.5% ten-year average. Catastrophe bond issuance reached $18bn year-to-date, with some US clients now placing over 25% of their reinsurance through cat bonds, while sidecars expand beyond property into whole-account and casualty structures. Pennay said clients can expect to secure multi-year capacity at favorable pricing amid strong demand into year-end and 2027.

Brokers gain ground as delegated underwriting reshapes the market

Insurance Business America, citing Moody's Ratings · Sep 4
Moody's Ratings reports MGA premiums more than doubled between 2020 and 2024, and business written through Lloyd's coverholders now accounts for about 40% of Lloyd's gross written premium, which itself grew from roughly £36bn in 2020 to £58bn in 2025. Seven of the ten largest London brokers now sponsor or arrange their own facilities, including Aon Client Treaty, Marsh Fast Track and WTW Gemini. Moody's describes this as a broad structural shift in how commercial risk is sourced, priced and placed.

Government must do more to prepare for food supply shocks

CIR Magazine, citing the National Audit Office · Sep 4
The UK National Audit Office says risks to the food supply chain from extreme weather, cyber-attacks and disease are becoming more likely and potentially more severe, and that Defra has done too little in recent years to encourage businesses, households and communities to strengthen resilience. National scenario exercises testing preparedness for severe food supply disruption have largely excluded food supply chain businesses, with the most recent exercise in 2023. The NAO recommends Defra learn from other countries' approaches and test plans jointly with local government and industry.
