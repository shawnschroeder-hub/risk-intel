Hacking Attempts Surge as Companies, Governments Rush to Patch PHP Flaws

Insurance Journal, carrying Reuters · · Oct 26 · develops last Thursday’s PHP item
Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince said exploitation attempts against the newly disclosed PHP flaws grew from thousands before disclosure to hundreds of thousands within two hours and “millions now,” and Amazon also reported widespread exploitation. In Japan, Nomura, Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group, ANA and East Japan Railway temporarily suspended some internet services, and Tokio Marine suspended its group website. Finland’s state IT provider Valtori said restoring services “can take days or even weeks.”

Critical PHP flaws put cyber policy waiting periods and security conditions in focus

Insurance Business America, citing Crawford & Company (claims adjuster) · · Oct 25 · no insured-loss estimate yet
The two flaws, nicknamed “PHP2Shell” and scored 9.2 on CVSS, allow unauthenticated remote code execution on web servers, and PHP disclosed a third remote-code-execution flaw on Oct. 23, according to CERT-EU. Crawford expects a sharp rise in cyber notifications and claims, with business interruption the main issue, and says the key distinction is between precautionary shutdowns and a confirmed cyber incident. The article also flags policy security conditions and outsourced patching as potential coverage and liability questions.

Geopolitical Tensions Now Limit Business Growth for 90% of Executives, Beazley Finds

Risk & Insurance, citing Beazley (insurer survey) · · Oct 26
Beazley’s July 2026 survey of 3,500 global business leaders found 90% say geopolitical tensions and economic uncertainty limit their growth plans, up from 78% in January 2026 and 68% in January 2025. Some 77% plan to change suppliers or supply chains over the next 12 months, and 82% say political change affects their ability to trade profitably. Cybersecurity, including systemic attacks and third-party outages, was a top-three concern for 44%, up from 37%.

Data Centers Face Downgrade Risk Amid Local Pushback, S&P Says

Insurance Journal, carrying Bloomberg, citing S&P Global Ratings · · Oct 26 · report dated Oct 23
S&P Global Ratings tracked 146 delayed or cancelled US data center projects, about three-quarters of them tied to local pushback, and says local governments in 40 states have imposed bans. Frequently cited concerns include energy costs, water scarcity, and air and noise pollution. S&P says the effect on credit ratings has been minimal so far, but rent credits and tenant termination rights “pose credit risk” if projects are meaningfully delayed.

Data center boom brings premium growth, risks

Business Insurance, quoting Aon and EPIC (brokers) · · Oct 26 · remarks from an early-October industry forum
Aon’s Ryan Barber said many data center projects are valued at $3–5 billion, and that Aon placed limits as high as $8.5 billion over a year ago and could likely place more than $10 billion now. Aon estimates data centers could generate more than $100 billion of new premium over five to 10 years, with risk accumulation across phased campuses the main challenge. EPIC’s Marc Kunney said professional liability is “a much more limited marketplace,” while property and business interruption capacity remains plentiful.

Senator Grassley Adds to Fight for Federal TPLF Disclosure

Insurance Journal · · Oct 26 · letter to a rules committee; bill status not stated
Senate Judiciary Committee chairman Chuck Grassley wrote on Oct. 21 urging the federal Advisory Committee on Civil Rules to amend procedural rules to increase public disclosure of third-party litigation funding. He also sponsors the Litigation Funding Transparency Act of 2026, which would require disclosure in multidistrict litigation, class actions and other large cases; the article does not state its legislative status. Committee minutes show members have discussed the issue for years and worried that one rule may not capture its varied circumstances.

Senior care liability claims grow costlier

Business Insurance, citing MedPro, Marsh and CNA · · Oct 26 · $110M verdict under appeal
MedPro data show average senior care indemnity payments rose to $253,000 in 2024 from $179,000 in 2019, and the article cites a Sacramento County jury award of $110 million, including $100 million in punitive damages, which the defendant plans to appeal. Marsh says excess and umbrella rates can rise 30% or more and typical individual excess limits have fallen from $10–15 million to about $5 million. CNA says about 90% of the sector’s premium is now tied to a deductible or self-insured retention.

Hannover Re secures 125% upsized $450m Acorn Re parametric cat bond

Artemis · · Oct 26 · completes the deal carried Oct 22
Hannover Re’s seventh Acorn Re parametric US earthquake cat bond grew from a $200 million target to $450 million of three-year limit, focused on California with neighbouring states also covered. It priced at a 2% spread, the bottom of initial guidance, against a 0.78% expected loss. The ceding insurer is Oak Tree Assurance, a Kaiser Permanente captive.

Liberty Mutual Re and Celest.Science explore parametric trigger data fallback

Artemis · · Oct 26 · exploratory agreement, not a product
Liberty Mutual Reinsurance and Celest.Science have agreed to explore a fallback method for characterising tropical cyclone events in parametric insurance when key weather data is unavailable or delayed. Any use in an LM Re product would depend on technical validation, governance approvals and contract terms. Artemis notes parametric cat bonds always name a fallback data provider, but parametric insurance policies often do not.

Sidecars and data centres key opportunity areas for HCMA in 2027: CEO Madea

Artemis, interviewing Howden Capital Markets & Advisory (broker) · · Oct 26 · executive view
HCMA chief executive Jarad Madea named collateralized reinsurance sidecars and data centre build-out as key opportunity areas for 2027, and estimated at least 20 true end-investors were in Monte Carlo this year, against two three to five years ago. He said no finalised insurance-linked securities solutions for data centre risk exist yet, and that traditional insurers and reinsurers will be needed to solve it.

Property owners invest in resilience, but insurers slow to recognize efforts

Business Insurance, citing Marsh (broker survey) · · Oct 26
Only 26% of more than 120 organizations in Marsh’s 2026 Climate Adaptation Survey said their adaptation efforts were recognized in insurance negotiations. Prologis, which owns about 6,000 properties in 20 countries, could not point to direct premium savings from earthquake retrofits and longer-life roofs. A $2.5 million wildfire policy for Tahoe Donner carried a 39% lower premium and an 84% lower deductible because of forest management work, as described by The Nature Conservancy.

Third-party breaches hit more people than the numbers suggest

Insurance Business America, citing Kroll (vendor research, own caseload) · · Oct 26
Kroll’s Data Breach Outlook 2026, based on breaches it handled from June 2025 to June 2026, found 69% of all affected individuals were hit through a breach at a vendor, contractor or service provider. In its companion survey, 54% of IT leaders named vendor and third-party breaches as their biggest compliance risk, against 40% of C-suite executives. Kroll’s Katherine Keefe said a single vendor compromise can generate claims from many unrelated policyholders.

It may take real losses to sell AI insurance: CRC Specialty CEO

Insurance Business America, interviewing CRC Group (wholesale broker) · · Oct 26 · executive view; Deloitte figure is a projection
CRC Group’s Neil Kessler said AI insurance products are not selling quickly and buyers may need real losses first, while Deloitte projects about $4.7 billion of annual global AI premiums by 2032. ISO’s generative AI exclusion endorsements became available in January 2026, and a Trades Coverage review counted 2,369 such exclusion records in force across 49 states and DC as of July 31. Kessler expects the market to sort out AI coverage over the next six to 24 months.
