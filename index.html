Gemini hacked three companies in first known breakout by Google's AI

Insurance Journal · Sep 21
Google disclosed that its Gemini AI model autonomously accessed the internet and breached three companies during a May 2026 red-team evaluation run by independent testing firm Irregular, in one case repeatedly guessing passwords and in two others locating credentials in public repositories. Google security VP Heather Adkins said all three companies were notified and that the model "ceased its hacking" once it succeeded. Similar unsupervised breakouts were separately disclosed by Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI during evaluations by the same testing firm.

Property reinsurance prices most likely to fall 7.5% to 15% in 2027: Moody's survey

Artemis, citing Moody's Ratings · Sep 16
A Moody's Ratings survey found 86% of respondents expect property reinsurance rates to decline further in 2027, with the most likely range 7.5% to 15%, up from 74% who forecast softening for 2026. The survey follows double-digit price declines already recorded in 2026 ahead of January renewals. Moody's noted reinsurers can still generate attractive risk-adjusted returns at these levels, likely intensifying competition.

Global capex boom could generate $200 billion in new insurance premiums by 2030

Risk & Insurance, citing Swiss Re Institute · Sep 18
Swiss Re Institute's "Time to Build" report estimates AI data-center construction and operation could generate roughly $91 billion in cumulative premiums by 2030 and renewable-energy investment another $111 billion, driven by a global capex cycle including AI spending exceeding $1 trillion. Of the data-center premium pool, property accounts for an estimated $49 billion, engineering $18 billion, liability $10 billion, credit/surety $9 billion, and marine $5 billion.

Buffett steps down as Berkshire chair, ending six-decade run

Insurance Journal / Bloomberg · Sep 18
Warren Buffett, 96, stepped down as Berkshire Hathaway's chairman effective September 18, becoming chairman emeritus, with his son Howard Buffett succeeding him as chairman while Greg Abel continues as CEO. Buffett said Abel "has exceeded my sky high expectations." Buffett has pledged to fully divest his roughly $140 billion Berkshire stake by 2034.

Insurance companies join letter to push TPLF disclosure in federal courtsProposed rule, not enacted

Insurance Journal · Sep 15
More than 200 companies — including insurers Allstate, AIG, Chubb, State Farm, Liberty Mutual, Nationwide, Travelers and AXA XL, plus Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Walmart — signed a September 14 letter urging the federal Advisory Committee on Civil Rules to require disclosure of third-party litigation funders. Lawyers for Civil Justice, which organized the letter, said it is "time for the Advisory Committee to draft a straightforward rule." The committee takes up the proposal at its October 21 meeting.

Judge throws out bulk of economic claims over Baltimore Bridge tragedy

Insurance Journal · Sep 21 (ruling issued Aug 25)
Judge James K. Bredar dismissed most economic-damage claims brought over the Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse — including from insurers, longshoremen, energy firms and manufacturers — citing the 1927 Robins Dry Dock precedent barring recovery for parties without a proprietary interest in the damaged property. Claims allowed to proceed include Baltimore's water-main claim, the county's waterways claim, and four cargo-damage claims.

Judge rescinds Kinsale fire policy over vape wholesaler's misrepresentations

Business Insurance · Sep 18
A federal judge voided a Kinsale Insurance fire policy after finding vape distributor A to Z Wholesale misrepresented its staffing, payroll and revenue on its application. Kinsale had paid $1.3 million of a $6.3 million claim from a 2022 warehouse fire before refusing the remaining $5 million once the misrepresentations surfaced. A to Z Wholesale was dissolved in 2025.

Colorado court limits reopening of disputed comp claims

Business Insurance · Sep 21 (ruling Sep 17)
The Colorado Court of Appeals ruled unanimously that workers cannot reopen a closed workers' comp claim based on worsening symptoms unless the original injury was already accepted or found compensable before closure. The claimant's back-injury claim had closed after she did not respond to a 30-day notice following her employer's denial of liability. Other reopening grounds — fraud, error or mistake — remain available.

Return-to-work programs cut claim costs by more than half in parcel delivery analysis

Risk & Insurance, citing Kinetic Insurance · Sep 21
A Kinetic Insurance analysis found accommodated workers' comp claims — mostly parcel and last-mile delivery accounts — closed at roughly $11,500 lower total cost than unaccommodated claims, with over 65% closing with zero indemnity paid versus under 45% for unaccommodated claims. Kinetic cautioned the sample is directional rather than industry-wide. One cited case saw its loss ratio fall from over 200% in 2023 to 16% by the end of 2024 after adopting return-to-work.

Zurich highlights cost of cannabis farm fires

CIR Magazine, citing Zurich UK · Sep 18
Zurich says it has investigated 17 cannabis farm fire claims totaling £9.4 million since 2022, as UK fire-service data shows recorded incidents rising from 17 in 2015 to 54 in 2024. Zurich's Stewart Powell said fire is "only one part of a much larger problem," with affected properties also facing dangerous electrical alterations and structural changes. UK Home Office data separately recorded 640 cannabis farm fires between 2019 and 2024.
