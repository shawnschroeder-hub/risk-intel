Microsoft outage’s financial toll may fall short of disruption

Business Insurance, citing Morningstar DBRS (rating agency) and Marsh Re (broker) · Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · Nov 13 and Nov 15 · preliminary estimate
Morningstar DBRS preliminarily estimates global insured losses of $300 million to $500 million from last week’s Microsoft 365 outage, against $400 million to $1.5 billion for the 2024 CrowdStrike outage. Cyber policies typically apply waiting periods of eight to 12 hours before business-interruption cover responds, which Marsh Re says can be as short as four hours for smaller organizations. Microsoft said on November 15 that all services were restored and attributed the outage to an error introduced during a network capacity upgrade, with no evidence of malicious activity.

Airlines work to restore flights as FAA probes faulty router

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg · Nov 16 · outage began Friday, Nov 13; investigation ongoing
An air-traffic-control communications outage that began at 3:30 p.m. New York time on Friday, November 13, affected 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and more than 12,000 flights were canceled in total. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a router at a remote site in El Dorado, Arkansas flooded the system with connection attempts, and the FAA is also examining equipment in Amarillo, Texas. Duffy raised his request for emergency FAA modernization funding from $30 billion to $50 billion.

White House urges easing liability to encourage AI disclosure

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg (first reported by Semafor) · Nov 16 · informal guidance from an anonymous official; no order, rule or bill
Officials from the administration’s AI task force advised agencies at a closed White House meeting on November 12 to limit regulatory liability for the next 12 months, possibly longer, to encourage AI companies to report critical cyber incidents, according to an anonymous administration official. No executive order, rule or bill was reported. The article says this contrasts with the task force’s warning last month that developers must report and resolve security incidents or face unspecified consequences.

Intel sued by shareholders following 29% stock plunge

Business Insurance, via Reuters · Nov 16 · proposed class action; allegations only, no ruling
Shareholders led by a Michigan pension fund filed a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose against Intel, its CEO and its CFO, alleging the company was too slow to disclose how weaker PC sales and excess inventory would hurt revenue. Intel shares fell about 28.6% on October 30 after it projected weaker fourth-quarter results, erasing roughly $140 billion of market value. The suit seeks unspecified damages for investors who bought between July 23 and October 29, and points to Intel raising about $23 billion by selling stock at $95 a share in mid-August.

Lawyer’s letter without a dollar figure counts as insurance claim: court

Business Insurance · Nov 13 · Illinois appellate ruling
The Appellate Court of Illinois, First District, unanimously held in Church Mutual Insurance Co. v. Frontier Management that a lawyer’s lien letter claiming a one-third fee on any recovery was a claim under a claims-made policy, even though it named no dollar figure and no lawsuit followed. The court declined to adopt a rule requiring an explicit demand for money, relying on the policy’s language and a 1995 precedent. It affirmed dismissal of the insurer’s suit against an assisted-living facility and its owners.

New California legal opinion sets guardrails for parametric insurance deployment: Ceres

Artemis, citing Ceres (nonprofit advocacy group) · Nov 16 · regulator’s opinion letter, not a new regulation
The California Department of Insurance issued a General Counsel opinion letter, requested by Ceres, setting out when a parametric product counts as insurance, including an insurable interest, genuine risk transfer and proof of loss. Qualifying products fall under the Insurance Code and, where applicable, Proposition 103’s prior rate-approval rules. The letter recommends objective third-party triggers, clear disclosure that payouts may differ from actual losses, and complete rate filings before sale.

California Earthquake Authority secures its second-largest cat bond, $900m Ursa Re 2026-1

Artemis · Nov 16
The California Earthquake Authority’s Ursa Re 2026-1 catastrophe bond closed at $900 million, 50% above its $600 million target. Both classes priced at the low end of final guidance, at spreads of 3.5% and 8%, against expected losses of 2.68% and 6.35%. The notes provide about four years of fully collateralized California earthquake reinsurance on an indemnity, annual aggregate basis.

Where geopolitical risk leaves multinational programs exposed

Insurance Business America, citing HUB International (broker) and Howden (broker) · Nov 16 · broker survey and views
HUB International’s 2026 North American Report found one-third of business leaders see geopolitical risk as a direct threat to profit, but only 15% feel completely confident managing it. HUB’s Will Mulé recommends a controlled master program with locally issued underlying policies, with political risk insurance covering hazards such as political violence, currency inconvertibility and expropriation. Howden research cited in the article found more than half of surveyed multinationals reported a political loss on an international investment between 2020 and 2025.

Uber says drivers’ license data stolen in data breach

Insurance Journal, via Reuters · Nov 16 · company breach notice; vendor named by TechCrunch, not by Uber
Uber says a service provider told it on October 9 that credentials had been compromised, and its investigation confirmed on October 13 that an unauthorized party used them to access Uber data including personal information. Its notice, dated November 10, covers about 1,873 Rhode Island residents and offers free credit monitoring. TechCrunch reported that the provider was Fireworks AI and that driver’s license data was taken.

Data center investors underestimate climate risk, report says

Insurance Journal, via Bloomberg, citing Jupiter Intelligence (climate-analytics firm) · Nov 16 · report published Oct 13
A Jupiter Intelligence report puts potential annual losses to data centers from floods, wind and wildfires at around $4.4 billion, with 61% of the risk falling on IT equipment rather than buildings. The article says such equipment impacts are largely uninsured. North America holds 44% of data center value but 17% of potential loss, while China, including Hong Kong, holds 17% of value and 49% of potential loss.

New Orleans tanker collision puts marine liability market on notice

Insurance Business America · Business Insurance · Nov 16 and Nov 14 · collision Nov 13; cause under investigation
On November 13 the asphalt tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the docked river cruise ship American Heritage at the Port of New Orleans, and the cruise ship capsized within 22 minutes; all 190 people aboard were accounted for and about 30 were hospitalized with non-critical injuries. The NTSB attributed the crash to a steering casualty that left the rudder locked, and the port sued the tanker’s owner and manager in federal court the same day seeking at least $1 million. Marine insurer Gard confirmed it provides the tanker’s protection and indemnity and hull and machinery cover.

Aviva’s premiums grow 20%

Business Insurance, citing Reinsurance News · Nov 16 · insurer’s nine-month trading update
Aviva’s general insurance premiums rose 19% year on year to £11.8 billion ($15.6 billion) in the first nine months of 2026, with UK and Ireland business up 27%. Its combined ratio held steady at 94%.
