Swiss Re P&C hits 76.7% combined ratio but the pricing gap widens

Insurance Business America · Aug 6 · H1 2026 results
Swiss Re reported a 76.7% property-casualty combined ratio, net income of $1.446bn (up 18% from $1.223bn), an insurance service result of $1.821bn (up 16%), and $19.5bn of treaty volume renewed year to date. Natural catastrophe losses were $169m and man-made $129m. The pricing gap: at mid-year renewals nominal pricing fell 1.2% while risk-adjusted pricing fell 5.3%; year to date the split is −0.2% nominal against −4.6% risk-adjusted. Swiss Re attributes the divergence to record capital levels shifting pricing power to cedants. Why it matters: the single most useful number today is the difference between those two figures. Nominal pricing understates how much ground reinsurers are actually giving; the risk-adjusted figure is the real concession, and it is the one to ask for when a programme is quoted.

Global wildfire surge hits the US, propelled by rising temperatures

Insurance Journal · Aug 6
Fires near Spokane have destroyed hundreds of structures and forced the evacuation of more than 60,000 people — roughly 10% of the city — with three major fires burning over 8,000 acres uncontained and 17 active fires across Washington state. Greece, France and Spain have all seen major fires. A cited climate scientist frames the driver as both traditional fire regions becoming hotter and drier and the geographic range of fire-prone areas expanding; research cited identifies "fire weather waves" occurring on 4% of days from 1979 to 2024 yet coinciding with over 25% of burned forest area. Why it matters: the second half of that framing is the underwriting problem. If the map of where major fires happen is expanding, historical loss experience at a given site understates the exposure, and property schedules built on that history will be repriced.

CSU maintains a well below-normal hurricane forecast as landfall probabilities fall further

Artemis, citing Colorado State University · Aug 5
CSU held its forecast at 9 named storms (two already occurred), 4 hurricanes, 1 major hurricane and an ACE index of 50. Landfall probabilities continue to fall: 16% for the entire US coastline (from 17% in July and 24% in June), 7% for the East Coast and Florida, 9% for the Gulf Coast. The team cites strong El Niño conditions driving high wind shear. Why it matters: read directly against the Swiss Re item above. A quiet season forecast is a large part of why reinsurers are conceding risk-adjusted price, and it is a fair argument to make on any property placement incepting before the season ends. It is a forecast, not an outcome.

As wildfires destroy and displace, APCIA says Congress must pass the Fix Our Forests Act

Insurance Journal · Aug 6 · bill passed Senate committee Oct 2025 · NOT enacted
The American Property Casualty Insurance Association is urging Congress to pass the Fix Our Forests Act, which would address wildfire risk through forest management and hazardous fuels reduction; its president is quoted saying Congress "should act now to advance proven mitigation solutions." Status matters here: the Senate version, S.1462, passed committee in October 2025 and has seen no further action, and more than 40 organisations have sought its inclusion in the FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act. Why it matters: a trade body lobbying is not itself news, but the stalled status is the useful fact. Federal mitigation is not going to change the wildfire underwriting picture in this cycle — site-level mitigation a buyer controls is the only lever with a near-term effect on terms.

Where litigation-funding disclosure law actually stands: one federal bill introduced, seven states enacted

Congress.gov and Institute for Legal Reform · status check, not new news · federal bill INTRODUCED onlystill relevant
The federal Litigation Funding Transparency Act of 2026 (S.3826), introduced by Senators Grassley, Tillis, Kennedy and Cornyn, would require disclosure of third-party funders and their agreements in federal class actions and multi-district litigation, require parties to state whether a funder is a foreign state or sovereign wealth fund, and bar funders from controlling litigation decisions. It remains introduced — not passed, not law. At state level, seven states adopted funding laws during 2025: Arizona, Colorado, Kansas, Georgia, Montana, Oklahoma and Tennessee, with varying mixes of disclosure duties, control prohibitions and joint liability for costs. New Jersey's Assembly Bill 2159 passed one chamber on June 30, 2026, as reported in Edition 006, and is also not law. Why it matters: the direction is consistent and the pace is slow. For planning purposes, assume funded claims stay largely invisible in most jurisdictions through the current renewal cycle, because the disclosure regime that would change that is still mostly proposals.

Zurich secures a $1bn data centre quota share and breaks out its US peak peril reinsurance tower

Artemis · Aug 6
Zurich arranged a $1bn quota share to support a data centre book of roughly 500 projects on which it leads about 70%, citing accelerating demand from AI infrastructure. It also separated US peak peril catastrophe reinsurance into a standalone tower for the first time, topped by its new $150m Turicum Re Ltd. (Series 2026-1) catastrophe bond. US peak peril retention is unchanged at $650m; the North America earthquake swap rose to $225m from $215m; Europe all-perils retention fell to $489m from $505m and the Europe regional cat treaty to $449m from $550m. Why it matters: the mechanism, not the client. When value concentrates on a small number of very large sites, the traditional market stops absorbing it alone and the buyer ends up blending quota share, a dedicated tower and capital-markets capacity. That is the same path a manufacturer with one outsized flagship location eventually walks.

Record UK wildfire year sees fires move closer to cities

CIR, citing LexisNexis Risk Solutions · Aug 6 · 2025 full-year data
Analysis of VIIRS satellite data found 4,659 UK wildfire detections in 2025, the highest on record against a previous peak of 3,305 in 2018 and just 951 in 2024. Spring accounted for 2,635 detections and summer 1,606, but summer fires occurred closer to populated areas. London was the largest urban wildfire hotspot. The analysis is quoted as saying wildfire risk to homes and commercial property has become "almost as important as understanding flood or subsidence risk." Why it matters: a near-fivefold jump in one year is a data artefact worth treating carefully, but the direction is unmistakable and it pairs with the US item above. UK and European sites have generally not carried wildfire as a rated peril; that assumption is being revisited.
