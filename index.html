WTW, Ivans reports signal more commercial rate moderationUS data only

Insurance Journal, citing WTW's CLIPS survey and the Ivans Index · Sep 14
WTW's Commercial Lines Insurance Pricing Survey recorded a 0.5% increase in US commercial rates in the second quarter of 2026, down from 2.5% in the first quarter and 3.8% a year earlier. Excess and umbrella liability posted high-single-digit increases while commercial property saw the largest decreases. The separate Ivans Index for August 2026 showed year-on-year renewal premium changes of 6.82% for umbrella, 5.69% for commercial property, 5.44% for business owners policies, 4.18% for general liability, 3.58% for commercial auto and -1.1% for workers' compensation.

AI data centers are on track to fuel 'explosive' growth in captive insuranceBroker view

Insurance Journal, citing Marsh (broker view) · Sep 14
More than 6,000 captives worldwide now write about $240 billion in premium, nearly 20% more than two years ago. Marsh manages roughly 1,900 captives writing about $79 billion in premium, of which $11.5 billion is reinsured. Marsh's US and Canada captive solutions leader Michael Serricchio projected explosive growth in captives taking on data-center portfolio risk.

28% of reinsurance buyers expect to increase alternative capital use in 2027: Moody's

Artemis, citing Moody's Ratings (rating-agency survey) · Sep 14
Moody's Ratings' annual reinsurance buyer survey found 28% of respondents expect to increase their use of alternative capital in 2027, with the remainder expecting it to stay stable. Catastrophe bonds were the preferred structure at 83% of respondents, and sentiment toward ILS funds was the strongest in several years. Moody's did not disclose the number of respondents.

Broker consolidation reshapes power balance for middle market insurers

Risk & Insurance, citing RSM US (advisory research) · Sep 11
RSM US reports that broker M&A ran at roughly 700 deals a year worth about $4 billion from 2018 to early 2020, after which deal counts normalised but average deal values rose sharply from 2023 to 2025. The research finds premium volume has concentrated among fewer, larger platforms, shifting negotiating leverage away from middle-market carriers. RSM identifies regulatory asymmetry and broker concentration as the two main pressures on those carriers.

LA County opens investigation into Farmers over wildfire claims handlingInvestigation, not a finding

Insurance Business America · Sep 14
The Los Angeles County Office of County Counsel opened a civil investigation into Farmers Insurance on September 9 over its handling of claims from the January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires. County lawyers allege delayed or denied smoke and contamination testing, resistance to authorising industrial hygienists, and underpayment for remediation of homes contaminated with lead, asbestos and chromium. Farmers disputes the characterisation and says it will cooperate through the appropriate process; no findings of wrongdoing have been announced.

Insurance Journal · Sep 14
Senator Elizabeth Warren wrote to NAIC chief executive Jeffrey Johnston asking whether the body is examining other insurers for conduct similar to Delaware Life and Clear Spring Life and Annuity, which Warren says failed to properly label more than $20 billion in affiliated loans. Bayer faces pivotal test of $7.25 billion Roundup settlementProposed, not approved

Insurance Journal · Sep 11
A proposed $7.25 billion settlement covering roughly 65,000 US Roundup cancer claims was set for a hearing today before Missouri state judge Timothy Boyer, who is expected to hear objections without ruling immediately. Payouts would range from $10,000 to $165,000 depending on the severity of the cancer, age at diagnosis and whether exposure was at home or at work. The settlement was announced in February 2026 and follows a 2020 agreement worth about $10 billion that left Bayer exposed to future claims.

Appeals court revives tubing injury suit over deflated slide bumpersLiability not decided

Insurance Business America · Sep 14
The US Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit on September 8 reversed the dismissal of Stupak v. Mont du Lac Snowsports, remanding the case for further proceedings against the resort and its insurer Nova Casualty. A visitor who broke his spine, ribs and hip when his tube left a closed slide and struck a tree was technically a trespasser, but the court held that recklessness — conscious disregard of an unreasonable and substantial risk of serious bodily harm — could still support liability. A jury would decide whether recklessness occurred and whether the rider assumed the inherent risks of tubing.

Admiral sues CUMIS over refusal to repay injury defense costsNo ruling yet

Insurance Business America · Sep 14
Admiral Insurance sued CUMIS Insurance Society in the US District Court for the Western District of New York on September 10 over which carrier owes the primary defense to New York State in a construction worker's injury claim. The worker alleged he was struck by an unsecured bundle of insulation at a Buffalo State College roofing project; Admiral wrote the contractor's general liability policy and CUMIS the commercial auto policy, both naming the State as an additional insured on a primary and non-contributory basis. Admiral seeks $65,496.28 in defense costs and a declaration that CUMIS must assume the defense, while CUMIS calls the payments voluntary and points to an auto exclusion in Admiral's policy.

Ford to recall about 223,500 vehicles over fuel tank issues

Insurance Journal, reporting NHTSA · Sep 14
Ford is recalling 223,472 US vehicles — certain 2023–2027 F-150 models — over fuel tanks that may leak or detach from the vehicle while being driven, according to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.
