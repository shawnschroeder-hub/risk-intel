Reinsurers post second-best first-half return in a decade as excess capital mounts

Risk & Insurance, citing Gallagher Re (broker research) · Sep 11
Gallagher Re's September 2026 market report puts its reinsurance composite's first-half return on equity at 19.9%, the second-highest half-year result of the past decade, with its composite combined ratio improving to 85.8% from 92.1%. Total reinsurance capital rose 5% in the first half to $688bn, while P&C reinsurance premiums fell 6%. Natural catastrophe losses were at least $46bn in H1 2026, down from $84bn a year earlier.

Casualty ILS inflows an incremental negative, cat may soften more than RVS suggests: KBWAnalyst opinion

Artemis, citing KBW (analyst opinion) · Sep 11 · Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous
KBW analysts describe capital entering casualty reinsurance through ILS structures as an incremental negative for casualty reinsurers, noting that longer tails make pricing errors slower to correct. They expect property catastrophe rate decreases of roughly 10% at the January 1, 2027 renewals, with larger declines at higher layers. KBW adds that actual pricing typically underperforms Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous expectations, so steeper falls are possible.

Surplus lines hit $143bn record as placement market shifts

Insurance Business America, citing AM Best (rating agency research) · Sep 11
AM Best's annual US surplus lines market segment report puts 2025 direct premium written at $143.3bn, up 10.4% from $129.8bn and an eighth consecutive year of double-digit growth. Surplus lines now account for 12.9% of total P&C direct premium, against 3.6% in 2000. AM Best reports no surplus lines impairments in 2025 or through mid-2026, and notes a possible inflection as admitted carriers re-enter segments and capacity expands.

The traditional casualty program is getting harder to defend

Insurance Business America, citing HUB International (broker view) · Sep 11
HUB International's Mary-Beth Hahn says commercial auto and umbrella insurers remain cautious, with loss trends of 12–15% being factored into pricing and some carriers cutting umbrella capacity limits so brokers must assemble towers from more insurers. She advises reviewing programs in totality rather than line by line, with property savings redirected to casualty placements. Hahn says clients are increasingly being moved from guaranteed-cost to deductible programs, with captives, parametric covers and alternative collateral entering the conversation.

Federal terrorism insurance backstop prompted by 9/11 still waits for reauthorizationHouse-passed, Senate pending

Insurance Journal · Sep 11
The federal terrorism risk insurance program is authorized through December 31, 2027, and the House passed a measure in June 2026 to renew it through 2034, which now awaits Senate consideration. An act must cause more than $5m in insured losses to be certified as terrorism. Industry groups warn that leaving reauthorization until 2027 creates uncertainty, because past delays led insurers to write conditional exclusions that drop terrorism coverage if the program is not renewed.

Nuclear verdicts go boom, increase 40.7% in 2025Sep 7 · still relevant

Insurance Journal, citing Marathon Strategies (research firm) · Sep 7
Marathon Strategies counted nearly 200 verdicts of $10m or more in 2025, 40.7% more than in 2024, with awards totalling roughly $25.6bn. Forty verdicts exceeded $100m and four passed $1bn. Product liability accounted for 29 verdicts worth $12bn, and 68 industries recorded a nuclear verdict against 55 in 2024.

Casualty insurers face $100 billion reserve correction as analytics lag: Moody'sSep 3 · still relevant

Risk & Insurance, citing Moody's (vendor research) · Sep 3
Moody's reports more than $35bn of cumulative adverse reserve development in general liability over eight years, rising to about $100bn once commercial auto is included, against roughly $300bn of annual US casualty premium. Other Liability Occurrence recorded $8bn of adverse development in the most recent calendar year, after $10bn the year before. Moody's attributes the lag to casualty risk accumulating through litigation and evolving legal theory before it becomes directly observable.

Ariel Re sees growing role for ILS in cyber exposure landscape: CarrExecutive view

Artemis, quoting Ariel Re (reinsurer) · Sep 11
Ariel Re head of cyber Dan Carr says the firm remains constructive on the outlook for alternative capital in cyber as exposure grows and becomes more systemic. Cyber catastrophe bonds currently represent about 1.3% of the outstanding cat bond and ILS market, with the first such bond issued in 2023. Carr expects alternative capital to extend beyond standalone cyber bonds into multi-peril structures, while competition from traditional reinsurance currently limits issuance.

Ensuring recovery from a food safety crisisFood sector survey

Risk Management Magazine, citing a GS1 survey · Sep 11
A GS1 survey published in September 2026 finds 94% of US adults worry about recall frequency, 67% have avoided an entire food category after a recall, and 66% hesitate to buy from the same brand again. The article notes that standard policies often exclude voluntary recalls, and that specialist product recall cover addresses lost profits, recall and business interruption costs and brand restoration. It cites a summer 2026 cyclospora outbreak that sickened more than 11,000 people across 20 states.

Cumulative trauma claims are cited as the main cost driver, reaching 26% of all indemnity claims in 2024 against 13% a decade earlier.

New US-Canada tariffs raise claims costs and insurance exposuresAug 25 · still relevant

Beinsure · Aug 25
Beinsure reports a 50% US tariff on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods, raising prices for vehicle parts, steel, aluminium and construction materials and with them repair and replacement costs. It cites an American Property Casualty Insurance Association estimate from spring 2025 of $30bn to $61bn added to personal auto claims over twelve months, and Verisk reconstruction costs up 3.6% between April 2025 and April 2026. AI agents push cyber insurers to rethink policy languageSep 3 · still relevant

Beinsure · Sep 3
Cyber insurers including MSIG, QBE and Beazley are clarifying existing wordings rather than adding broad exclusions, as autonomous AI agents make decisions without continuous human direction and complicate coverage triggers built around identifiable human actors. Munich Re put the global cyber insurance market at close to $15bn in 2025 and projects $28bn by 2030, while Aon forecasts generative AI will be involved in nearly 20% of cyberattacks by 2027. Verisk's Jenny Soubra flags shared AI platforms as a systemic exposure that could trigger claims across many organisations at once.

Insurance Journal, citing the US Climate Prediction Center · Sep 11
The US Climate Prediction Center puts a 75% probability on this El Niño becoming a historic event, with equatorial Pacific sea-surface temperatures already 1.8°C above normal and potentially reaching 2.5°C against the 1982-83 record of 2.4°C. Global catastrophe losses set to average $171 billion a year, Verisk findsSep 4 · still relevant

Risk & Insurance, citing Verisk (vendor modelling) · Sep 4
Verisk puts the global average annual insured loss from natural catastrophes at $171bn, against $59bn in 2012, with North America accounting for $124bn. Its modelled exceedance figures are $309bn at a 20-year return period and $477bn at 100 years. Verisk estimates insured losses represent 38% of total economic losses, implying an economic average annual loss above $450bn, with global property exposure growing about 7% a year from 2021 to 2025.

Accreditation scheme aims to reduce business driving risksUK scheme

CIR Magazine · Sep 11
Road safety charity IAM RoadSmart has launched a three-tier accreditation scheme assessing how organisations manage driver risk, legal compliance and safety performance, with Gold reserved for a fully embedded road safety culture. Applicants submit driver risk policies, telematics data, training records and incident data against a structured scoring framework. Policy director Nicholas Lyes says the accreditation helps firms reduce driving risk and prevent collisions.
