Colorado Supreme Court rules Chubb unit not liable for insolvent primary insurer's coverage gap

Business Insurance · Sep 22
The Colorado Supreme Court ruled unanimously that Federal Insurance, a Chubb unit, is not obligated to cover defense and liability costs left uncovered when primary insurer Reliance Insurance became insolvent. The court held that "not covered" in the umbrella policy refers to the scope of coverage, not whether the primary insurer can pay, overruling a 1989 Colorado Court of Appeals decision a manufacturer had relied on. The ruling aligns Colorado with other states holding that a scheduled primary insurer's insolvency does not expand an umbrella or excess insurer's obligations.

Verisk raises global insured catastrophe loss benchmark to $171 billion annually

Verisk, via Beinsure · Sep 4 · older but still relevant — annual benchmark
Verisk raised its estimate of average annual global insured catastrophe losses to $171 billion, based on updated exposure and peril modeling. Verisk president Rob Newbold said insurers and reinsurers should plan around this level going forward. The figure spans natural catastrophe perils across multiple regions, not a single event or year.

DOJ guidance directs prosecutors to seek more whistleblower suit dismissals

Insurance Journal · Sep 23 · policy guidance, not a court ruling
The Justice Department issued revised Justice Manual guidance encouraging prosecutors to use their dismissal authority more aggressively against False Claims Act whistleblower (qui tam) suits. The change gives prosecutors greater discretion to terminate cases and lets defendants petition for dismissal. DOJ officials linked the shift partly to a rapid rise in qui tam filings driven by AI and data-analytics tools.

Nuclear verdicts rose 40.7% to $25.6 billion in 2025, Marathon Strategies finds

Marathon Strategies, via Insurance Journal · Sep 7 · older but critical — annual benchmark data
Marathon Strategies tracked nearly 200 verdicts of $10 million or more in 2025, a 40.7% increase over 2024, totaling roughly $25.6 billion. Product liability accounted for 29 of those verdicts and $12 billion of the total, the largest single category. Sixty-eight industries were hit by at least one nuclear verdict in 2025, up from 55 in 2024.

UK regulator closes consultation on stricter third-party litigation funding disclosure

Solicitors Regulation Authority, via Lawyer Monthly · Sep 17 · consultation closed, no rule yet
The UK's Solicitors Regulation Authority closed a 10-week consultation on September 17 proposing that law firms using third-party litigation funding in consumer claims notify the SRA, prepare a funding risk assessment, and give clients a funding summary before signing. No final rule has been adopted yet. The proposal targets funding arrangements in consumer legal claims specifically, not commercial litigation funding broadly.

Cat bond risk premiums stay historically wide even as reinsurance spreads soften

Sage Advisory and Marsh Securities, via Artemis · Sep 23
Sage Advisory said catastrophe bond risk premiums remain among the widest available in the market even as broader reinsurance pricing softens. Artemis separately reported 2026 cat bond and ILS issuance of $18.9 billion year-to-date, with $65.6 billion outstanding. Bermuda regulator to weigh substance over form for new parametric risk-transfer structures

Artemis, citing the Bermuda Monetary Authority · Sep 17
The Bermuda Monetary Authority said it will prioritize the economic substance of parametric risk transfer over its contractual form when reviewing new "PSPI" structures. The guidance is aimed at ensuring parametric products are regulated according to how they actually transfer risk rather than how they are labeled. No specific structures or insurers were named in the reporting.

Crane safety survey finds 98% of professionals face communication breakdowns

National Safety Council Work to Zero & NCCCO Foundation, via Business Insurance · Sep 22
A survey of 376 US crane professionals by the National Safety Council's Work to Zero program and the NCCCO Foundation found 98% had experienced communication challenges on the job, most often from weather, noise, or limited visibility. Riggers' knowledge gaps (54%), inattention (49%), production pressure (48%) and poor supervision (47%) were the leading contributors to unsafe rigging identified. Nearly half of respondents said communication failures had halted a lifting operation.

New York Assembly bill would penalize late workers' comp medical payments

Business Insurance · Sep 22 · introduced, pending in committee
New York Assembly Bill A.11714, introduced and pending before the Assembly Labor Committee, would charge employers and insurers 3% monthly interest on workers' compensation medical bills unpaid more than 15 days after a board award. The bill would also let the board assess penalties of up to 25% for repeat nonpayment, defined as three or more violations within 12 months. The bill has not passed either chamber.

Accommodated return-to-work programs cut comp claim costs by more than half, analysis finds

Kinetic Insurance, via Risk & Insurance · Sep 21
Kinetic Insurance analyzed more than 600 closed workers' compensation claims and found accommodated return-to-work programs reduced total claim costs by more than $11,500 per claim compared with unaccommodated claims. In the parcel delivery sector studied, adoption of such programs rose from 55% in 2023 to more than 75% in 2025. A structured, technology-guided approach was most effective at limiting wage-replacement benefits.

Joint War Committee expands Black Sea war-risk zone, raising shipping costs

Beinsure · Sep 23 · zone change made Sep 16 · figures are preliminary market estimates
The Joint War Committee expanded its Listed Area to cover most of the Black Sea on September 16, excluding the territorial waters of Turkey, Bulgaria, Romania and Georgia. Large vessels sailing to Greater Odesa now face estimated additional war-risk costs of roughly $150,000 per voyage, or $2–3 per tonne of cargo; smaller Handysize vessels face $12,000–$24,000 per transit. These are preliminary market estimates, not published rates.

IUMI proposes data-sharing platform to close freight forwarding liability gap

Insurance Business America · Sep 23
The International Union of Marine Insurance is proposing an information-sharing platform and standardized wording for freight forwarding liability insurance, citing inconsistent coverage across jurisdictions. IUMI noted marine cargo premiums are tracked at $22.6 billion for 2024, but no comparable data exists for freight forwarding liability premiums or losses. The proposal is at an early, non-binding stage.
